The Foundation

5 Rules Every Dog Trainer Lives By

Before you teach a single command, these principles will determine whether your training succeeds or fails.

1
🔄

Be Consistent

Use the exact same word and hand signal every time. "Down" and "lie down" are different commands to a dog. Pick one and stick with it — every person in the house must use the same cue.

2
🎯

Reward Immediately

You have a 2-second window. The treat or praise must come within 2 seconds of the correct behavior — otherwise your dog connects the reward to whatever they did last, not what you wanted.

3
⏱️

Keep Sessions Short

5–10 minutes per session, 2–3 times a day. Dogs learn better in short bursts than marathon sessions. Always end on a success — ask for something easy before finishing so your dog feels confident.

4
😊

Stay Positive

Positive reinforcement (rewarding what you want) works faster and builds a stronger bond than punishment. Never punish a dog for not understanding — it creates fear, not learning.

5
📈

Raise the Bar Gradually

Once your dog gets a command right 8 out of 10 times, add a distraction, increase distance, or practice in a new location. This is called "proofing" and it's what makes commands stick for life.

Breed-Specific Tips

Training Difficulty by Breed

Every breed was developed for a different purpose — and that affects how they learn. Here's what to expect.

Easiest to Train
🐕 Border Collie 🐕‍🦺 German Shepherd 🦮 Labrador Retriever 🐕 Golden Retriever 🐩 Poodle 🐕 Australian Shepherd

These breeds were bred to work closely with humans and follow instructions. They pick up commands quickly and thrive with mental challenges.

Moderate Difficulty
🐶 Beagle 🐺 Siberian Husky 🐕 Boxer 🌭 Dachshund 🐶 Shih Tzu 🦊 Corgi

Smart but independent thinkers. They can be trained very well — they just need more patience, shorter sessions, and especially motivating rewards.

More Independent
🐶 Bulldog 🐕 Chihuahua 🐕 Rottweiler 🐕 Great Dane 🐕 Cane Corso 🐕 Dalmatian

Not impossible — just requires more experience and patience. These breeds often have strong wills or were not bred for obedience. Consistent rules from day one are essential.
