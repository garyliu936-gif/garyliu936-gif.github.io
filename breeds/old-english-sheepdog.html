Breed Overview

The Old English Sheepdog — affectionately called the "OES," "Bobtail," or "Shaggy Dog" — is one of Britain's most beloved herding breeds. Developed in western England in the early 19th century to drive cattle and sheep to market, the OES was bred for endurance, intelligence, and the ability to work in all weather conditions. Their thick, shaggy double coat provided protection against England's damp, cold climate.

Their distinctive rolling gait, dense coat covering even the eyes, and characteristic low "woof" bark make them instantly recognizable. Famous as the Dulux paint mascot in the UK, and starring in the 1959 Disney film "The Shaggy Dog," the OES has become a cultural icon of lovable, woolly chaos.

Temperament

OES dogs are gentle, playful, and deeply loving with their families. They're wonderful with children — patient, tolerant, and often protective in a soft, herding-instinct way. They're sociable with strangers and generally get along well with other pets. Their herding instinct means they may try to "herd" children or other pets by nudging them. They're intelligent, sometimes clownish, and thrive on family involvement — they don't do well when isolated for long periods.

Exercise Needs

Moderate exercise — 45–60 minutes of activity daily. They enjoy walks, fetch, and herding activities. Despite their large, fluffy appearance, they're surprisingly agile. Avoid vigorous exercise in heat — their thick coat makes them prone to overheating. Mental stimulation is equally important; boredom leads to destructive behavior.

✈️ Travel Tip: At 60–100 lbs, OES dogs must travel as checked baggage or cargo on commercial flights. Their thick coat creates serious overheating risk in warm temperatures — summer travel should be approached with extreme caution. Many airlines restrict heavily coated large breeds during summer months. Ground transport is typically safest and most comfortable for this breed.

Grooming

The OES's thick double coat is one of the most demanding in all of dogdom. Without regular brushing — ideally daily — it mats into dense, painful clumps that can require full shaving. Brushing takes 3–5 hours per week for a dog in full coat. Professional grooming every 6–8 weeks is standard. Many owners keep their OES in a "puppy cut" — significantly shorter than the show coat — for practicality. The hair over the eyes is a breed trademark; in pet dogs, it's often tied back or trimmed. Food and water routinely get caught in the beard — regular face washing is needed.

Training

OES dogs are intelligent and eager to please — they respond well to positive reinforcement and are capable of high-level obedience and agility. They can be a bit clownish and test limits, but genuine stubbornness is less common than in terrier breeds. Early training and socialization produce a well-rounded adult. Their herding instinct means they benefit from having a "job" to do — even if that job is learning tricks or participating in dog sports.

Health Considerations

Hip Dysplasia Progressive Retinal Atrophy Cataracts Hypothyroidism Cerebellar Ataxia

Hip dysplasia is the primary orthopedic concern. Eye conditions — particularly cataracts and PRA — warrant annual ophthalmologist checks. Cerebellar ataxia (a neurological condition causing coordination problems) occurs in some OES lines — DNA testing is available. Their dense coat can mask skin problems and cause hot spots in warm weather.

Feeding Your Old English Sheepdog

OES dogs eat 2.5–4 cups of quality large breed dry kibble daily, split into two meals. Their thick coat and active nature require good nutrition. Monitor weight — their coat makes it hard to visually assess body condition.

Life StageDaily AmountMeals/Day
Puppy (2–6 mo)1.5–2.5 cups (large breed puppy)3
Adult (1–7 yr)2.5–4 cups2
Senior (8+ yr)2–3 cups2

Omega-3 fatty acids maintain coat health and reduce skin issues. Large breed puppy formula during growth protects developing joints. Fresh water always available — especially after play given overheating risk.

Popular Old English Sheepdog Mixes

Sheepadoodle (OES + Poodle)

One of the most popular designer mixes — the Sheepadoodle combines the OES's gentle, playful nature with Poodle intelligence and reduced shedding. Typically large, fluffy (often in striking black and white), and wonderfully family-friendly.

OES-Golden Retriever Mix

Combines two of the friendliest, most family-oriented large breeds. The result is typically a large, shaggy, golden-coated dog with a wonderful temperament — affectionate, trainable, and great with kids.

OES-Australian Shepherd Mix

Two herding breeds combine into a highly active, intelligent, and loyal dog. Often striking in appearance with the OES's shaggy coat and the Aussie's coloring. Needs substantial daily exercise and mental stimulation.

Fun Facts About Old English Sheepdogs

  • 🎨 Dulux Dog: The OES has been the mascot for Dulux paint in the UK since 1961 — one of the most enduring brand mascots in British advertising history. The dogs used in Dulux ads are so famous they have their own fan following.
  • 🎬 Disney's Shaggy Dog: The 1959 Disney film "The Shaggy Dog" made the OES famous in America, starting a trend that saw the breed's popularity surge through the 1960s and 70s.
  • ✂️ The Bobtail: OES dogs were traditionally docked (had their tails removed as puppies) to identify them as working dogs exempt from taxation. This gave them the nickname "Bobtail" — still used today even though docking is no longer standard or legal in many countries.
  • 👁️ Peek-a-Boo Eyes: The OES's characteristic hair-over-the-eyes look is functional — the hair doesn't actually block their vision (they can see through it) but acts as a sun visor and protection against flies in the field.
  • 🔊 Ring-Road Sound: The OES has a distinctive "pots and pans" or "ring-road" bark — a loud, low, resonant "woof" that's different from most other breeds. Owners say it sounds more like a large instrument than a dog.
