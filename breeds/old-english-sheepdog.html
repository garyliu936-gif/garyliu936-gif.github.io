Old English Sheepdog

A rolling, shaggy, bear-like bundle of joy — the OES is a playful, gentle herding giant instantly recognized by its iconic flowing coat and shuffling, lumbering gait.

😊 Temperament & Personality

Old English Sheepdogs are known for being highly intelligent and responsive. They form deep bonds with their families and thrive on consistent human connection. They strike a good balance — lively enough to enjoy playtime and adventures, but calm enough to settle indoors when the day is done.

Old English Sheepdogs can be trained with patience and consistency, though they may occasionally test boundaries. With proper socialization they can do well with children, though supervision is always wise.

  • Moderately active — enjoys exercise and playtime but appreciates downtime at home
  • Affectionate and people-oriented — thrives on closeness with their family
  • Generally good with kids when properly socialized from puppyhood
  • Capable learner who benefits from short, varied training sessions
  • Breed-typical personality is reliable and predictable — makes planning your lifestyle together easier
  • Genuinely enjoys relaxing — equally happy on a couch as on a trail with the right owner

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

Old English Sheepdogs need regular daily exercise to maintain a healthy weight and an even temperament. They are versatile companions who enjoy active outings but are equally content to relax at home after their needs are met.

  • Daily exercise: 45 – 60 minutes of moderate activity
  • Daily vigorous exercise plus significant mental stimulation — herding instincts must have outlets or they'll herd children, cats, and anything that moves
  • Daily walks, play sessions, and occasional trips to a dog park are ideal
  • Mental enrichment (puzzle toys, training) complements physical exercise
  • Adjust intensity based on age — puppies and seniors need gentler, shorter sessions
  • Aim for a mix of on-leash walks and free play

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

Old English Sheepdogs have a short, low-maintenance coat that requires minimal grooming compared to longer-haired breeds. A consistent basic routine keeps them clean, comfortable, and healthy.

  • Brush once a week with a soft bristle brush or rubber grooming mitt
  • Bathe every 6–8 weeks, or when dirty — overbathing strips natural coat oils
  • Clean ears weekly and check for redness or odor that may indicate infection
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks — they often don't wear down naturally indoors
  • Wipe down with a damp cloth between baths to keep the coat gleaming
  • Double-coated herding breeds shed seasonally and benefit from regular brushing with a slicker brush and undercoat rake during shed seasons.

🎓 Training

Old English Sheepdogs are moderately trainable and respond well to patient, consistent positive reinforcement. They benefit from clear expectations and a calm, confident trainer who establishes routines early.

Keep sessions short — 10 to 15 minutes — and always end on a success. Enrolling in a puppy or adult obedience class provides structured learning and valuable socialization.

  • Begin training and socialization as early as possible — the puppy window is critical
  • Use high-value rewards (real meat, cheese) to compete with distractions
  • Be consistent — the same rules must apply every session and every family member must agree
  • Herding breeds are among the most trainable dogs in the world. They thrive in obedience, agility, and rally sports. Challenge their minds daily.
  • Crate training establishes boundaries, aids house training, and gives your dog a safe personal space
  • Short, positive sessions daily beat long, infrequent sessions every time

🏠 Is a Old English Sheepdog Right for You?

A Old English Sheepdog fits well with families or individuals who can provide moderate daily exercise and consistent companionship. They adapt reasonably well to various living situations as long as their exercise and social needs are met. Not ideal for owners away from home for long hours without a pet care solution in place.

Feeding Your Old English Sheepdog

OES dogs eat 2.5–4 cups of quality large breed dry kibble daily, split into two meals. Their thick coat and active nature require good nutrition. Monitor weight — their coat makes it hard to visually assess body condition.

Life StageDaily AmountMeals/Day
Puppy (2–6 mo)1.5–2.5 cups (large breed puppy)3
Adult (1–7 yr)2.5–4 cups2
Senior (8+ yr)2–3 cups2

Omega-3 fatty acids maintain coat health and reduce skin issues. Large breed puppy formula during growth protects developing joints. Fresh water always available — especially after play given overheating risk.

Popular Old English Sheepdog Mixes

Sheepadoodle (OES + Poodle)

One of the most popular designer mixes — the Sheepadoodle combines the OES's gentle, playful nature with Poodle intelligence and reduced shedding. Typically large, fluffy (often in striking black and white), and wonderfully family-friendly.

OES-Golden Retriever Mix

Combines two of the friendliest, most family-oriented large breeds. The result is typically a large, shaggy, golden-coated dog with a wonderful temperament — affectionate, trainable, and great with kids.

OES-Australian Shepherd Mix

Two herding breeds combine into a highly active, intelligent, and loyal dog. Often striking in appearance with the OES's shaggy coat and the Aussie's coloring. Needs substantial daily exercise and mental stimulation.

Fun Facts About Old English Sheepdogs

  • 🎨 Dulux Dog: The OES has been the mascot for Dulux paint in the UK since 1961 — one of the most enduring brand mascots in British advertising history. The dogs used in Dulux ads are so famous they have their own fan following.
  • 🎬 Disney's Shaggy Dog: The 1959 Disney film "The Shaggy Dog" made the OES famous in America, starting a trend that saw the breed's popularity surge through the 1960s and 70s.
  • ✂️ The Bobtail: OES dogs were traditionally docked (had their tails removed as puppies) to identify them as working dogs exempt from taxation. This gave them the nickname "Bobtail" — still used today even though docking is no longer standard or legal in many countries.
  • 👁️ Peek-a-Boo Eyes: The OES's characteristic hair-over-the-eyes look is functional — the hair doesn't actually block their vision (they can see through it) but acts as a sun visor and protection against flies in the field.
  • 🔊 Ring-Road Sound: The OES has a distinctive "pots and pans" or "ring-road" bark — a loud, low, resonant "woof" that's different from most other breeds. Owners say it sounds more like a large instrument than a dog.
