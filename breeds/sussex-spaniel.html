Breed Overview

The Sussex Spaniel is one of the oldest of the spaniel breeds and, tragically, one of the rarest — a heavy-boned, golden-liver English spaniel developed in the dense, brambly farmland of Sussex county in southern England beginning in the early 1800s. The breed was created specifically for the unique challenges of hunting in Sussex's thick, tangled undergrowth, where the dense cover required a slow, methodical, powerfully-built dog that would work game thoroughly rather than dash ahead of hunters on foot. The result was a spaniel unlike any other: long, low, and massively built, with enormous strength relative to its height and a characteristic rolling gait — more plodding than flowing.

The Sussex Spaniel's most distinctive and unusual trait is its voice — unlike all other spaniels, which work silently in the field, the Sussex gives tongue freely while hunting, producing a rich melodious howl that historically guided hunters through dense cover where they could not see their dog. This howling tongue was considered a feature by Sussex hunters who needed to track their dogs by sound through bramble thickets that blocked all visual contact. The breed nearly vanished completely during World War II when breeding almost entirely ceased in Britain; post-war recovery traced to the work of a single dedicated breeder, Joy Freer, who kept eight dogs through the war years and rebuilt the population almost single-handedly. Today fewer than 100 Sussex Spaniels are born in the US each year.

Health Notes

  • Hip dysplasia
  • Intervertebral disc disease (low, long body)
  • Ear infections — long, heavy ears need regular cleaning

Feeding Your Sussex Spaniel

A medium-weight, lower-energy spaniel with a long, low body, the Sussex needs careful feeding to maintain a healthy weight — excess weight strains their elongated spine.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 12 mo)1.5–2 cups550–850 kcal
Adult (1–8 yrs)1.5–2.5 cups650–900 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)1–1.75 cups500–750 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • Maintain lean weight — excess weight accelerates spinal and joint stress in this long-bodied breed
  • Omega-3 fatty acids support the dense, feathered spaniel coat
  • Medium-breed formula appropriate for their size and moderate energy level
  • Joint supplements (glucosamine) beneficial as they age given their elongated spine

Fun Facts About Sussex Spaniels

  • 🏆 The Sussex Spaniel won Best in Show at the 2009 Westminster Kennel Club Dog Show — one of the most celebrated upsets in Westminster history. "Stump," a 10-year-old Sussex Spaniel, became the oldest dog ever to win Best in Show at Westminster, at an age when most dogs of comparable size have long retired. His victory sparked enormous interest in the breed and briefly made the Sussex Spaniel a household name.
  • 📣 The Sussex Spaniel is the only spaniel breed that gives tongue freely while hunting — producing a melodious, resonant howl during active work that all other spaniels are specifically bred to suppress. This howling voice was not an error or a fault but a deliberate design feature: Sussex hunters needed to track their dogs audibly through the dense brambly undergrowth of Sussex's farmland, where visual contact with the dog was impossible.
  • 🎖️ The Sussex Spaniel very nearly went extinct during World War II. When wartime food restrictions made maintaining large kennels impossible, most British breeders ceased operations. After the war, the entire surviving Sussex Spaniel population in England could be traced to a single breeder — Joy Freer — who had kept eight dogs through the war years at great personal sacrifice. Every modern Sussex Spaniel alive today descends from Freer's wartime survivors.
  • 🐢 Unlike most sporting spaniels, which are bred for speed and flash, the Sussex was deliberately developed to work slowly and thoroughly — plodding methodically through thick cover, ensuring no bird is missed rather than flushing game fast and wide. Sussex hunters on foot preferred this steady pace; the breed's working style is sometimes described as "hunting furniture" — solid, unhurried, and utterly reliable.
  • 🥇 The Sussex Spaniel was one of the original nine breeds registered at the founding of the AKC in 1884, making them a genuine founding member of the American purebred dog world. That an original AKC breed should now be one of the rarest in the country — with some years seeing fewer than 50 AKC registrations — makes the Sussex Spaniel's story one of the more poignant narratives in American dog history.
