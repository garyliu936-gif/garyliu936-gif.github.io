Breed Overview

The Puli (plural: Pulik) is one of Hungary's most ancient and iconic breeds, with a herding history stretching back over 1,000 years to the Magyar tribes that settled the Carpathian Basin. What makes the Puli instantly unmistakable is its extraordinary corded coat — long, naturally forming cords of fur that resemble dreadlocks or the strands of a mop — that covers the dog from head to tail. Despite looking like something you'd clean a floor with, the Puli is an agile, athletic, and remarkably quick herding dog.

Pulik were so valued by Hungarian shepherds that a good herding Puli was said to be worth the price of an entire flock. They are known for an unusual herding technique — rather than circling the flock from the outside, they run directly on top of the sheep's backs to direct the flock. Off the field, the Puli is an energetic, loyal, somewhat stubborn companion with a clownish sense of humor. They are devoted to their family and wary of strangers, but full of personality for those they trust.

Feeding Your Puli

An energetic herding dog, the Puli needs quality nutrition to fuel their activity. Their cords can disguise weight changes — monitor body condition by feel rather than sight.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 12 mo)1–2 cups500–900 kcal
Adult (1–8 yrs)1.5–2.5 cups700–1,100 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)1–2 cups550–900 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • High-quality protein supports their agile, working herding physique
  • Omega fatty acids promote healthy skin beneath the cords
  • Avoid overfeeding — excess weight hidden under the cords causes joint stress
  • Dry food is preferred — wet food and cords don't mix well

The Puli's most demanding cost is the coat — cords take 2–4 years to fully form, require regular separation to prevent matting together, and need thorough drying after bathing (which can take hours). A committed grooming routine is non-negotiable.

Fun Facts About Pulik

  • 💻 Mark Zuckerberg's Puli, "Beast," became one of the most famous dogs on the internet — with a Facebook page that amassed over 2 million followers and introduced millions of people worldwide to the breed for the first time.
  • 🐑 Pulik traditionally herded by running directly on top of the sheep's backs — their lightweight build (25–35 lbs) and quick, bouncing movement allowed them to literally walk across the flock to direct it, a technique unique among herding breeds.
  • 💰 In medieval Hungary, a single skilled Puli herder was so valued that it was said to cost the equivalent of an entire year's wages for a shepherd — a prize more valuable than most of the sheep they herded.
  • 🔐 The Puli's corded coat is not just decorative — it provided formidable protection against wolf bites when the dog was guarding flocks on the Hungarian plains. The thick cords could absorb a bite that would injure a dog with a normal coat.
  • ⏰ A Puli's cords take 2–4 years to fully form and mature — owners describe the process of raising a corded Puli as an ongoing art project that requires daily attention to separate and guide each individual cord.
