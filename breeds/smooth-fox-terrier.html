Breed Overview

The Smooth Fox Terrier is one of the oldest and most historically significant of all terrier breeds — and the original fox terrier from which the Wire Fox Terrier was later developed. Bred in England for centuries to bolt fox from underground dens during hunts, the Smooth Fox Terrier accompanied mounted foxhunting packs as the "earth dog" that would follow quarry into the earth and drive it back out for the hounds to pursue. Its smooth, glossy, predominantly white coat (selected so hunters could distinguish the dog from the fox in dim conditions underground) and its compact, muscular body were ideal for this work.

The Smooth Fox Terrier was one of the first breeds to be shown at dog shows in England — it appeared at the first organized dog show in Newcastle in 1859 — making it one of the founding breeds of the modern dog show tradition. For much of the late 19th and early 20th century, Fox Terriers (both Smooth and Wire) were among the most popular breeds in Britain and America, beloved for their alert, lively character and elegant appearance. Today the Smooth is less common than the Wire, ranking in the lower half of AKC registrations, but maintains a devoted following among terrier fanciers who value its clean lines, loyal character, and genuine working heritage.

  • Size: Small–Medium
  • Coat: Short, hard, dense, flat — predominantly white with black or tan markings
  • Colors: White with black and tan, white with tan, or white with black markings
  • Good for Apartments: Possible but challenging — high energy and vocal terrier

Health Notes

  • Lens luxation — heritable eye condition; DNA testing available
  • Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease — hip joint condition in small breeds
  • Deafness — white coat breeds can carry congenital deafness genes; BAER testing recommended
  • Generally a healthy, long-lived small terrier with few hereditary issues

Feeding Your Smooth Fox Terrier

A small, high-energy terrier, the Smooth Fox Terrier needs quality nutrition to fuel its active lifestyle without adding weight to its lean, athletic frame.

Nutrition Tips

  • High-quality protein supports lean muscle in this athletic small terrier
  • Avoid overfeeding — the Smooth Fox Terrier should maintain a lean, athletic silhouette
  • Small-breed formula is appropriate given the lightweight frame
  • Treats should not exceed 10% of daily caloric intake for this food-motivated breed

The Smooth Fox Terrier's short, dense coat is very low-maintenance — a weekly brush and occasional bath covers grooming needs with no professional trimming required. Finding quality puppies may require searching for dedicated breeders, as the Smooth is less common than its Wire cousin. The breed's primary demand is exercise — a Smooth Fox Terrier without adequate daily running and mental stimulation becomes an energetic, vocal, mischievous house companion.

Fun Facts About Smooth Fox Terriers

  • 🏆 The Smooth Fox Terrier appeared at the very first organized dog show ever held — the 1859 Newcastle dog show — making it one of the founding breeds of the entire dog show tradition. In the following decades, Fox Terriers (both Smooth and Wire varieties) dominated early dog shows in Britain and America and were among the most intensely competitive and valuable show dogs of the Victorian era. The breed helped establish the concept of showing dogs as a sport and the development of breed standards as measures of ideal type.
  • 🦊 The Smooth Fox Terrier's predominantly white coat was a deliberate working selection — a white dog emerging from a dark underground den could be distinguished from the quarry fox more easily, preventing accidental shooting by hunters. This practical origin of the white-based coat explains why both Fox Terrier varieties are predominantly white with colored markings, unlike most other terrier breeds that come in solid or more varied colors.
  • 📺 Asta, the beloved wire-haired dog in the "Thin Man" film series, introduced millions of Americans to the Fox Terrier type in the 1930s — though depicted as a Wirehaired, the character brought the Fox Terrier personality (clever, playful, mischievous) into popular culture during the Golden Age of Hollywood. The films sparked enormous interest in the breed and contributed to the Fox Terrier being one of the most popular breeds in America during that decade.
  • 🌍 The Smooth Fox Terrier is distinct from the Wire Fox Terrier and has been classified as a separate breed since 1984 by the AKC — before that, both varieties were shown as one breed. The Wire variety is descended from coarser-coated working terriers crossed into the Smooth type in the 19th century. The two breeds share the same ancestry and temperament but are shown separately, with the Wire typically more prevalent in modern show rings.
  • ⚡ Smooth Fox Terriers are extraordinarily fast and agile for their size — built with a lean, square body, powerful hindquarters, and long legs relative to their frame that give them a loping, ground-covering stride. Their speed was essential in the field: after bolting a fox from its earth, the terrier had to escape quickly before the mounted hunters and hounds arrived. This built-in acceleration makes the Smooth Fox Terrier one of the fastest small breeds and an exceptional agility competitor.
