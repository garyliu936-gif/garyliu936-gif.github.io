🐾 Overview

The Norwegian Buhund (from the Norwegian "bu" meaning farm or homestead, and "hund" meaning dog) is a compact, foxy Spitz-type herding dog that has been a fixture on Norwegian farms for over a thousand years. Archaeological evidence from the Gokstad Viking ship burial (circa 900 AD) includes the skeletal remains of six Spitz-type dogs believed to be Buhund ancestors, confirming their presence in Viking Scandinavia. They were the all-purpose Norwegian farm dog — herding sheep and cattle, guarding homesteads, warning of bears, and serving as affectionate family companions.

The Norwegian Buhund is a medium-small, tightly built Spitz with the characteristic curled tail, erect pointed ears, and wedge-shaped head of the Nordic breeds. Their coat is most commonly wheaten (varying from pale cream to bright orange-gold) or black. They are remarkably versatile — while traditionally a herding breed, they excel equally at agility, obedience, hearing-assistance dog work, and search and rescue. They are more trainable and less independent than many Spitz breeds, with a genuine eagerness to please that makes them stand out in the herding group. Rare in the US but deeply appreciated by those who discover them.

😊 Temperament & Personality

Norwegian Buhunds are known for being loyal with strong instincts. They form deep bonds with their families and thrive on consistent human connection. As a high-energy breed, they are best matched with active owners who enjoy outdoor activities and can commit to regular exercise.

Training is generally a pleasure with Norwegian Buhunds — they are responsive, motivated, and pick up commands quickly. They are typically gentle and patient with children, making them a reliable family companion.

  • Energetic and playful — needs daily outlets and consistent exercise to stay balanced
  • Affectionate and people-oriented — thrives on closeness with their family
  • Patient and gentle with children — a dependable family companion
  • Intelligent and eager to learn — responds enthusiastically to positive training methods
  • Breed-typical personality is reliable and predictable — makes planning your lifestyle together easier
  • Mental stimulation is as important as physical exercise — puzzle toys and training prevent boredom

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

Norwegian Buhunds are high-energy dogs that need significant daily exercise to stay physically and mentally healthy. Without adequate activity they can become restless, vocal, or destructive. They are happiest with owners who genuinely enjoy an active lifestyle.

  • Daily exercise: 1.5 – 2+ hours of vigorous activity — split into morning and evening sessions
  • Daily vigorous exercise plus significant mental stimulation — herding instincts must have outlets or they'll herd children, cats, and anything that moves
  • Off-leash time in a securely fenced yard or dog park is highly beneficial
  • Mental enrichment (puzzle feeders, scent games, obedience training) is as important as physical activity
  • Without adequate exercise they may develop destructive habits from boredom
  • Puppies: limit impact exercise to 5 minutes per month of age to protect developing joints

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

Norwegian Buhunds have a dense double coat built for protection against the elements. They shed year-round with two major blow-out seasons in spring and fall. Regular brushing dramatically reduces the amount of hair around your home and keeps the coat healthy.

  • Brush 2–3 times per week; daily during heavy shedding seasons
  • Use a slicker brush followed by an undercoat rake to reach the dense undercoat
  • Bathe every 6–8 weeks — avoid overbathing as it strips the natural protective oils
  • Never shave a double coat — it disrupts insulation and the coat may grow back unevenly
  • Clean ears weekly and check for signs of infection
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks — they rarely wear down naturally indoors

🎓 Training

Norwegian Buhunds are highly responsive to training and genuinely enjoy learning. Their combination of intelligence and eagerness to please makes them a joy to work with — they pick up new commands quickly and retain them well.

Keep training sessions varied and engaging to prevent boredom. Norwegian Buhunds can handle advanced training — agility, nose work, therapy work, or competitive obedience are all excellent options for channeling their intelligence.

  • Begin training and socialization as early as possible — the puppy window is critical
  • Reward-based training (treats, praise, play) produces the best and fastest results
  • Be consistent — the same rules must apply every session and every family member must agree
  • Herding breeds are among the most trainable dogs in the world. They thrive in obedience, agility, and rally sports. Challenge their minds daily.
  • Crate training establishes boundaries, aids house training, and gives your dog a safe personal space
  • A well-exercised dog is a focused dog — always exercise before training sessions for best results

✅ Best Foods for Norwegian Buhunds

Look for dog foods where the first ingredient is a named protein — chicken, beef, salmon, or lamb. Medium or large-breed formulas are calibrated for their metabolism and nutritional needs.

  • Small-to-medium breed formula suits their size and fast herding-dog metabolism
  • Omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids support the thick double coat through seasonal shedding
  • High-quality protein maintains their lean, athletic frame
  • Avoid overfeeding — Spitz breeds can become overweight without adequate exercise

🚫 Foods That Are Dangerous for Norwegian Buhunds

These common human foods can be toxic — even life-threatening — for dogs. Keep them safely out of reach at all times.

Chocolate Grapes & Raisins Onions & Garlic Xylitol (artificial sweetener) Macadamia Nuts Alcohol Avocado Raw yeast dough

🦴 Healthy Treats for Norwegian Buhunds

  • Carrots — low calorie, great for dental health
  • Blueberries — antioxidants and a sweet reward
  • Plain cooked chicken or turkey (no seasoning)
  • Apple slices (remove seeds and core)
  • Plain rice cakes — low-calorie training reward
  • Commercial treats sized appropriately for a medium breed

💡 Tip: Boarding your Norwegian Buhund?

Always bring your Norwegian Buhund's regular food when boarding. Switching food suddenly can cause digestive upset. Provide the facility your exact feeding schedule and portion sizes.

💡 How to Save Money as a Norwegian Buhund Owner

  • Get pet insurance before your dog turns 1 — premiums are lower and pre-existing conditions won't be excluded
  • Buy food in larger bags when possible — significantly cheaper per pound
  • Learn basic grooming at home — brushing, ear cleaning, and nail trimming save groomer fees
  • Ask your vet about wellness plans — many clinics offer annual packages that bundle routine care
  • Use a rewards credit card for larger vet bills
  • Adopt instead of buying — rescue Norwegian Buhunds are just as loving and cost a fraction of the price

🎉 Amazing Facts About Norwegian Buhunds

Norwegian Buhunds are full of surprises. Here are some of the most fascinating, funny, and heartwarming facts about this breed.

  • ⚔️ Skeletal remains of Spitz-type dogs found in the Gokstad Viking ship burial in Norway (circa 900 AD) are believed to be ancestors of the Norwegian Buhund — placed in the burial to accompany their Viking owner into the afterlife. This makes the Buhund one of the best-archaeologically-documented Nordic breeds.
  • 🐑 The "Bu" in Buhund means farm or homestead in Norwegian — but the breed was valued beyond simple herding. Norwegian farmers used them to herd reindeer in the north, guard properties at night, and alert households to the presence of bears and wolves, making them a genuinely multi-purpose working partner.
  • 🎓 The Norwegian Buhund is one of the few Spitz breeds that has demonstrated real aptitude as an assistance dog — several have been trained as hearing dogs for deaf individuals, and they have been used as search-and-rescue dogs. Their trainability sets them apart from the typical Spitz independence profile.
  • 🌾 In Australia, where the breed arrived with Norwegian immigrants, the Norwegian Buhund found a second life as a farm herding dog suited to the Australian climate. The Australian Buhund population helped keep the breed's gene pool healthy during periods when Norwegian numbers fell.
  • 🎨 The wheaten coat of the Norwegian Buhund spans an enormous range of color — from pale cream through golden yellow to deep orange — giving individuals wildly different appearances despite carrying the same gene. No two wheaten Buhunds look precisely the same, which owners find a delightful feature of the breed.
