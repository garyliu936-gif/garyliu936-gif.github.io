Overview

The Bedlington Terrier looks like a lamb but acts like a terrier. Originally bred in the Northumberland mining town of Bedlington, England, these dogs were used to hunt rats, badgers, and foxes — often racing against whippets for sport.

Don't be fooled by the soft, curly coat and gentle appearance. Bedlingtons are energetic, courageous, and tenacious. They are loyal family companions who can be stubborn but respond well to positive training. Their unique appearance never fails to turn heads at the dog park.

Did You Know?

  • Originally used to race against Whippets
  • Named after a mining town in Northumberland, England
  • Their crisp curly coat is neither wire nor soft
  • AKC recognized in 1886

Common Health Issues

  • Copper toxicosis (hereditary liver disease)
  • Patellar luxation
  • Distichiasis (extra eyelashes)
  • Retinal dysplasia

Is a Bedlington Terrier Right for You?

If you want a unique-looking dog with a spirited terrier personality in a manageable size, the Bedlington is an unforgettable companion.

Bedlington Terrier Diet & Nutrition Guide

🐶 Puppy (0–12 months)

Approach: 3 meals daily of small-breed puppy formula

Calories: ½–¾ cup per day total

🐕 Adult (1–7 years)

Approach: 2 meals daily; low-copper diet recommended

Calories: ¾–1 cup per day

🦮 Senior (7+ years)

Approach: 2 meals daily of senior formula; monitor copper levels

Calories: ½–¾ cup per day

Key Nutritional Priorities

  • Low-copper diet critical for breed health
  • High-quality protein for muscle maintenance
  • Antioxidants for liver support
  • Omega-3s for curly coat condition

Fun Facts About the Bedlington Terrier

  • Their curly coat was historically thought to shed in rain to keep them dry
  • Once known as the "Gypsy Dog" due to their use by Romani people for poaching
  • Among the fastest terriers — can outrun some sighthounds over short distances
  • The distinctive topknot was traditionally used to protect the skull in fights
