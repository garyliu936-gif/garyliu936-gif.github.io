🐾 Overview

The Poitevin is one of France's oldest and most distinguished scent hounds, tracing its origins to the 17th century in the Poitou region of western France. It was created by the Marquis François de Larrye, who crossed local French hounds with English and possibly Eastern European bloodlines to produce a dog of exceptional speed, stamina, and scenting ability. Originally bred to hunt wolves — a major threat to French livestock at the time — the Poitevin became the pinnacle of French pack-hunting culture, prized by aristocrats and serious chasseurs alike.

The Poitevin is a large, athletic hound with a striking tricolour coat, long legs, a deep chest, and a noble, proud bearing. Its population nearly collapsed during the French Revolution, but careful reconstruction using similar French hounds saved the breed. Today, the Poitevin remains primarily a working pack hound in France, rarely kept as a companion, and is considered rare outside its home country. It is recognized by the FCI under Group 6 and remains a proud symbol of French venery tradition.

😊 Temperament & Personality

The Poitevin is a proud, spirited, and energetic hound that was bred for the hunt first and companionship second. It is typically gentle and even-tempered with people it knows but can be aloof with strangers. Its strong pack instinct means it generally does well with other dogs, particularly other hounds. However, its high prey drive and deeply independent nature make it challenging for first-time dog owners.

  • Proud and noble in bearing — carries itself with natural elegance
  • Energetic and tireless outdoors; calm and quiet at home when well exercised
  • Excellent pack hound — thrives in the company of other dogs
  • Can be aloof or reserved with strangers
  • Very strong prey drive — should not be trusted off-leash in open areas
  • Gentle and affectionate with familiar family members

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

The Poitevin is a high-energy working hound that requires substantial daily exercise. Without adequate physical and mental stimulation, it can become frustrated, vocal, and destructive. This breed is best suited to rural environments where it can cover ground freely and safely.

  • Minimum 60–90 minutes of vigorous daily exercise
  • Excels at running, hiking, tracking, and pack hunting
  • Needs a large, securely fenced property — can cover miles when scenting
  • Not suited for apartment living or sedentary households
  • Nose work and scent-tracking games provide excellent mental exercise
  • Thrives when used for actual hunting or field work

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

The Poitevin has a short, dense, smooth tricolour coat that is easy to maintain. Regular brushing removes loose hair and keeps the coat gleaming. Ear care is particularly important, as the long drop ears typical of French hounds are prone to moisture accumulation and infection.

  • Brush weekly with a hound glove or soft bristle brush
  • Bathe monthly or after hunting and outdoor excursions
  • Inspect and clean ears at least once per week
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks
  • Check for cuts, ticks, or debris after field activities
  • Brush teeth several times per week for dental health

🎓 Training

The Poitevin is an intelligent but headstrong breed that can be challenging to train in a typical pet context. It responds well to positive reinforcement and is highly motivated by scent-based activities. Socialization from puppyhood is essential to raise a well-adjusted dog. Because the Poitevin was bred for pack work, it may struggle to focus on a single handler without proper engagement.

  • Begin obedience training and socialization by 8–10 weeks of age
  • Use positive reinforcement — food rewards and play work best
  • Keep training sessions short and varied to hold the dog's attention
  • Recall and leash training are critical given the breed's scent-driven independence
  • Exposure to different environments, people, and animals builds confidence
  • Consider involvement in tracking sports or field trials to channel instincts constructively

🏥 Health & Common Issues

The Poitevin is generally a robust and healthy breed, having been selected for working stamina over generations. Like most large, deep-chested hounds, it has some predisposition to bloat and joint issues. Routine veterinary care and maintaining a healthy weight are the best preventative measures.

🏠 Is a Poitevin Right for You?

The Poitevin is best suited to experienced dog owners, hunters, or those with a deep appreciation for working hounds. It is not a typical family pet and is rarely kept as one in France. Those who provide this breed with space, regular work, and the company of other dogs will find it a loyal, impressive, and deeply satisfying companion. It requires commitment to exercise and a thorough understanding of hound behavior.

🍽️ How Much to Feed a Poitevin

The Poitevin is a large, high-energy working breed that requires a nutritious, calorie-dense diet to support its active lifestyle. Feed a premium large-breed dog food with a named meat protein as the primary ingredient. Divide meals into two portions per day to reduce the risk of bloat, a condition to which deep-chested breeds are susceptible. Adjust portions based on activity level and body condition score.

Puppy (8–12 weeks)
3–4 small meals per day
Puppy (3–6 months)
3 meals per day
Adult (1+ year)
2 meals per day
Senior (7+ years)

✅ Best Foods

  • High-quality large-breed kibble with real meat (beef, chicken, or lamb) listed first
  • Active or working dog formulas with elevated protein and fat for sustained energy
  • Foods containing glucosamine and chondroitin to support joint health in a large-breed hound
  • Omega-3 rich options (salmon oil, fish-based foods) for coat and anti-inflammatory benefits
  • Always provide fresh water, especially after exercise or hunting activity

🎉 Amazing Facts About Poitevins

  • 🐺 The Poitevin was originally developed specifically to hunt wolves, which were a major threat to livestock in 17th-century France.
  • 👑 The breed was a favourite of French aristocrats and was often maintained in large, prestigious packs on noble estates.
  • ⚔️ The French Revolution nearly wiped out the Poitevin entirely, as many noble kennels were disbanded or destroyed during the upheaval.
  • 🔬 Breed enthusiasts reconstructed the Poitevin after the Revolution using closely related French hounds, carefully restoring its original type.
  • 🦌 Today, the Poitevin is used almost exclusively for hunting deer and large game in packs — it remains a true working hound at heart.
  • 🎨 Its tricolour coat — white, black, and tan — is considered one of the most visually striking among all French hound breeds.
  • 🌍 Outside of France, the Poitevin is extremely rare, making it one of the most obscure FCI-recognized breeds in the world.
