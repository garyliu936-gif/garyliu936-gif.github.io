Breed Overview

The Kooikerhondje (pronounced "KOY-ker-hond-ye" and meaning "small duck-decoy dog" in Dutch) is a cheerful, athletic, orange-and-white spaniel-type breed developed in the Netherlands for a very specific and historically fascinating purpose — working in "eendenkooi" duck decoy traps. These elaborate systems of channels, nets, and curved pipes were used to catch large numbers of wild ducks alive for food markets and for banding and study. The Kooikerhondje's role was to trot along the banks of the channels, its bushy, white-tipped tail wagging and glinting in the light, enticing curious ducks to follow it deeper into the narrowing trap where nets awaited. This unique working role required a dog with the exact right combination of size, coat color, movement, and tail action — and the Kooikerhondje was shaped by centuries of careful selection for this precise function.

The breed has a very long history in the Netherlands — dogs that appear to be Kooikerhondjes appear in Dutch paintings from the 17th century, including works by Jan Steen and Rembrandt van Rijn. However, the breed nearly disappeared entirely after World War II, its numbers devastated by the wartime disruption of Dutch society. In the 1940s, Baroness van Hardenbroek van Ammerstol undertook a nationwide search for surviving dogs matching the old type, eventually locating a dog named Tommy who became the foundation of the breed's revival. From that single surviving bloodline, Dutch breeders painstakingly rebuilt the Kooikerhondje population. The AKC recognized the breed in 2018. Today it is prized for its cheerful, adaptable personality and striking appearance.

Health Notes

  • Von Willebrand's disease — hereditary bleeding disorder; DNA testing available and recommended
  • Hereditary necrotizing myelopathy (ENM) — a serious neurological condition in the breed; genetic test available
  • Epilepsy — seizures reported in the breed at higher rates than average
  • Patellar luxation — small-medium breed joint issue; OFA evaluation recommended

Fun Facts About Kooikerhondjes

  • 🦆 The Kooikerhondje's entire existence is built around one of the most ingenious hunting devices ever created — the Dutch eendenkooi (duck decoy). These elaborate systems, still found preserved at historic estates in the Netherlands, consist of a central pond with curved channels leading off it, narrowing progressively and covered in netting. The Kooikerhondje would trot along the banks, its white tail flashing, while ducks — naturally curious about the movement — would follow the dog deeper into the narrowing channel. Specially trained ducks ("call ducks") sometimes accompanied the dog to encourage the wild ducks to follow. At the end of the channel, the ducks could be driven into a net-covered trap and caught alive by the thousands.
  • 🖼️ Dogs strongly resembling the Kooikerhondje appear in several famous Dutch Golden Age paintings — most notably in works by Jan Steen, where an orange-and-white dog with characteristic ear feathering and tail appears in domestic scenes. Some art historians also identify Kooiker-type dogs in Rembrandt's works and other 17th-century Dutch and Flemish paintings. This artistic documentation confirms that the breed type existed and was recognizable in the Netherlands by at least the 1600s — making it one of the few modern breeds with clear artistic records from the 17th century.
  • 🌺 The Kooikerhondje was saved from extinction by a single determined woman — Baroness van Hardenbroek van Ammerstol — who in the 1940s conducted a search throughout the Netherlands for dogs matching the traditional type. She eventually found Tommy, a farm dog in Friesland, whose appearance matched historical descriptions and images of Kooikerhondjes. Tommy became the sole foundation dog for the breed's modern revival. All modern Kooikerhondjes are descended from this single individual, making the breed a remarkable conservation success story but also creating a genetically narrow founding population that influences health monitoring to this day.
  • 🎀 The Kooikerhondje's most distinctive feature — the black-tipped ear feathering called "earrings" — is not merely decorative but was historically functional. In duck decoy work, the flashing black tips of the dog's ears were believed to capture the attention of ducks and enhance the luring effect as the dog moved through the channels. Whether or not this is true, the earrings became a fixed breed characteristic and remain a key identifying trait: Kooikers without proper earring markings are considered to lack type in show competition.
  • ⚡ The Kooikerhondje is often described as a breed with an "on/off switch" — capable of settling calmly indoors after exercise but able to switch instantly to full athletic intensity when given the opportunity to work or play. This adaptability was essential to its historical role: a decoy dog that was too energetic inside the house or too calm while working would be useless. The result is a breed that makes an excellent active companion — genuinely relaxed at home but ready and eager for vigorous outdoor activity at a moment's notice.
