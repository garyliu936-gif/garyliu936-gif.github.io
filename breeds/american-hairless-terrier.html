Breed Overview

The American Hairless Terrier (AHT) is the first and only hairless breed to have originated entirely in the United States — a genuinely American creation born not from deliberate exotic breeding but from a spontaneous natural mutation in a Rat Terrier litter in Louisiana in 1972. Edwin and Willie Scott of Trout, Louisiana discovered a hairless female puppy they named Josephine, and spent decades carefully developing the trait into a stable, healthy breed. Their work produced a dog that captures every bit of the Rat Terrier's firecracker terrier spirit in a completely unique package.

The American Hairless Terrier comes in two varieties: the fully hairless (born with a soft fuzz that disappears within a few weeks) and the coated variety (which carries the hairless gene but retains a short, dense coat). Both share the same alert, wedge-shaped head, muscular compact body, and high-spirited terrier personality. Hairless individuals have smooth, soft skin that can range from solid pink to spotted with a remarkable range of pigment patterns. AKC fully recognized in 2016, they are an ideal choice for allergy sufferers who want a full-sized terrier experience without the coat — though their bare skin requires sunscreen and cold-weather clothing.

Health Notes

  • Sunburn risk — needs pet-safe sunscreen outdoors
  • Cold-sensitive — needs clothing in cool weather
  • Skin prone to acne and scrapes without fur protection
  • Generally very healthy with excellent longevity

Feeding Your American Hairless Terrier

A small, high-energy terrier with no insulating coat, the AHT burns extra calories regulating body temperature — factor this into feeding if your dog is often in cool environments.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 12 mo)½–1 cup250–450 kcal
Adult (1–8 yrs)¾–1.5 cups350–550 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)½–1 cup300–450 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • Small-breed, high-protein formula suits their terrier metabolism
  • Omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids help maintain healthy, supple skin
  • Hairless dogs may need slightly more calories in cold weather for thermoregulation
  • Avoid overfeeding — extra weight is immediately visible on their lean frame

Fun Facts About American Hairless Terriers

  • 🌟 The entire breed traces to a single hairless puppy — "Josephine" — born in a Rat Terrier litter in Trout, Louisiana in 1972. Her owners, Edwin and Willie Scott, recognized her as something extraordinary and dedicated 12 years to selectively breeding her descendants until they reliably produced hairless offspring. Every American Hairless Terrier alive today descends directly from Josephine.
  • 🧬 The hairlessness gene in the AHT works differently from most other hairless breeds. In breeds like the Chinese Crested or Xolo, the hairless gene is dominant and linked to dental abnormalities (missing teeth). The AHT's hairlessness is caused by a completely different, recessive gene — and AHTs have full, normal dentition, a significant health advantage.
  • 🤧 The American Hairless Terrier is one of the most genuinely hypoallergenic dogs available — not just low-shedding, but truly non-shedding. Since most dog allergies are triggered by dander carried on shed fur (rather than the fur itself), the AHT's complete absence of a coat makes them a genuine option for many allergy sufferers who cannot live with any other breed.
  • ☀️ AHT owners discover a surprising secondary benefit: their dog's skin shows emotion vividly. When excited, embarrassed, or cold, the skin can visibly flush with pink or develop goosebumps — making them remarkably expressive in ways no coated dog could be. Owners describe being able to tell exactly how their dog is feeling just by looking at their skin.
  • 🇺🇸 The American Hairless Terrier holds the distinction of being the only terrier breed — and one of very few AKC breeds of any group — that was developed entirely within the modern United States. All other AKC terrier breeds trace their origins to the British Isles. This makes the AHT a genuinely unique American contribution to the world's dog heritage.
