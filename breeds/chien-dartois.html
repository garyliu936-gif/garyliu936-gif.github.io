🐾 Overview

The Chien d'Artois (also historically known as the Picard or Briquet) is one of France's oldest and most historically significant hunting hound breeds, originating in the Artois region of northern France. References to hounds from this region appear in French hunting literature as far back as the 14th century, and the breed was well established as a distinct type by the 17th century — prized by French hunters for its courage, powerful nose, and ability to hunt deer, boar, and hare across the flat, agricultural landscapes and forests of northern France.

By the early 20th century, the breed had been so thoroughly crossed with English Foxhounds and other breeds introduced during the 19th century's fox-hunting craze that the original Artois type nearly disappeared entirely. A careful reconstruction effort led by French breeder Audrechy in the 1970s and 1980s re-established the breed's original type using remaining examples that retained the authentic Artois conformation. The reconstructed breed received FCI recognition and today exists in modest numbers in France, respected as a living bridge to medieval French hunting culture.

😊 Temperament & Personality

The Chien d'Artois is energetic, bold, and passionate on the hunt — a true working pack hound at heart. At home it can be gentle and affectionate, but it requires an experienced owner who understands hound independence.

  • Bold and courageous in the field — a determined, energetic hunter
  • Affectionate and sociable with family when properly exercised
  • Strong pack instinct — thrives with other dogs
  • Independent thinker; recall unreliable near strong scents
  • Not typically aggressive but can be stubborn
  • Vocal on the trail — loud, resonant hound bay
  • Needs significant daily exercise — not a sedentary companion

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

  • Daily exercise: 60–90 minutes — a high-energy working hound
  • Needs room to run; best suited for rural or large suburban properties
  • Thrives with hunting, tracking, and field work
  • Secure fencing essential — will follow scent across property lines
  • Nose work and mantrailing are excellent outlets for urban owners
  • Not suited for apartment life

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

  • Short, dense, tricolor coat (black mantle, tan, and white) — minimal maintenance
  • Weekly brushing sufficient; more during seasonal shedding
  • Ears require weekly inspection and cleaning — hound conformation traps moisture
  • Bathe every 6–8 weeks; trim nails every 3–4 weeks
  • After field work, check for ticks and burrs in coat and paw pads

🎓 Training

  • Intelligent but requires patient, consistent handling — not naturally biddable
  • Positive reinforcement with food rewards; harsh corrections backfire
  • Recall training is a long-term project — critical given scent-following drive
  • Early socialization with people and animals reduces stubbornness
  • Excels in tracking, mantrailing, and field trials
  • Best suited for experienced hound handlers, hunters, or active families

🏥 Health & Common Issues

The Chien d'Artois is a robust, athletic breed with few breed-specific health issues. The reconstruction process used a narrow gene pool, so genetic diversity monitoring is important.

🏠 Is a Chien d'Artois Right for You?

The Chien d'Artois is best suited for active hunters, experienced hound owners, or rural families who can provide ample space and daily exercise. It is a working dog at heart that does not adapt well to low-activity households. For those who appreciate the history of French pack hunting culture, the Chien d'Artois is a remarkable living artifact — a medieval hunting hound that came within a generation of being lost forever.

💡 Tip: Boarding your Chien d'Artois?

The Chien d'Artois is a pack dog that generally tolerates boarding well when housed with other dogs. Alert staff to its high energy needs — daily exercise is non-negotiable. The breed's vocal nature means it will bay freely if bored or under-exercised, so facilities with outdoor run time are preferable. Its short coat is easy to maintain, and it is generally non-aggressive with staff and other dogs.

🧬 Chien d'Artois Mix Breeds

Designer or intentional Chien d'Artois crosses are essentially non-existent. The breed is maintained strictly as a purebred working hunting dog in France, and its small population makes any deliberate mixing counterproductive to the breed's preservation goals.

🎉 Amazing Facts About Chien d'Artois

  • 📜 The Chien d'Artois is one of the breeds referenced in Gaston Phoebus's celebrated 14th-century hunting treatise *Livre de Chasse* (Book of the Hunt), one of the most important documents in European hunting history. Gaston III, Count of Foix, described in extraordinary detail the characteristics, management, and uses of French hunting hounds of his era, and northern French hounds from the Artois region were already recognized as a distinct and valuable hunting type. This places the Artois hound lineage among the most historically documented dog breeds in Western Europe.
  • ⚠️ The breed's near-extinction is one of the most dramatic stories in French cynology. During the 19th century, English fox hunting became fashionable among the French aristocracy and wealthy bourgeoisie, and English Foxhounds were imported in large numbers and crossed freely with native French pack hounds to increase speed. By the early 20th century, the original Artois type — slower, more powerful, and better suited to cold-nose work than the English Foxhound — had been almost completely submerged by English blood. By mid-century, authentic Artois-type hounds could be counted on the fingers of one hand.
  • 🔬 The reconstruction of the Chien d'Artois in the 1970s–80s was accomplished primarily by breeder Audrechy, who identified the remaining examples of authentic Artois type and systematically bred them back toward the historical standard while eliminating English Foxhound influence. The success of this effort is a landmark case study in rare breed preservation — demonstrating that even breeds reduced to a tiny remnant population can be recovered if sufficient genetic material remains and reconstruction is guided by knowledgeable specialists.
  • 🌿 The Artois region of northern France, from which this breed takes its name, is a gently rolling agricultural landscape — chalk plateaus, valleys, and mixed forest — quite different from the mountainous terrain associated with many European hunting hounds. This landscape shaped the Chien d'Artois into a somewhat different type than its southern French counterparts: more powerful through the body, bred for endurance over varied open country rather than the steep Alpine terrain that shaped breeds like the Tyrolean Hound, requiring a dog that could hunt all day across open fields and woodland edges in pursuit of hare, deer, and boar.
  • 🏆 Despite its small numbers, the Chien d'Artois has attracted renewed interest in France among hunters who value native breeds over the English Foxhound crosses that dominate much of French pack hunting today. Breed clubs actively promote the Artois as a living heritage breed with irreplaceable historical value — a connection to centuries of French hunting culture that was nearly severed in the 20th century and deserves to be preserved for future generations.
