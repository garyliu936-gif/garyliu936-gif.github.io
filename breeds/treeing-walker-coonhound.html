Breed Overview

The Treeing Walker Coonhound is a fast, competitive American scent hound developed in the early 19th century in the American South. Descended from the English Foxhound and named for Thomas Walker (who imported English Foxhounds to Virginia in 1742), this breed was specifically refined for treeing — tracking quarry like raccoons, squirrels, bears, and mountain lions up into trees and holding them there with a sustained, chilling bay until the hunter arrives.

Off the trail, the Treeing Walker is an affectionate, energetic companion. They are known for their exceptional speed — the fastest of all the coonhound breeds — and their striking tricolor (black, white, and tan) coat. Indoors, they are calm and gentle; outdoors, they are tireless hunters driven by one of the most powerful noses in the dog world.

  • Polyradiculoneuritis (a paralysis condition)
  • Obesity if under-exercised

Feeding Your Treeing Walker Coonhound

A high-energy working breed, Treeing Walkers need quality nutrition to fuel long hunts. On non-hunting days, reduce portions to prevent weight gain.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 18 mo)2–3 cups800–1,200 kcal
Adult — active (1–7 yrs)2.5–4 cups1,000–1,600 kcal
Senior (7+ yrs)2–3 cups800–1,200 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • High-protein diet supports the athletic, muscular build
  • Feed twice daily to reduce bloat risk in this deep-chested breed
  • Omega-3 fatty acids support ear and skin health
  • Regular ear cleaning after water exposure prevents infections

Fun Facts About Treeing Walker Coonhounds

  • 🌲 The "Treeing" in the name refers to their primary job — chasing quarry up a tree and barking continuously (baying) at the base to hold it there until the hunter arrives.
  • 🏃 Treeing Walker Coonhounds are the fastest of all six AKC-recognized coonhound breeds — their longer legs and lighter build give them remarkable speed over open ground.
  • 🗺️ Their name honors Thomas Walker, an 18th-century explorer and Virginia frontiersman who brought English Foxhounds to America that eventually contributed to the breed's development.
  • 🐻 Beyond raccoons, Treeing Walkers are trained to chase and tree much larger prey including bears, mountain lions, and bobcats across rugged mountain terrain.
  • 🏛️ The AKC recognized the Treeing Walker Coonhound in 2012, making it one of the more recently recognized American breeds despite having a history going back nearly 300 years.
