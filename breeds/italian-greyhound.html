🐾 Overview

The Italian Greyhound (IG) is a miniaturized sighthound with a history stretching back over 2,000 years to ancient Egypt and Greece. During the Renaissance, they were prized by Italian nobility and depicted in countless paintings. Often called "IGs" by enthusiasts, they combine the speed and athleticism of a sighthound with the size and affection of a lapdog.

IGs are deeply devoted to their families and crave physical contact — they are notorious "velcro dogs" who love to burrow under blankets and curl up against their owners. Despite their delicate appearance, they are fast, agile runners who need regular bursts of exercise. Their short coat offers little insulation, making them cold-sensitive and big fans of sweaters.

😊 Temperament & Personality

Italian Greyhounds are known for being surprisingly bold for their small size. They form deep bonds with their families and thrive on consistent human connection. As a high-energy breed, they are best matched with active owners who enjoy outdoor activities and can commit to regular exercise.

Italian Greyhounds can be trained with patience and consistency, though they may occasionally test boundaries. With proper socialization they can do well with children, though supervision is always wise.

  • Energetic and playful — needs daily outlets and consistent exercise to stay balanced
  • Affectionate and people-oriented — thrives on closeness with their family
  • Generally good with kids when properly socialized from puppyhood
  • Capable learner who benefits from short, varied training sessions
  • Breed-typical personality is reliable and predictable — makes planning your lifestyle together easier
  • Mental stimulation is as important as physical exercise — puzzle toys and training prevent boredom

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

Italian Greyhounds are high-energy dogs that need significant daily exercise to stay physically and mentally healthy. Without adequate activity they can become restless, vocal, or destructive. They are happiest with owners who genuinely enjoy an active lifestyle.

  • Daily exercise: 1 – 1.5 hours of vigorous activity — split into morning and evening sessions
  • Short daily walks and indoor play sessions — tiny legs cover ground faster than you think, but overall distance needs are modest
  • Off-leash time in a securely fenced yard or dog park is highly beneficial
  • Mental enrichment (puzzle feeders, scent games, obedience training) is as important as physical activity
  • Without adequate exercise they may develop destructive habits from boredom
  • Puppies: limit impact exercise to 5 minutes per month of age to protect developing joints

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

Italian Greyhounds have a longer coat that requires consistent grooming to stay mat-free and healthy. Establishing a grooming routine early ensures your dog is comfortable being handled and brushed, which makes the process enjoyable for both of you.

  • Brush daily or every other day to prevent tangles and matting — especially behind the ears and under the legs
  • Professional grooming every 6–8 weeks helps maintain coat shape and length
  • Bathe every 4–6 weeks using a detangling shampoo and conditioner
  • Clean eyes and the area around the face regularly to prevent staining
  • Check and clean ears weekly — longer-eared breeds are prone to moisture buildup
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks

🎓 Training

Italian Greyhounds are moderately trainable and respond well to patient, consistent positive reinforcement. They benefit from clear expectations and a calm, confident trainer who establishes routines early.

Keep sessions short — 10 to 15 minutes — and always end on a success. Enrolling in a puppy or adult obedience class provides structured learning and valuable socialization.

  • Begin training and socialization as early as possible — the puppy window is critical
  • Use high-value rewards (real meat, cheese) to compete with distractions
  • Be consistent — the same rules must apply every session and every family member must agree
  • Toy breeds are often underestimated — they're very intelligent. "Small dog syndrome" comes from inconsistent rules. Train them exactly as you would a large dog.
  • Crate training establishes boundaries, aids house training, and gives your dog a safe personal space
  • A well-exercised dog is a focused dog — always exercise before training sessions for best results

🏥 Health & Common Issues

Italian Greyhounds are generally healthy for their size, though regular screening helps catch issues early. Responsible breeders screen breeding stock for the most common conditions, significantly reducing risk in puppies from health-tested parents.

🏠 Is a Italian Greyhound Right for You?

A Italian Greyhound is ideal for active families or individuals who genuinely enjoy outdoor activities and can commit to daily vigorous exercise. Their trainability makes them approachable for first-time owners who are prepared for an engaged lifestyle. Not the right fit for sedentary households or those with very limited time for exercise.

Feeding Your Italian Greyhound

IGs are small and naturally lean. Feed a high-quality small-breed formula twice daily. Their metabolism runs fast, so calorie-dense food supports their energy without overloading tiny stomachs.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 12 mo)¼–½ cup200–300 kcal
Adult (1–8 yrs)¼–½ cup200–280 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)¼ cup160–220 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • Dental health is critical — hard kibble or dental chews help
  • Calcium and phosphorus balance supports their fragile bone structure
  • Avoid obesity — extra weight stresses the fine legs
  • Warm meals in cold weather help these temperature-sensitive dogs

Fun Facts About Italian Greyhounds

  • 👑 Italian Greyhounds were favored by royalty throughout history — King James I, Catherine the Great, and Frederick the Great of Prussia all kept them.
  • 🎨 Renaissance paintings by Pisanello, Velázquez, and Van Eyck all depict Italian Greyhounds lounging beside noble ladies.
  • 💨 Despite their delicate frame, IGs can run up to 25 mph — faster than most medium-sized dogs.
  • 🌡️ They have essentially no body fat and a very thin coat, making them one of the most cold-sensitive breeds — many IG owners have an entire wardrobe of dog sweaters.
  • 🦴 Puppy Italian Greyhounds have remarkably fragile legs — jumping off furniture can cause fractures, so many IG owners use dog stairs everywhere.
