🐾 Overview

The Hungarian Greyhound (Hungarian Greyhound) is one of Europe's most ancient sighthound breeds, with roots tracing back over 1,000 years to the Steppe dogs of Central Asia. The ancestors of the Hungarian Greyhound were brought to the Carpathian Basin by the Magyar tribes during their conquest of the region in the late 9th century. For centuries the breed was used by Hungarian nobility and hunters for coursing hare, fox, and other game across the vast Hungarian plains (puszta).

The Hungarian Greyhound is larger and sturdier than the Greyhound, with a denser short coat that provides better protection in the harsher Central European climate. It is classified in FCI Group 10 with other sighthounds and is recognized as a Hungarian national breed alongside the Vizsla and Puli. Though rare outside Hungary and neighboring countries, the Hungarian Greyhound is gaining attention internationally for its athleticism, loyalty, and gentle home character.

😊 Temperament & Personality

The Hungarian Greyhound is a sensitive, affectionate, and loyal breed that bonds deeply with its family. Like most sighthounds, it tends to be reserved with strangers but is never aggressive. At home it is gentle and calm; outside it is an explosive athlete capable of breathtaking speed.

  • 💨 Blazingly fast outdoors — one of the fastest dogs in Central Europe
  • ❤️ Devoted and affectionate with its family; loves close contact
  • 🧍 Reserved and somewhat aloof with strangers — never aggressive
  • 🐕 Generally good with other dogs, especially other sighthounds
  • 🐈 High prey drive — caution needed around cats and small animals
  • 🏠 Calm and quiet indoors when adequately exercised

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

The Hungarian Greyhound is a high-energy sighthound that requires daily vigorous exercise, including opportunities to run at full speed. Like all sighthounds, it has a sprint-based exercise pattern — intense bursts of speed followed by long rest periods at home.

  • 🏃 Needs daily walks plus at least one off-leash sprint session in a secure area
  • 🔒 Must always be exercised in a fully enclosed, escape-proof area — sighthounds can outrun almost anything
  • 🌳 A large fenced yard is ideal; lure coursing is a perfect sport for this breed
  • 🛋️ After exercise, Hungarian Greyhounds are remarkably calm and couch-loving at home
  • 🏆 Excels at lure coursing, racing, and agility sports
  • ⚠️ Never let off-leash in an unfenced area — their chase instinct overrides recall

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

The Hungarian Greyhound has a short, smooth, dense coat that is extremely easy to care for. It is one of the lowest-maintenance breeds in terms of grooming. However, its thin skin and minimal body fat mean it can be sensitive to cold weather.

  • 🖌️ Weekly brushing with a rubber curry comb keeps the coat clean and gleaming
  • 🛁 Bathe every 6–8 weeks or as needed — the short coat dries very quickly
  • 🧥 Provide a coat or jumper in cold weather — sighthounds feel the cold acutely
  • 🦷 Brush teeth 2–3 times per week for dental health
  • ✂️ Trim nails monthly; check and clean ears weekly
  • 💆 Sighthounds appreciate gentle massage — helps maintain circulation and muscle condition

🎓 Training

Hungarian Greyhounds are intelligent but can be independent-minded — a characteristic of sighthounds bred to make split-second coursing decisions without human guidance. Positive, patient training works well; harsh methods will cause this sensitive breed to shut down entirely.

  • ✅ Positive reinforcement only — food rewards and praise are highly effective
  • 📋 Consistent, gentle leadership establishes trust and cooperation
  • 🧒 Socialize early and broadly to reduce stranger wariness
  • 🐕 Recall training is critical — practice in safe, enclosed areas first
  • 🏆 Thrives in lure coursing, agility, and free-run activities that satisfy hunting instincts
  • ⏳ Patient training yields a reliable, well-mannered companion; expect gradual progress

🏥 Health & Common Issues

The Hungarian Greyhound is generally a healthy, hardy breed. Like all sighthounds, it has unique physiology — lower body fat and different blood values than most breeds — which veterinarians must account for, especially when administering anesthesia. Owners should ensure their vet is familiar with sighthound-specific protocols.

🏠 Is a Hungarian Greyhound Right for You?

The Hungarian Greyhound suits active owners who can provide daily vigorous exercise and a secure, fenced running area. It is a gentle, affectionate home companion that is surprisingly calm indoors. It is not suitable for homes with small pets given its prey drive. First-time sighthound owners should research the unique needs of the breed before committing.

🍽️ How Much to Feed a Hungarian Greyhound

Hungarian Greyhounds are lean, athletic dogs with high metabolisms. Their diet should be high in quality protein to support muscle development and stamina. Because sighthounds have minimal body fat, maintaining healthy weight is especially important — they can look too thin even at a healthy weight.

Puppy (8–12 weeks)
3–4 small meals per day
Puppy (3–6 months)
3 meals per day
Adult (1+ year)
2 meals per day
Senior (7+ years)
2 smaller meals per day

✅ Best Foods for Hungarian Greyhounds

  • 🥩 High-protein kibble or raw diet with lean meat (chicken, rabbit, or fish) as the base
  • 🐟 Omega-3 fish oil supplements support joint health and the short, dense coat
  • ⚖️ Monitor weight carefully — visible ribs are normal for sighthounds; protruding hip bones are not
  • ⏱️ Feed at least two meals per day and wait 1 hour after meals before exercise to reduce bloat risk
  • 💧 Always provide fresh, clean water — especially important after sprinting exercise

🚫 Foods That Are Dangerous for Hungarian Greyhounds

Chocolate Grapes & Raisins Onions & Garlic Xylitol (artificial sweetener) Macadamia Nuts Alcohol Avocado Raw yeast dough

🦴 Healthy Treats

  • Carrots — low calorie, great for dental health
  • Blueberries — antioxidants and a sweet reward
  • Plain cooked chicken or turkey (no seasoning)
  • Apple slices (remove seeds and core)
  • Plain rice cakes — low-calorie training reward

💡 Tip: Boarding your Hungarian Greyhound?

Always bring your Hungarian Greyhound's regular food when boarding. Switching food suddenly can cause digestive upset.

🎉 Amazing Facts About Hungarian Greyhounds

  • 🏇 The Hungarian Greyhound has been clocked at speeds exceeding 60 km/h (37 mph), making it one of the fastest dog breeds in Europe.
  • 🏛️ Archaeological evidence suggests that sighthounds resembling the Hungarian Greyhound were present in the Carpathian Basin as far back as the 10th century CE.
  • 🇭🇺 The breed is considered a Hungarian national treasure alongside the Vizsla, Puli, Komondor, and Mudi.
  • 🌾 The Hungarian Greyhound was specifically developed for coursing on the open Hungarian steppe (puszta) — a flat, treeless landscape requiring speed above all else.
  • 🧬 The breed is distinct from the Greyhound — it is stockier, has a denser coat, and was developed independently from Western European sighthounds.
  • 💉 Like all sighthounds, Hungarian Greyhounds have unique blood chemistry and are sensitive to certain anesthetic drugs — always alert your vet to this before any procedure.
  • 🛋️ Despite their explosive speed, Hungarian Greyhounds are famously calm and lazy at home — earning sighthounds the nickname "45 mph couch potatoes."
