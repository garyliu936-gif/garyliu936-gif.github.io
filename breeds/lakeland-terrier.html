Overview

The Lakeland Terrier was developed in the Lake District of England to protect sheep from foxes during lambing season. Unlike most terriers that hunted solo, Lakies worked in packs with hounds, needing the stamina to keep up all day in rugged fell country.

Lakeland Terriers are bold, self-confident, and friendly. They tend to be more adaptable than many terriers and generally get along better with other dogs. Their wiry coat requires regular grooming, including hand-stripping for show dogs. They are spirited companions with a sense of humor.

Did You Know?

  • Worked in packs alongside hounds in the Lake District
  • AKC recognized in 1934
  • Won Best in Show at Crufts and Westminster in the same year (1967)
  • Originally called the Patterdale Terrier or Fell Terrier

Common Health Issues

  • Legg-Calvé-Perthes disease
  • Lens luxation
  • Hypothyroidism
  • Von Willebrand's disease

Is a Lakeland Terrier Right for You?

If you want a spirited, adaptable, and friendly terrier with genuine working heritage and a winning show record, the Lakeland is a great choice.

Lakeland Terrier Diet & Nutrition Guide

🐶 Puppy (0–12 months)

Approach: 3 meals daily of small-breed puppy formula

Calories: ½–¾ cup per day total

🐕 Adult (1–7 years)

Approach: 2 meals daily of quality small-breed food

Calories: ¾–1 cup per day

🦮 Senior (7+ years)

Approach: 2 meals daily of senior small-breed formula

Calories: ½–¾ cup per day

Key Nutritional Priorities

  • High protein for active terrier lifestyle
  • Omega fatty acids for wiry coat health
  • Joint support for mountain-bred dog
  • Antioxidants for immune function

Fun Facts About the Lakeland Terrier

  • In 1967, a Lakeland Terrier named Stingray of Derryabah won Best in Show at both Crufts and Westminster
  • Their compact size allowed them to follow foxes deep into rocky dens
  • Can climb nearly vertical rock faces when motivated
  • One of the most versatile and athletic terrier breeds
