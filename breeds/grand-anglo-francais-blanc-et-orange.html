🐾 Overview

The Great Anglo-French White and Orange Hound is one of three Grand Anglo-Français varieties, created in 19th-century France by crossing large French scenthounds — particularly those of Poitevin and Saintongeois lineage — with English Foxhounds. The "Blanc et Orange" (white and orange) variety is the rarest of the three Grand Anglo-Français types. It is a large, powerful pack hound built for the demanding French tradition of grande vénerie — mounted pack hunting of deer and wild boar over long distances. Like its Blanc et Noir and Tricolore siblings, it is a strictly working breed maintained almost exclusively in private French hunting packs. FCI recognized (Standard No. 322).

😊 Temperament & Personality

Bold, energetic, and pack-oriented. The Great Anglo-French White and Orange Hound shares the temperament of its sibling varieties — a confident, hard-driving pack hound that is sociable with other dogs, affectionate with familiar people, and completely focused on hunting when in the field. Its strong prey drive and independence from individual commands make it poorly suited to pet or urban life. In working pack conditions it is a passionate and tireless hunting partner.

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

Extremely high energy. Bred to run for many hours pursuing large game, this breed requires vast amounts of exercise in large, safe outdoor spaces. Standard urban or suburban exercise routines are entirely insufficient for its needs. Pack kenneling with regular hunting participation or field exercise is the only appropriate management approach for this breed. It is not a suitable companion for typical households.

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

The short, dense, smooth coat is minimal in maintenance needs. A weekly rub-down with a hound glove keeps the coat clean and glossy. The pendant ears must be inspected and cleaned regularly to prevent moisture buildup and infection — the primary health concern in this breed. Shedding is moderate. Bathing is infrequent unless after heavy field work. Nails require periodic trimming.

🎓 Training

Like all Grand Anglo-Français varieties, this breed is bred to work within a pack under a huntsman's direction rather than as an individually trained companion. It responds to pack dynamics and hunting cues but is not oriented toward individual obedience commands. Experienced French venery practitioners who understand pack hound management find it highly effective in the field. It is not suited to competitive obedience or companion dog roles.

🏥 Health & Common Issues

A generally robust working breed with a lifespan of 10–12 years. Ear infections are the most common recurring health issue due to pendant ears and outdoor work. Hip dysplasia can occur in large hound breeds. The breed's working population is maintained largely in pack kennels where active management and regular veterinary oversight are standard practice. Individual health data from pet ownership is extremely limited given the breed's working-only status.

🍽️ Feeding Guide

The Great Anglo-French White and Orange Hound does best on a good-quality complete dog food matched to its age, size, and activity level. As a rough guide, a typical adult eats about 2½–3¾ cups of dry food per day, split into two meals — but a hard-working or very active dog needs more, while a couch companion needs less. Use a large-breed formula and avoid heavy exercise right after meals to reduce the risk of bloat.

📏 Daily Portion Guide

Puppies: 3 meals a day of a puppy formula to fuel growth.
Active adults: around 2½–3¾ cups daily, in two meals, adjusted to keep a trim waistline.
Seniors (7+ yrs): slightly fewer calories and, if needed, a joint-supporting formula as they slow down.

🚫 Foods to Avoid

Never feed chocolate, grapes or raisins, onions, garlic, xylitol, macadamia nuts, or cooked bones. Keep treats to no more than about 10% of daily calories so they don't crowd out balanced nutrition or lead to weight gain.

💰 Cost Information

Where puppies are available, a Great Anglo-French White and Orange Hound from a responsible breeder typically costs around $1,000–$2,500, with rarer bloodlines and imported dogs sitting at the higher end. Rescue or adoption, where possible, is far less — usually a few hundred dollars in fees.

📅 Typical Monthly Costs

Food, treats & preventatives, routine care and supplies together usually run about $110–$200 per month for a healthy adult, not counting emergencies or boarding.

📊 Lifetime Estimate

Over a full lifetime the total cost of ownership generally lands around $18,000–$28,000, including the purchase price. Pet insurance ($15–$45/month) and a small emergency fund are smart ways to cushion against unexpected veterinary bills.

🎉 Fun Facts About the Great Anglo-French White and Orange Hound

  • The "Blanc et Orange" variety is the rarest of the three Grand Anglo-Français types — the Blanc et Noir is far more common, with the Tricolore in between.
  • Its orange markings derive from the French hound heritage — many old French breeds such as the Poitevin and the Billy carry the distinctive white-and-orange (lemon) coloration.
  • Like all Grand Anglo-Français, it participates in hunts that may involve running for 6–8 hours across many kilometers of French countryside — one of the most physically demanding activities a domestic dog can perform.
  • The breed is almost exclusively found on private hunting estates (châteaux) in France, typically kept in packs of 20–40 individuals managed by a professional huntsman.
  • FCI Standard No. 322 governs this variety, while the Blanc et Noir is Standard No. 323 and the Tricolore is Standard No. 324 — each is considered a distinct breed despite sharing the same origin.
