Overview

The Norfolwich crosses two closely related British terrier breeds that were only officially separated into distinct breeds in the 1960s. The Norfolk has drop ears; the Norwich has prick ears. Both are small, wiry, and feisty working terriers with very similar personalities.

Temperament

Feisty, loyal, and curious. Two nearly identical terrier breeds crossed together produce consistent, predictable results - a small, spirited working terrier.

  • Alert and curious
  • Loyal to family
  • Strong prey drive
  • Independent thinker

Exercise

Moderate - 30-45 minutes daily. Indoor play plus walks satisfy this small but active terrier type.

Grooming

Wiry coat needs hand stripping twice yearly for show dogs. Pet owners can opt for professional grooming or trimming instead.

Training

Intelligent but independent. Terrier stubbornness requires consistent positive reinforcement with patience. Food rewards work well.

Health

Generally healthy and long-lived terrier types. Luxating patella and dental disease are primary small-breed concerns.

Fun Facts About Norfolwich

👂

Ears Tell Them Apart

The only reliable visual difference between Norfolk and Norwich Terriers is their ears - Norfolk have folded drop ears, Norwich have erect prick ears. Norfolwich puppies can have either!

🦊

Fox Hunters

Both parent breeds were bred to flush foxes from their underground dens. Norfolwich dogs retain powerful digging and hunting instincts that need appropriate outlets.
