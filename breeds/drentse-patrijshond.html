Drentse Patrijshond

🐾 Overview

The Drentse Patrijshond (Dutch Partridge Dog) is one of the oldest and most distinctive of the continental pointing breeds, originating in the Drenthe province of the northeastern Netherlands. First documented in 16th-century Dutch paintings, it has remained largely unchanged for centuries — a testament to its excellence as a versatile hunting companion. Unlike many gun dogs, it was bred to work in small family hunting operations rather than large estate shoots, giving it an unusually calm and devoted temperament that makes it an exceptional family dog as well as an effective hunter. FCI recognized in 1943.

😊 Temperament & Personality

Calm, gentle, and deeply affectionate. The Drentse Patrijshond is one of the few gun dog breeds that transitions seamlessly from the field to the family home. It is exceptionally good with children, patient with other animals, and bonds closely with every member of the household. Less intense than many sporting breeds — a trait deliberately maintained throughout the breed's history.

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

Medium to high energy: 60–90 minutes of daily exercise keeps this breed happy. Enjoys long walks, swimming, field work, and nose games. It is adaptable — more content with moderate activity than most pointing breeds, though it still needs daily outdoor time. Suitable for active suburban families as well as rural homes.

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

Dense, slightly wavy coat with feathering on the ears, chest, and legs. Brush 2–3 times per week to prevent tangles in the feathered areas. Moderate shedder. Ears should be checked and cleaned regularly. Bathe only as needed; the coat has natural water resistance.

🎓 Training

Highly cooperative and sensitive — responds beautifully to gentle, positive training methods. Harsh corrections are counterproductive with this breed. A natural pointer and retriever with instincts that emerge early; field training is intuitive. Equally capable in obedience, agility, and tracking. A wonderful breed for active families new to gun dogs.

🏥 Health & Common Issues

Generally a healthy breed with a relatively small gene pool maintained by dedicated Dutch breed clubs. Watch for hereditary stomatocytosis (a rare blood condition specific to this breed and a few others), hip dysplasia, and progressive retinal atrophy (PRA). Health-tested breeding is practiced by reputable breeders.

🎉 Fun Facts About the Drentse Patrijshond

  • One of the few gun dog breeds depicted in 16th-century Dutch Golden Age paintings — appearing in hunting scenes alongside noblemen centuries before formal breed standards existed.
  • "Patrijshond" translates to "partridge dog" in Dutch — a direct reference to its original quarry on the Drenthe heathlands.
  • Deliberately bred for small family hunts rather than large estates — giving it a uniquely calm, home-loving temperament rare among pointing breeds.
  • Susceptible to hereditary stomatocytosis — a breed-specific blood disorder that responsible breeders DNA-test for.
  • FCI recognized in 1943; remains one of the Netherlands' most treasured native breeds alongside the Keeshond and Stabyhoun.
