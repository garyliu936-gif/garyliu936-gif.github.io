Breed Overview

The American English Coonhound — also called the Redtick Coonhound — is America's fastest coonhound, descended directly from English Foxhounds brought to the colonies by early American settlers including George Washington and Robert Brooke in the 17th century. Over generations of selective breeding for American terrain and quarry, the English Foxhound was refined into a specialized coonhound capable of hunting both by day on fox and by night on raccoon, opossum, and bear.

The American English Coonhound is athletic, built more like a foxhound than its heavier coonhound cousins — lean, long-legged, and fast. Its hot nose (ability to work fresh, fast-moving scent at speed) makes it different from the cold-nosed Bluetick or Bloodhound. They come in a variety of coat patterns including redtick, bluetick, tricolor, and red and white. Friendly and social with people, they are among the most vocal of all dog breeds — a characteristic that makes them spectacular hunters and challenging neighbors.

Feeding Your American English Coonhound

One of the most energetic dog breeds in existence, the American English Coonhound burns enormous calories during hunts. Their lean build needs consistent, quality fuel.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 12 mo)2–3 cups800–1,200 kcal
Adult (1–7 yrs)2.5–3.5 cups1,000–1,600 kcal
Senior (7+ yrs)2–2.5 cups800–1,100 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • High-protein diet sustains muscle mass and endurance during long hunts
  • Feed two meals rather than one — reduces bloat risk in deep-chested breeds
  • Avoid exercise 1 hour before and after feeding to prevent GDV
  • Monitor weight — they can lose condition quickly during active hunting seasons

Fun Facts About American English Coonhounds

  • 🏛️ George Washington was one of the most passionate dog breeders in early American history — he kept extensive packs of English Foxhounds and is credited with helping establish the lineage that eventually became the American English Coonhound. His breeding records at Mount Vernon are still preserved.
  • 🦊 Unlike other coonhounds bred exclusively for night hunting, the American English Coonhound historically worked both day and night — following fox during daylight hours and switching to raccoon after dark. This dual-purpose ability required a "hot nose" (following fresh scent quickly) rather than a "cold nose."
  • 📢 The American English Coonhound is considered one of the loudest dog breeds in the world. Their baying during a hunt carries for miles — a deliberate trait that allowed hunters on horseback to track the dog's location through dense forest. This vocal nature makes them challenging in suburban settings.
  • 🏃 Built for speed over Southern terrain — flatter, faster country than the Appalachian mountains where Plotts and Blueticks excel — the American English Coonhound is the most foxhound-like of all coonhound breeds, retaining the speed and long-striding gait of their English ancestors.
  • 🌈 The American English Coonhound comes in more coat color variations than almost any other coonhound — redtick, bluetick, tricolor, red and white, lemon and white, and brindle — making it one of the most visually diverse of the American coonhound breeds.
