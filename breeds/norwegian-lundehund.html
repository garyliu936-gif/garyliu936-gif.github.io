🐾 Overview

The Norwegian Lundehund (from "lunde" meaning puffin, and "hund" meaning dog) is unquestionably the most anatomically extraordinary dog breed in existence — a small Norwegian Spitz developed to hunt Atlantic puffins in the near-vertical sea cliffs of Vaeroy and Røst islands off the Norwegian coast. To navigate the crevices, overhangs, and tunnels of these brutal cliff faces, the Lundehund evolved — or was selectively developed — with a set of physical adaptations found in no other dog breed on Earth: six fully functional toes on each foot (instead of the normal four-plus-dewclaw), joints that allow the front legs to extend 90 degrees to the side like a human doing a Y, and a neck that can bend so far backward that the head touches the spine.

These extraordinary features were not accidental — each solved a specific problem of cliff hunting. The extra toes gave traction on wet, steep rock faces. The splaying legs allowed the dog to brace in narrow crevices. The folding neck allowed them to squeeze backward out of the rock tunnels where puffins nest. The ears can even fold completely shut (folding forward or backward) to keep out water and debris when crawling. The breed nearly became extinct twice in the 20th century — once during a distemper epidemic in the 1940s that reduced the entire world population to six dogs — and was saved only by extraordinary breeding efforts. AKC recognized in 2011, the Norwegian Lundehund remains one of the rarest breeds registered.

😊 Temperament & Personality

Norwegian Lundehunds are known for being friendly, active, and eager to please. They form deep bonds with their families and thrive on consistent human connection. As a high-energy breed, they are best matched with active owners who enjoy outdoor activities and can commit to regular exercise.

Norwegian Lundehunds can be trained with patience and consistency, though they may occasionally test boundaries. With proper socialization they can do well with children, though supervision is always wise.

  • Energetic and playful — needs daily outlets and consistent exercise to stay balanced
  • Affectionate and people-oriented — thrives on closeness with their family
  • Generally good with kids when properly socialized from puppyhood
  • Capable learner who benefits from short, varied training sessions
  • Breed-typical personality is reliable and predictable — makes planning your lifestyle together easier
  • Mental stimulation is as important as physical exercise — puzzle toys and training prevent boredom

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

Norwegian Lundehunds are high-energy dogs that need significant daily exercise to stay physically and mentally healthy. Without adequate activity they can become restless, vocal, or destructive. They are happiest with owners who genuinely enjoy an active lifestyle.

  • Daily exercise: 1.5 – 2+ hours of vigorous activity — split into morning and evening sessions
  • Vigorous daily exercise — swimming, fetch, trail hiking, or dog sports — plus mental enrichment
  • Off-leash time in a securely fenced yard or dog park is highly beneficial
  • Mental enrichment (puzzle feeders, scent games, obedience training) is as important as physical activity
  • Without adequate exercise they may develop destructive habits from boredom
  • Puppies: limit impact exercise to 5 minutes per month of age to protect developing joints

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

Norwegian Lundehunds have a dense double coat built for protection against the elements. They shed year-round with two major blow-out seasons in spring and fall. Regular brushing dramatically reduces the amount of hair around your home and keeps the coat healthy.

  • Brush 2–3 times per week; daily during heavy shedding seasons
  • Use a slicker brush followed by an undercoat rake to reach the dense undercoat
  • Bathe every 6–8 weeks — avoid overbathing as it strips the natural protective oils
  • Never shave a double coat — it disrupts insulation and the coat may grow back unevenly
  • Clean ears weekly and check for signs of infection
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks — they rarely wear down naturally indoors

🎓 Training

Norwegian Lundehunds are moderately trainable and respond well to patient, consistent positive reinforcement. They benefit from clear expectations and a calm, confident trainer who establishes routines early.

Keep sessions short — 10 to 15 minutes — and always end on a success. Enrolling in a puppy or adult obedience class provides structured learning and valuable socialization.

  • Begin training and socialization as early as possible — the puppy window is critical
  • Use high-value rewards (real meat, cheese) to compete with distractions
  • Be consistent — the same rules must apply every session and every family member must agree
  • Sporting dogs are among the most trainable breeds. They respond enthusiastically to positive reinforcement and food rewards. Begin obedience training early.
  • Crate training establishes boundaries, aids house training, and gives your dog a safe personal space
  • A well-exercised dog is a focused dog — always exercise before training sessions for best results

✅ Best Foods for Norwegian Lundehunds

Look for dog foods where the first ingredient is a named protein — chicken, beef, salmon, or lamb. Small-breed formulas are calibrated for their metabolism and nutritional needs.

  • Many Lundehunds require a specialized low-fat, highly digestible diet — consult your vet
  • Multiple small meals daily are often recommended to support GI health
  • Monitor weight carefully — GI issues can cause sudden weight loss
  • Vitamin B12 supplementation is often required for affected individuals

🚫 Foods That Are Dangerous for Norwegian Lundehunds

These common human foods can be toxic — even life-threatening — for dogs. Keep them safely out of reach at all times.

Chocolate Grapes & Raisins Onions & Garlic Xylitol (artificial sweetener) Macadamia Nuts Alcohol Avocado Raw yeast dough

🦴 Healthy Treats for Norwegian Lundehunds

  • Carrots — low calorie, great for dental health
  • Blueberries — antioxidants and a sweet reward
  • Plain cooked chicken or turkey (no seasoning)
  • Apple slices (remove seeds and core)
  • Plain rice cakes — low-calorie training reward
  • Commercial treats sized appropriately for a small breed

💡 Tip: Boarding your Norwegian Lundehund?

Always bring your Norwegian Lundehund's regular food when boarding. Switching food suddenly can cause digestive upset. Provide the facility your exact feeding schedule and portion sizes.

💡 Money-saving tip

Pet insurance is worth considering for any breed. Buying before your dog turns 1 gives the best rates and fewest pre-existing condition exclusions. Compare 2–3 providers before committing.

💡 How to Save Money as a Norwegian Lundehund Owner

  • Get pet insurance before your dog turns 1 — premiums are lower and pre-existing conditions won't be excluded
  • Buy food in larger bags when possible — significantly cheaper per pound
  • Learn basic grooming at home — brushing, ear cleaning, and nail trimming save groomer fees
  • Ask your vet about wellness plans — many clinics offer annual packages that bundle routine care
  • Use a rewards credit card for larger vet bills
  • Adopt instead of buying — rescue Norwegian Lundehunds are just as loving and cost a fraction of the price

🎉 Amazing Facts About Norwegian Lundehunds

Norwegian Lundehunds are full of surprises. Here are some of the most fascinating, funny, and heartwarming facts about this breed.

  • 🦶 The Norwegian Lundehund has six fully functional, load-bearing toes on each foot — a feature unique among all dog breeds and which gives them the most toes of any dog. The extra toe (an inner toe with two joints, plus an outer polydactyl toe) evolved for gripping the near-vertical wet rock faces of Norwegian sea cliffs. Each of those six toes ends in a working claw, giving the Lundehund a climbing grip found in no other domesticated animal.
  • 🤸 The Lundehund's shoulder joints allow the front legs to extend straight out to the sides — perpendicular to the body, like a person making a T shape with their arms — enabling them to brace across narrow cliff passages. Additionally, their neck bends so far backward that their head can rest flat against their spine, pointing upward. No other dog breed approaches this degree of skeletal flexibility, which was purpose-built for squeezing in and out of puffin burrows.
  • 🐦 Puffin hunting on Norwegian cliff islands was a major subsistence activity in the 16th–17th centuries — puffins were a significant food source, and their feathers were prized for filling and export. A single skilled Lundehund could retrieve several hundred puffins in a season, making them economically valuable to island communities. When the Danish government taxed dog ownership in the 18th century, many Lundehunds were killed, beginning the breed's long decline.
  • 🧬 The entire modern Norwegian Lundehund population traces to a tiny gene pool that nearly collapsed twice — first during a distemper epidemic in the 1940s that reduced the worldwide population to just six dogs, and again in the 1960s when disease struck again. This extreme genetic bottleneck explains why Lundehund Syndrome (the breed's characteristic GI disorder) is so widespread — virtually every living Lundehund is highly related to those six survivors.
  • 👂 The Lundehund's ears can fold completely shut in two directions — folding forward to close over the ear canal, or folding backward flat against the skull. This voluntary ear-closing ability is unique in the dog world and was developed to protect the ear canal from water, debris, and soil when the dog was crawling backward through tight sea-cliff burrows. Owners find that Lundehunds often close their ears when bathed, which is entirely intentional on the dog's part.
