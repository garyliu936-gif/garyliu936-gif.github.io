Breed Overview

The Redbone Coonhound is one of America's oldest and most recognized native breeds — a striking, deep-red hunting hound developed in the American South from foxhounds brought by Scottish immigrants in the 1700s. Named for their distinctive rich mahogany coat, Redbones are celebrated for their exceptional "cold nose" — the ability to follow scent trails that are many hours, or even days, old through any terrain and weather.

Redbones are passionate hunters who have historically been used to track raccoon, bear, mountain lion, deer, and wild boar. Their deep, melodious bay carries for miles through mountain hollows. At home, they are calm, sweet-natured, and affectionate — often described as "easygoing" for a hound. They love people and are known for their soulful, pleading eyes. They do require significant exercise and are happiest with a job to do.

Health Notes

  • Hip and elbow dysplasia
  • Ear infections — long floppy ears trap moisture
  • Obesity if under-exercised
  • Generally a very healthy, robust breed

Feeding Your Redbone Coonhound

An active, athletic hound, Redbones need quality nutrition calibrated to their activity level. On active hunting days they burn significantly more calories than on rest days.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 18 mo)2–3 cups800–1,200 kcal
Adult — active (1–7 yrs)2.5–3.5 cups1,000–1,500 kcal
Senior (7+ yrs)2–3 cups800–1,100 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • High-quality protein supports hunting endurance and athletic build
  • Feed twice daily rather than once to reduce bloat risk
  • Omega-3 fatty acids support skin, coat, and ear health
  • Clean ears regularly after swimming or water exposure

Fun Facts About Redbone Coonhounds

  • 📚 Two Redbone Coonhounds — Old Dan and Little Ann — are the beloved heroes of Wilson Rawls' 1961 novel "Where the Red Fern Grows," one of the best-selling children's books in American history and a staple of school reading programs for generations.
  • ❄️ Redbones are celebrated for their exceptional "cold nose" — the ability to follow scent trails that are hours or even days old, through snow, rain, and rugged terrain that defeats most other hound breeds.
  • 🔴 The Redbone's rich mahogany coat is its most defining feature — the AKC breed standard requires a deep red color and allows no other. The name "Redbone" refers directly to this distinctive coloring.
  • 🇺🇸 The Redbone Coonhound is a purely American breed, developed from foxhounds brought by Scottish immigrants to the American South in the 1700s. It was registered with the UKC in 1902 but wasn't AKC-recognized until 2009.
  • 🎬 A Redbone Coonhound named "Copper" is the star of Disney's 1981 animated film "The Fox and the Hound" — making the breed one of the most beloved animated dog characters in film history.
