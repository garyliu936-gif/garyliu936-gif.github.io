Breed Overview

The Saint Bernard — also spelled St. Bernard — is one of the world's most iconic dog breeds. Originally bred by monks at the Great St. Bernard Hospice in the Swiss Alps to rescue travelers lost or buried in snow, these dogs became legendary for their ability to locate and warm avalanche victims over centuries of work in the mountain passes between Switzerland and Italy.

Today's Saint Bernard is a family companion rather than a working rescue dog, but retains the breed's hallmark gentleness, patience, and devotion. They come in two coat varieties: short-haired (smooth) and long-haired (rough). Both types share the same massive, barrel-chested frame, expressive face with drooping jowls, and calm, loving temperament.

Temperament

Saints are famously patient, gentle, and friendly — earning the reputation as one of the best family dogs for households with children. They're affectionate without being overwhelming (except possibly their drool) and tend to be calm, easygoing, and non-aggressive. They're naturally gentle with small children and tend to show remarkable patience. However, like all giant breeds, they should be supervised with toddlers purely due to the risk of accidentally knocking them over.

Exercise Needs

Saint Bernards have moderate exercise needs despite their working heritage. Two 20–30 minute walks per day plus some low-key play satisfies most adults. Over-exercising puppies is particularly dangerous — Saints have an extended growth period (up to 2–3 years) during which excessive running or jumping can cause lasting joint damage. Avoid exercise in hot weather — their large body mass and thick coat make them prone to overheating quickly.

✈️ Travel Tip: At 120–180 lbs, Saint Bernards must travel as cargo on commercial flights. Many airlines ban giant breeds outright or restrict brachycephalic-adjacent large breeds during warmer months due to heat risk. Their massive size and tendency to drool makes ground transport in a van or large SUV typically more practical. Saints are generally calm travelers once properly crate-acclimated.

Grooming

The smooth-coated Saint Bernard requires weekly brushing to manage shedding. The long-haired variety needs brushing 3–4 times per week to prevent mats, especially around the ears, collar area, and hindquarters. Both types are heavy shedders, particularly in spring and fall. Drool management is a major aspect of Saint Bernard ownership — their loose jowls produce significant saliva, especially after drinking, eating, or exercise. Keep towels accessible throughout the house. Ear cleaning weekly is important to prevent infections in their floppy ears.

Training

Saints are gentle, eager-to-please dogs that respond well to positive reinforcement. They're not as quick to learn as working breeds but they're biddable and genuinely want to please their families. Early training is absolutely critical for giant breeds — a poorly trained 150-lb dog is genuinely dangerous not through aggression but through sheer weight and momentum. Basic obedience and leash manners should be instilled before the dog reaches full size. They do not respond well to harsh correction methods.

Health Considerations

Giant breeds have specific health challenges, many related to the stresses of carrying such a large body frame. Saint Bernards have a relatively short lifespan for a beloved companion dog.

Hip & Elbow Dysplasia Bloat (GDV) Heart Disease (DCM) Osteosarcoma Entropion (Eye) Epilepsy

Bloat (GDV) is a life-threatening emergency that Saint Bernards are at elevated risk for due to their deep chest. Prophylactic gastropexy (stomach tacking surgery, often done at time of spay/neuter) is frequently recommended by veterinarians. Feeding two smaller meals instead of one and avoiding exercise immediately after meals also reduces risk significantly.

Feeding Your Saint Bernard

Adult Saint Bernards typically eat 5–8 cups of high-quality giant breed dry kibble per day, split into at least two or three meals. Giant breed puppy formula is critical during growth — it controls the calcium/phosphorus ratio that affects bone development.

Nutrition Tips

  • Always use giant/large breed puppy formula — regular puppy food can cause dangerous overgrowth
  • Never feed one large meal per day — split into 2–3 meals to reduce bloat risk
  • Glucosamine and chondroitin supplements support joint health under the heavy frame
  • Omega-3 fatty acids support heart, joint, and coat health
  • Watch weight carefully — even a few extra pounds is significant stress on giant breed joints

Foods to Avoid

  • Grapes and raisins (toxic)
  • Onions and garlic (toxic)
  • Chocolate (toxic)
  • Gas-producing foods (reduces bloat risk)
  • High-fat table scraps (pancreatitis risk)

Popular Saint Bernard Mixes

Saint Berdoodle (Saint Bernard + Poodle)

The Saint Berdoodle combines the Saint's gentle giant personality with the Poodle's intelligence and potentially reduced shedding. One of the most popular giant doodle mixes — affectionate, friendly, and often more manageable in terms of drool than a purebred Saint.

Saint Bernese (Saint Bernard + Bernese Mountain Dog)

Two Swiss mountain breeds combine in the Saint Bernese — a massive, tri-colored gentle giant with the Saint's patience and the Bernese's striking coat pattern. Typically calmer than both parents, with a deeply loyal family temperament.

Saint Lab (Saint Bernard + Labrador)

The Saint Lab blends the Lab's friendly, trainable personality with the Saint's gentle giant nature. Typically large, affectionate, and excellent with children — often more energetic than a purebred Saint and somewhat easier to train.

Saintweiler (Saint Bernard + Rottweiler)

A powerful, loyal mix of two historically working breeds. The Saintweiler tends to be a calm, protective family dog — typically inheriting the Saint's patience with the Rottweiler's confident, watchful nature. Requires experienced handling.

Fun Facts About Saint Bernards

  • 🏔️ Real Rescuers: The dogs at the Great St. Bernard Hospice in Switzerland are documented to have rescued more than 2,000 travelers from the Alpine passes between the early 1700s and mid-1800s. Their ability to locate buried avalanche victims was extraordinary.
  • 🪣 The Barrel Myth: The iconic image of a Saint Bernard carrying a small barrel of brandy around its neck is fictional — popularized by an 1820 painting by Edwin Landseer. The real rescue dogs carried no such barrels. The tradition was never part of their actual work.
  • 🐕 Barry the Legend: The most famous Saint Bernard, Barry (1800–1814), is credited with saving between 40–100 lives during his work at the hospice. His preserved body is displayed in Berne's Natural History Museum. A statue at the Cimetière des Chiens near Paris honors his memory.
  • 🎬 Beethoven: The 1992 film "Beethoven" and its sequels starring a lovable Saint Bernard introduced a new generation to the breed — and reportedly caused a surge in Saint Bernard puppy sales followed by a wave of shelter surrenders when owners underestimated the reality of owning a 150-lb drooling giant.
  • 📏 Record-Setters: The heaviest Saint Bernard on record weighed 357 lbs. While exceptional, Saints regularly reach 200 lbs in some breeding lines — among the heaviest dog breeds in the world.
  • ❄️ Snow Search Dogs: Saints were said to be able to detect travelers buried under feet of snow using their extraordinary sense of smell — and would then lie on top of them to share body heat while a companion dog returned to the hospice for help.
  • 🍺 Swiss Icon: The Saint Bernard is considered a cultural symbol of Switzerland, alongside the Swiss Army Knife, watches, and chocolate. Several hospices in the Swiss Alps still keep Saint Bernards as living heritage animals today.
