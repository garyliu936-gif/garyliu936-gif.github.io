Breed Overview

The Bloodhound is the world's most accomplished scent tracker — a large, gentle hound with an extraordinary nose that has been refined over centuries of breeding in Belgium and later England. The breed's tracking ability is so precise and reliable that Bloodhound evidence is legally admissible in US courts — the only dog breed whose trailing results carry such legal weight.

Despite their fearsome name (derived not from blood-lust but from "blooded hound" — meaning pure-bred nobility), Bloodhounds are gentle, affectionate, and famously stubborn. Their wrinkled face with drooping ears and soulful eyes gives them one of the most expressive and sympathetic faces in the dog world. They're lovable, often comical companions for patient owners who appreciate a dog that truly marches to the beat of its own (very scent-focused) drummer.

Temperament

Bloodhounds are gentle, patient, and affectionate with their families. They're generally good with children and other pets, tolerating the chaos of family life with remarkable equanimity. They're not aggressive — quite the opposite. However, they can be willfully independent, especially when their nose picks up an interesting scent. Once on a trail, a Bloodhound's focus narrows completely, and they become oblivious to commands. They can be vocal — their deep, melodious howl (called "baying") is impressive and can bother neighbors.

Exercise Needs

Bloodhounds need moderate daily exercise — 30–60 minutes of walking or sniffing exploration is ideal. They're not built for speed but can walk endlessly following a scent trail. They should always be on leash or in a securely fenced area — a Bloodhound on a scent will follow it without regard for traffic, boundaries, or calling. Nose work and tracking activities provide excellent mental exercise that leaves them happily tired.

✈️ Travel Tip: At 80–110 lbs, Bloodhounds must travel as cargo on commercial flights. Their droopy faces (though not fully brachycephalic) and large size means airlines may restrict them in summer heat. Ground transport in an SUV or van is generally better for this breed. Bloodhounds drool significantly, especially when excited or warm — travel with towels and plan for drool management during any journey.

Grooming

The Bloodhound's short, dense coat is relatively low-maintenance — weekly brushing removes loose hair. However, their numerous facial wrinkles and skin folds must be cleaned regularly (daily if possible) to prevent moisture buildup, bacterial growth, and skin infections. Their long, pendulous ears trap debris and restrict airflow — weekly ear cleaning is essential to prevent the chronic ear infections Bloodhounds are prone to. They drool significantly — especially after drinking, eating, or tracking — towels are a permanent fixture in Bloodhound homes.

Training

Training a Bloodhound requires patience, creativity, and a sense of humor. They are intelligent but extraordinarily independent — when their nose says "go this way," no amount of human instruction will redirect them. They respond to positive reinforcement, especially food-based rewards, but they have their own agenda. Basic obedience can be achieved, but a perfectly obedient Bloodhound is rare. The best approach is to channel their natural instincts through tracking, nose work, and mantrailing activities rather than fighting their instincts.

Health Considerations

Bloodhounds are prone to several health conditions, many related to their loose, wrinkled skin and large body size.

Bloat (GDV) Hip & Elbow Dysplasia Ear Infections Entropion/Ectropion (Eyes) Skin Fold Dermatitis Heart Disease

Bloat is the most serious health risk for Bloodhounds — their deep chest makes them highly susceptible, and it can be fatal within hours without emergency treatment. Prophylactic gastropexy is strongly recommended. Entropion and ectropion (inward and outward-rolling eyelids) are common due to the loose facial skin, often requiring surgical correction.

Feeding Your Bloodhound

Adult Bloodhounds typically eat 3–5 cups of high-quality large breed dry kibble per day, split into two meals. Given their significant bloat risk, meal splitting and avoiding exercise after eating are critical management steps.

Recommended Daily Intake

Nutrition Tips

  • Large breed puppy formula is essential — controls bone and joint development rate
  • Never use raised food bowls — contrary to old advice, they may increase bloat risk
  • Omega-3 fatty acids support healthy skin and reduce skin fold inflammation
  • Avoid exercise within 1 hour before or after meals — bloat prevention is critical
  • Fresh water always available — Bloodhounds drink heavily, especially after tracking

Foods to Avoid

  • Grapes and raisins (toxic)
  • Onions and garlic (toxic)
  • Chocolate (toxic)
  • Gas-producing foods (beans, dairy) that can contribute to bloat risk

Popular Bloodhound Mixes

Bloodhound-Labrador Mix (Labloodhound)

The Labloodhound combines the Lab's friendly, trainable personality with the Bloodhound's extraordinary nose. A large, affectionate dog that often retains impressive scenting ability alongside the Lab's more manageable temperament — popular with search and rescue organizations.

Bloodhound-Beagle Mix (Beagle Hound)

Two scent hound breeds combine in this medium-sized mix — inheriting exceptional noses from both sides. Typically more compact than a purebred Bloodhound, with the Beagle's slightly more trainable nature. Still very scent-driven and independent.

Bloodhound-German Shepherd Mix

Combining tracking mastery with GSD trainability, this mix is popular in law enforcement and search and rescue contexts. Large, capable, and loyal — requires experienced handling and substantial exercise.

Bloodhound-Basset Hound Mix

Two scent-oriented hound cousins create a medium-to-large, wrinkled, soulful dog with an incredible nose and a legendarily stubborn personality. Slower and more low-key than many mixes — a charming, if unhurried, companion.

Fun Facts About Bloodhounds

  • 👃 300 Million Receptors: Bloodhounds have approximately 300 million olfactory receptors in their noses — compared to about 5 million in humans. Their scenting ability is estimated to be 1,000–10,000 times more powerful than ours.
  • ⚖️ Legally Admissible: Bloodhound trailing evidence is legally admissible in US courts — the only dog breed whose nose-work results can be presented as evidence in criminal proceedings. Their accuracy has been validated in thousands of criminal cases.
  • 📅 Days-Old Trails: A Bloodhound can follow a scent trail that is several days old — across multiple surfaces, through weather changes, and even across water to some extent. No machine has been invented that can replicate their tracking ability.
  • 🏰 Medieval Origins: The Bloodhound's history traces to medieval Belgium, where monks at the Abbey of Saint-Hubert bred exceptional tracking hounds known as "Chiens de Saint-Hubert." William the Conqueror brought them to England in 1066.
  • 🎭 The Name Explained: "Bloodhound" does not refer to following blood trails — it refers to the breed being a "hound of pure blood" (i.e., a carefully maintained aristocratic breed). The name is about pedigree, not violence.
  • 😴 On/Off Switch: Bloodhounds have a remarkable on/off switch — when not tracking, they're often among the most laid-back, couch-loving dogs imaginable. The same dog that tracked a fugitive for 130 miles will happily snooze for 18 hours afterward.
  • 💧 Champion Droolers: Those long, pendulous ears aren't just decorative — they help funnel scent molecules upward toward the nose when tracking low to the ground. The loose skin folds around the face serve a similar purpose, trapping scent particles.
