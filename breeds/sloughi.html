Breed Overview

The Sloughi (pronounced "SLOO-ghee") — also called the Arabian Greyhound or North African Greyhound — is one of the oldest sighthound breeds in the world, with a history among the Berber people of North Africa (Morocco, Algeria, Tunisia, and Libya) stretching back at least 3,000–5,000 years. Cave paintings in the Sahara and ancient Egyptian tomb art both depict smooth-coated, lean sighthounds remarkably similar to the modern Sloughi, suggesting a continuous hunting tradition of extraordinary age.

Unlike the Saluki (the Middle Eastern sighthound often confused with the Sloughi), the Sloughi is strictly smooth-coated — no feathering on the ears or tail — and carries a somewhat different spirit. Berber nomads considered Sloughis almost sacred, treating them as valued family members who slept inside the tent and were mourned when they died. They are coursing dogs built to run down gazelle, rabbit, fox, and jackal across flat desert terrain at tremendous speeds. As companions they are gentle, deeply loyal to their family, and characteristically reserved with strangers — a combination that has given them a reputation as the "cat of the dog world."

Feeding Your Sloughi

A lean, deep-chested sighthound, the Sloughi needs quality nutrition without overfeeding. Sighthound metabolism and body composition is unique — they need more fat and protein relative to carbohydrates than many breeds.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 18 mo)2–3 cups700–1,200 kcal
Adult (1.5–8 yrs)2–2.5 cups800–1,100 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)1.5–2 cups650–900 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • High-quality protein and fat supports the sighthound's lean, athletic build
  • Avoid overfeeding — even small amounts of excess weight are very visible on a Sloughi's frame
  • Feed two small meals to reduce bloat risk — never one large meal for a deep-chested breed
  • Inform your vet of sighthound anesthesia sensitivity before any procedures

Fun Facts About Sloughis

  • 🎨 Images strikingly similar to the Sloughi appear in ancient Egyptian tomb paintings from over 3,000 years ago — smooth-coated, lean, upright-eared sighthounds used in desert hunting scenes. Similar dogs appear on artifacts from Mesopotamia and North Africa, suggesting a continuous cultural tradition of smooth sighthound breeding spanning millennia.
  • 🕌 Among the Berber people of North Africa, the Sloughi holds a semi-sacred status unlike almost any other dog breed in the world. While Islamic tradition generally considers dogs ritually unclean, the Sloughi was the notable exception — Berber hunters considered their Sloughis honored partners, permitted inside the tent, mourned at death, and never sold.
  • ⚡ The Sloughi was bred to course gazelle across open desert — an animal that can sustain speeds of 60 mph. The Sloughi's top speed approaches 45 mph, making them one of the fastest dog breeds on Earth. Unlike the Greyhound (which is built for short sprints), the Sloughi evolved for extended endurance at high speed over varying desert terrain.
  • 🧬 DNA analysis has confirmed the Sloughi as one of the oldest genetically distinct dog breeds — their DNA profile shows minimal relationship with European dog breeds and a direct genetic lineage to ancient North African dogs. This makes them a genuine "living fossil" of the ancient dog world.
  • 😢 Sloughis are famous for mourning deeply when a family member dies — there are well-documented accounts of Sloughis refusing to eat after the death of their primary owner, sometimes for days. This profound emotional sensitivity is a signature trait that Berber owners describe as making them "more like a family member than a dog."
