Breed Overview

The Nederlandse Kooikerhondje (pronounced "NAY-der-lahn-se KOY-ker-hond-ye") — often shortened to "Kooiker" — is a small Dutch sporting spaniel with one of the most unusual job descriptions in dog history: luring ducks into "kooientraps" (duck decoy pipe traps) used by Dutch hunters for sport and commercial duck catching from the 1600s onward. The dog's role was to flash its tail with its distinctive black ear tips in view of ducks, arousing the birds' natural curiosity enough to follow the dog into the narrowing tunnel trap, where they were captured alive.

The Kooiker is a beautiful, compact spaniel with a distinctive orange-red and white parti-color coat, feathered ears, and those famous black ear tips ("earrings") that were the key feature for the decoy work. They appear in 17th-century Dutch Golden Age paintings — Jan Steen's "The Merry Family" includes a dog that is unmistakably a Kooiker. Nearly extinct by the 1940s, the breed was revived by Baroness van Hardenbroek van Ammerstol. In the US, they were AKC recognized in 2018 and are rapidly gaining a devoted following for their cheerful, adaptable temperament.

Feeding Your Nederlandse Kooikerhondje

A small, moderately active sporting spaniel, the Kooiker thrives on quality small-to-medium breed nutrition. Their feathered coat benefits from omega fatty acid support.

Nutrition Tips

  • Small-to-medium breed formula at an appropriate life stage
  • Omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids maintain the silky, feathered coat
  • Avoid overfeeding — spaniels can become pudgy without adequate activity
  • Support joint health with appropriate supplements if very active

Fun Facts About Nederlandse Kooikerhondjes

  • 🦆 The Kooiker's historic job was uniquely Dutch — they were trained to run playfully along the edges of "eendenkooi" (duck pipe) traps, staying just visible to ducks on the water. Ducks, curious about the strange dog and attracted by the flash of its white-and-orange tail, would follow it into narrowing tunnel nets and be captured alive.
  • 🖼️ Kooikers appear in multiple Dutch Golden Age paintings from the 17th century — including works by Jan Steen, Jan Vermeer, and Rembrandt — making them one of the most historically documented dog breeds in European art. Their distinctive orange-and-white coloring and black ear tips are unmistakable across centuries.
  • 🏰 A Kooiker named "Kuntze" is credited with saving the life of William III of Orange (William of Orange) in 1572 by awakening him before a surprise Spanish assassination attempt at Hermigny — barking and pawing at the prince until he woke and fled. William rewarded the breed with royal patronage, cementing their status in Dutch culture.
  • ⚠️ By the 1940s, the breed had fallen to near-extinction — fewer than 25 individuals could be found in the Netherlands. Baroness van Hardenbroek van Ammerstol began a painstaking search of Dutch farms in the 1940s, eventually finding enough dogs to begin a revival breeding program that has successfully restored the breed.
  • 🧬 The Nederlandse Kooikerhondje was recognized by the AKC in 2018, making it a relative newcomer to the American show ring — but its popularity has grown rapidly, driven by its striking appearance, manageable size, and wonderfully balanced temperament that makes it one of the best all-around small sporting breeds available.
