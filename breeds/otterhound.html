🐾 Overview

The Otterhound is one of the oldest, rarest, and most distinctive of all British hound breeds — a large, shaggy, deeply devoted dog bred specifically to hunt otter in the rivers and streams of medieval England. With its extraordinarily dense, rough, oily double coat (water-resistant enough to work in cold water for extended periods), large webbed feet ideal for swimming, and a nose capable of following an otter's underwater scent trail across a river, the Otterhound is uniquely adapted to aquatic hunting in a way no other breed can match. The breed has been documented in England since at least the 12th century, with some historians suggesting connections to ancient French hound breeds brought over after the Norman Conquest.

Otter hunting was practiced in England for centuries, partly because otters were considered a pest that depleted fish stocks from rivers and streams important to the food supply. Packs of Otterhounds were maintained by dedicated masters of otterhound packs, hunting the riverbanks in summer when foxhunting was out of season. When otter hunting was banned in England in 1977 (the European otter having become endangered), the primary purpose for the Otterhound's existence disappeared almost overnight — and the breed fell into drastic decline. Today the Otterhound is one of the rarest breeds in the world, with some years seeing fewer than 50 puppies registered in the UK and fewer than 400 total estimated worldwide. The breed's large size, need for space, and shaggy coat that requires significant maintenance have limited its appeal as a companion breed, and dedicated breed enthusiasts are working urgently to ensure its survival.

😊 Temperament & Personality

Otterhounds are known for being driven by instinct — scent or sight. They form deep bonds with their families and thrive on consistent human connection. They strike a good balance — lively enough to enjoy playtime and adventures, but calm enough to settle indoors when the day is done.

Otterhounds can be trained with patience and consistency, though they may occasionally test boundaries. They are typically gentle and patient with children, making them a reliable family companion.

  • Moderately active — enjoys exercise and playtime but appreciates downtime at home
  • Affectionate and people-oriented — thrives on closeness with their family
  • Patient and gentle with children — a dependable family companion
  • Capable learner who benefits from short, varied training sessions
  • Breed-typical personality is reliable and predictable — makes planning your lifestyle together easier
  • Genuinely enjoys relaxing — equally happy on a couch as on a trail with the right owner

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

Otterhounds need regular daily exercise to maintain a healthy weight and an even temperament. They are versatile companions who enjoy active outings but are equally content to relax at home after their needs are met.

  • Daily exercise: 45 – 60 minutes of moderate activity
  • Daily off-leash running in a securely fenced area for sighthounds; long scent walks for scenthounds — never trust off-leash without a fence
  • Daily walks, play sessions, and occasional trips to a dog park are ideal
  • Mental enrichment (puzzle toys, training) complements physical exercise
  • Adjust intensity based on age — puppies and seniors need gentler, shorter sessions
  • Aim for a mix of on-leash walks and free play

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

Otterhounds have a distinctive wiry, rough coat that benefits from hand-stripping to maintain its correct texture and weather-resistant properties. Clipping changes the coat texture over time and removes the protective outer layer.

  • Hand-strip the coat 1–2 times per year to maintain proper wire texture
  • Brush weekly to remove loose dead hair and keep the undercoat tidy
  • Bathe every 6–8 weeks — overbathing softens the harsh outer coat
  • Check and clean beard and leg furnishings regularly for trapped food or dirt
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks and clean ears weekly
  • If hand-stripping is not preferred, regular clipping is an acceptable alternative — just know the coat texture will change

🎓 Training

Otterhounds have an independent side that can make training a test of patience. They are intelligent, but they may decide when (or if) they want to cooperate. Harsh corrections make this worse — they simply disengage.

High-value food rewards, very short sessions, and variety are your best tools. Consider working with a professional trainer who has experience with independent breeds. Once they understand what's expected, they can be reliably trained.

  • Begin training and socialization as early as possible — the puppy window is critical
  • Use high-value rewards (real meat, cheese) to compete with distractions
  • Be consistent — the same rules must apply every session and every family member must agree
  • Hounds can be selectively deaf when a scent or sight captures their attention. Keep training sessions short and engaging, and always train in a secure area.
  • Crate training establishes boundaries, aids house training, and gives your dog a safe personal space
  • Short, positive sessions daily beat long, infrequent sessions every time

🏥 Health & Common Issues

Otterhounds are generally robust dogs but like all breeds they carry some genetic predispositions. Responsible breeders screen breeding stock for the most common conditions, significantly reducing risk in puppies from health-tested parents.

Feeding Your Otterhound

A large, moderately active hound, the Otterhound needs substantial nutrition suited to its size. Their deep chest makes bloat a real concern — two meals daily and rest after eating are important.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 18 mo)3–4 cups1,100–1,600 kcal
Adult (1.5–8 yrs)3–4.5 cups1,200–1,800 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)2.5–3.5 cups950–1,400 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • Large-breed formula with quality protein supports the Otterhound's working hound physique
  • Two meals daily — never one large meal for this bloat-susceptible deep-chested breed
  • Elevated feeders are sometimes recommended for large breeds to reduce bloat risk — consult your vet
  • Monitor weight carefully; the dense shaggy coat can mask gradual weight gain

Fun Facts About Otterhounds

  • 💧 The Otterhound's coat is one of the most technically specialized in the dog world — a rough, oily, double-layered construction that functions like a wetsuit, keeping the dog warm and dry during extended swims in cold British rivers. The outer coat's natural oils repel water so effectively that after emerging from a river, an Otterhound can shake off most of the water and be relatively dry within minutes. The coat's texture also traps an insulating layer of air next to the skin, providing thermal protection during winter hunting.
  • 🦦 The Otterhound has larger, more prominently webbed feet than virtually any other dog breed — a specific anatomical adaptation for powerful swimming. These webbed feet function as natural paddles, allowing the Otterhound to swim with remarkable efficiency and speed, following otter quarry through rivers and across pools. The combination of webbed feet, an oily waterproof coat, and a powerful, muscular body makes the Otterhound one of the most capable swimming breeds ever developed.
  • 📜 Otter hunting with Otterhounds was a distinctly English sport with a history stretching from at least the reign of King John (1199–1216) through the mid-20th century. The sport had its own traditions, vocabulary, and equipment — the otter spear (a long pole used to guide the hound across water), the distinctive peal of the hunting horn, and the specialized cry of "Tally Ho!" adapted for waterside use. Masters of otter hunts were respected figures in rural English communities, and the Otterhound packs they maintained were considered important aspects of country life.
  • ⚠️ The Otterhound's population has declined so severely since otter hunting's ban that it is considered more endangered as a breed than many wild animal species on international protection lists. Some years see fewer than 40 puppies registered globally — fewer than Giant Pandas born in captivity in the same period. UK breed clubs and the Kennel Club have designated the Otterhound a Vulnerable Native Breed, and international cooperation between breeders is considered essential to maintaining enough genetic diversity for the breed's long-term survival.
  • 😂 Otterhound owners consistently describe their dogs as comedians — blessed with a clownish, endearing personality that belies their formidable working heritage. These are genuinely funny dogs that use their size and exuberance to express joy in ways that are simultaneously awe-inspiring and destructive. An Otterhound that has found a scent worth following will communicate its excitement through elaborate vocalizations — the breed's voice is one of the most musical and varied of any hound, ranging from deep baying to melodious singing that owners find irresistible even when directed at 2 AM squirrels.
