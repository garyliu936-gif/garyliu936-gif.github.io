Breed Overview

The Otterhound is one of England's most ancient and remarkable hound breeds — and today, one of the rarest dog breeds in the world, with fewer than 1,000 individuals estimated globally. Bred specifically to hunt otter in the rivers, streams, and marshes of medieval England, the Otterhound is a large, shaggy, deep-voiced scent hound purpose-built for aquatic work. King John of England kept packs of Otterhounds as early as 1212, and the breed was a fixture of royal and aristocratic hunts for the next 700 years — until otter hunting was banned in England in 1978, leaving the breed without a purpose and at serious risk of extinction.

The Otterhound's physical adaptations for water work are extraordinary: massive, broad, deeply webbed feet that function like paddles; a double coat of rough, oily outer hair over a dense woolly undercoat that repels water and insulates in cold rivers; and a booming, resonant voice capable of carrying across wide water. Their nose, considered among the best of all hound breeds, can follow an otter's underwater scent trail hours after the animal has passed — tracking the greasy, distinctive otter scent along riverbanks and through swirling currents. As companions they are affectionate, clownish, and enthusiastic — enormous dogs with enormous personalities who love water, mud, and food in equal measure.

Health Notes

  • Glanzmann's thrombasthenia (bleeding disorder — genetic test available)
  • Hip and elbow dysplasia
  • Bloat risk — deep chest requires careful feeding management
  • Ear infections — long, heavy ears need regular cleaning

Feeding Your Otterhound

A large, deep-chested hound, the Otterhound needs proper nutrition and careful feeding management to minimize bloat risk. Never feed one large daily meal.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 18 mo)3–4 cups1,000–1,600 kcal
Adult (1.5–8 yrs)3–4.5 cups1,200–1,800 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)2.5–3.5 cups900–1,400 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • Large-breed formula helps manage growth rate and support large joints
  • Always feed two meals minimum — never one large meal for this deep-chested breed
  • Avoid vigorous exercise for 60–90 minutes before and after meals to reduce bloat risk
  • Omega fatty acids support the rough, oily water-repellent coat

Fun Facts About Otterhounds

  • 🦦 The Otterhound is now considered rarer than the Giant Panda — with fewer than 1,000 individuals worldwide, they are listed on the UK's Vulnerable Native Breeds list and are among the most critically endangered dog breeds on Earth. In the UK, where the breed originated, fewer than 50 puppies are born each year — a population so small that breed survival requires active international cooperation between dedicated breeders.
  • 🏰 The Otterhound has been used for otter hunting in England since at least the 12th century — King John of England is recorded as maintaining a pack in 1212, and subsequent monarchs including Edward II, Edward IV, and Henry II also kept royal Otterhound packs. Otter hunting with Otterhounds was a royal and aristocratic sport enjoyed continuously for nearly 700 years before being banned.
  • 🏊 The Otterhound's webbed feet are among the most pronounced of any breed — the thick webs between each toe extend nearly to the tip of the toes, creating a genuine paddle structure that makes them powerful, natural swimmers who can sustain themselves in cold river water far longer than most breeds. Combine this with their waterproof, insulating double coat, and the Otterhound is physiologically closer to an aquatic animal than any other hound breed.
  • 👃 The Otterhound's scenting ability was rated second only to the Bloodhound by 19th-century scent trials — able to follow a cold, hours-old trail through water and across terrain that defeats other hounds. Their unusual ability to track through water (following the greasy otter scent that floats on the surface after the animal swims) was the specific adaptation that made them irreplaceable for their original purpose.
  • 🎭 Otterhounds are celebrated among their owners for an almost theatrical personality — they are clownish, boisterous, and enthusiastic about everything with an infectious joy that belies their dignified ancient history. They are known for greeting familiar people with their entire rear end wagging, leaning their full considerable weight affectionately against legs, and producing a repertoire of vocalizations ranging from deep bell-like baying to peculiar mumbled "talking" sounds unique to the breed.
