Breed Overview

The Plott Hound is the official state dog of North Carolina and the only AKC coonhound breed that does not trace its ancestry to English Foxhounds. Instead, Plotts descend from German Hanoverian Schweisshunds (hunting dogs) brought to North Carolina by Johannes Plott in 1750. For over 200 years, the Plott family bred these dogs in the Blue Ridge Mountains, using them to hunt bears, boars, and mountain lions through rugged terrain.

Plotts are known for their exceptional courage — they will engage prey many times their size without hesitation. With their characteristic brindle coat (one of the few hounds with this coloring) and deep, chilling bay, they are formidable hunters. At home, Plotts are affectionate, loyal, and gentle with family — a true dual-purpose dog who can go from wilderness to living room.

Feeding Your Plott Hound

Athletic and muscular, Plott Hounds need a quality diet to fuel their energetic lifestyle. Feed measured portions twice daily to reduce bloat risk.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 18 mo)2–3 cups800–1,200 kcal
Adult — active (1–7 yrs)2.5–3.5 cups1,000–1,400 kcal
Senior (7+ yrs)2–2.5 cups800–1,000 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • High-protein diet supports hunting endurance
  • Never feed immediately before or after vigorous exercise — bloat risk
  • Omega-3 fatty acids support ear health
  • Joint supplements from middle age for heavily worked dogs

Fun Facts About Plott Hounds

  • 🐗 Plott Hounds were historically used to hunt wild boar, black bear, and mountain lion in the Appalachian Mountains — some of the most dangerous quarry any hunting dog faces.
  • 🇩🇪 Unlike every other AKC coonhound, the Plott descends from German hunting stock, not English — brought to America by a German immigrant in 1750.
  • 🏛️ The Plott Hound has been the official state dog of North Carolina since 1989, honoring the breed's deep roots in the state's mountain communities.
  • 👨‍👩‍👦 The Plott family kept their breeding stock closely guarded for nearly 200 years — only introducing an outcross of the Blevins hound once before reverting to their original line.
  • 🎨 The distinctive brindle coat (striped like a tiger's pattern) comes from the German heritage — most American hound breeds are tricolor or solid, not brindle.
