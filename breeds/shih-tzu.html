🎬 Shih Tzu Facts

🐾 Overview

The Shih Tzu (pronounced "sheed-zoo" or "sheet-sue" — both are accepted) is one of the oldest and most purely companion-focused dog breeds in the world. Their name means "lion dog" in Chinese, a nod to their resemblance to Chinese guardian lions. For centuries, they were bred exclusively for Chinese royalty and were never sold, traded, or gifted — ownership was restricted to the imperial palace.

Today's Shih Tzu carries that heritage in their personality — they are confident, charming, and aware of their own importance. They are small in body but large in personality, and they genuinely believe they belong on every piece of furniture and in every lap. Their long, flowing coat and pushed-in face make them one of the most recognizable toy breeds.

😊 Temperament & Personality

Shih Tzus are happy, outgoing, and friendly little dogs whose entire reason for being is human companionship. They love everyone — adults, children, strangers, other dogs — and rarely show aggression. They were bred to be companion dogs, and they excel at exactly that. They thrive on attention and do not enjoy being ignored.

Unlike many toy breeds, Shih Tzus are not typically yappy or nervous. They are confident and sturdy despite their small size. They are playful but not overly energetic, making them ideal companions for apartment dwellers, older adults, and families alike. Their adaptable nature means they fit well into almost any household.

  • Outgoing and friendly with everyone — no aggression
  • Excellent with children and other pets
  • Adaptable — suits apartments, houses, urban and suburban environments
  • Confident and resilient — not a nervous or anxious toy breed
  • Loves attention and being the center of family life
  • Can develop separation anxiety if left alone frequently

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

Shih Tzus need modest daily exercise — a couple of short walks and some indoor playtime is typically plenty. Their flat faces mean they can overheat in hot weather and struggle with intense exercise. They are happy to lounge on the sofa for most of the day, but a little daily activity keeps them healthy and prevents obesity.

  • Daily exercise: 20–30 minutes of moderate activity
  • Two short walks daily is ideal for most adults
  • Avoid exercise in hot or humid weather — brachycephalic breathing issues
  • Indoor playtime counts toward their exercise needs
  • Obesity is a significant risk — monitor food intake carefully
  • Puppies: short play sessions several times a day

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

The Shih Tzu's long, flowing coat is beautiful but high-maintenance. Without daily brushing, it mats quickly. Most Shih Tzu owners opt for a "puppy cut" (trimmed short all over) for easier maintenance. Show coats require daily intensive grooming and are typically reserved for show dogs or very dedicated owners.

  • Full coat: brush daily to prevent mats — use a slicker brush and metal comb
  • Puppy cut (trimmed short): brush every few days; professional grooming every 6–8 weeks
  • Clean the face daily — tear staining and debris collect around the eyes
  • Clean facial skin folds around the nose daily
  • Bathe every 3–4 weeks
  • Dental care is critical — small breeds are very prone to dental disease

🎓 Training

Shih Tzus are intelligent and can learn a wide range of commands, but they are also famously stubborn. They were bred as companions, not working dogs, which means they may question why exactly they need to do something. Short, positive, reward-based sessions work best. Harsh training methods cause them to shut down entirely.

  • Food rewards work very well — high motivation for treats
  • Keep sessions short (5–10 min) and end with success
  • Housetraining can be challenging — be patient and consistent
  • Crate training helps significantly with housetraining
  • Socialize from an early age to prevent possessiveness
  • Avoid reinforcing "small dog syndrome" — same rules apply regardless of size

🏥 Health & Common Issues

Shih Tzus can live remarkably long lives — 10–18 years is typical. Their main health concerns relate to their flat face and large prominent eyes. The eyes are particularly vulnerable to injury and conditions like dry eye. Regular vet check-ups and proactive care help Shih Tzus live long, healthy lives.

🏠 Is a Shih Tzu Right for You?

A Shih Tzu is an excellent choice for apartment dwellers, seniors, families with gentle children, and anyone who wants a loving, low-exercise companion. They need regular grooming investment. They're not suited for very active families wanting a jogging partner, or owners who are away from home for long hours regularly.

✅ Best Foods for Shih Tzus

Look for foods where the first ingredient is a named animal protein. The best diets for this breed also address their specific health tendencies:

  • Small-breed formula with small kibble pieces — Shih Tzus have tiny mouths and flat faces that make large kibble hard to eat
  • High-quality protein (chicken or fish) as the first ingredient to support their flowing coat
  • Omega-3 fatty acids (fish oil or flaxseed) — the Shih Tzu's long, luxurious coat requires excellent nutritional support from the inside out
  • Avoid foods with artificial dyes — the Shih Tzu's white facial hair is very susceptible to staining from dyes and low-quality ingredients
  • Dental-health focused treats and foods — Shih Tzus are prone to dental disease due to their small mouths and crowded teeth
  • Avoid: artificial colors, BHA/BHT preservatives, and corn or soy as the primary ingredient

🚫 Foods Dangerous for Dogs

These common human foods can be toxic — even life-threatening — for your Shih Tzu. Keep them well out of reach.

Chocolate Grapes & Raisins Onions & Garlic Xylitol (artificial sweetener) Macadamia Nuts Alcohol Avocado Raw yeast dough

🦴 Healthy Treats

  • Carrots — low calorie and great for teeth
  • Blueberries — antioxidants, dogs love them
  • Plain cooked chicken or turkey (no seasoning)
  • Apple slices (remove seeds and core)
  • Green beans — filling and very low calorie
  • Commercial treats sized for your dog's weight class

💡 Coat health starts with diet

A Shih Tzu's coat is their crowning glory — and it's directly influenced by what they eat. If the coat looks dull or the skin is flaky, try adding a fish oil supplement and switching to a higher-quality protein source. You'll often see improvement within 6–8 weeks.

📊 Lifetime Cost Estimate

Over their full lifespan, a Shih Tzu typically costs $14,000 – $28,000 total — depending on health, lifestyle, and the services you use.

  • Grooming is the largest ongoing expense — Shih Tzus in a full coat require professional grooming every 4–6 weeks. Many owners opt for a shorter "puppy cut" to reduce both grooming time and cost
  • Dental care costs are higher than average — Shih Tzus are extremely prone to dental disease and often need professional cleanings under anesthesia annually
  • Shih Tzus have a long lifespan (10–18 years), which means lifetime costs can be significant despite their small size
  • Pet insurance is worth it — it pays for itself if your dog ever needs surgery
  • Boarding costs: plan $50–$100/night at quality facilities when you travel

💡 Money-saving tip

Learning to do basic Shih Tzu grooming at home — brushing daily and giving baths every 2–3 weeks — can save $600–$1,200/year in professional grooming costs. A quality slicker brush and dog-safe conditioner are your best investments.

💡 How to Save Money as a Shih Tzu Owner

  • Get pet insurance before your dog turns 1 — lower premiums and fewer exclusions
  • Buy food in bulk (large bags) — significantly cheaper per pound
  • Learn basic grooming at home — brushing and nail trims add up fast at a groomer
  • Ask your vet about annual wellness plans — many clinics offer bundled packages
  • Adopt instead of buying — rescue Shih Tzus are just as loving at a fraction of the cost

🎉 Amazing Facts About Shih Tzus

One of the world's oldest and most pampered breeds — the Shih Tzu has a history fit for royalty.

  • 👑 The Shih Tzu is one of the oldest dog breeds in the world — DNA analysis places them among the 14 most ancient breeds, with roots going back at least 2,000 years in China and Tibet.
  • 🏯 Shih Tzus were sacred dogs of Chinese royalty — kept exclusively in the Imperial Palace and treated as living treasures. Commoners were forbidden from owning them under penalty of death.
  • 🌸 The name "Shih Tzu" means "Lion Dog" in Chinese — they were bred to resemble the lion, a sacred animal in Buddhist iconography. The long hair parted on the face was meant to mimic a lion's mane.
  • 🌍 When the last Chinese Emperor was deposed in 1908, Shih Tzus were nearly lost forever — the imperial breeding program collapsed. The entire modern breed descends from just 14 dogs saved by British and Scandinavian breeders.
  • ✈️ The Shih Tzu wasn't seen outside of China until the 1930s, when a few dogs were brought to England. They weren't recognized by the AKC until 1969 — making them relative newcomers to Western dog culture.
  • 🐾 Despite their pampered reputation, Shih Tzus are sturdy, athletic little dogs with surprising endurance. They were originally used as watchdogs in Tibetan monasteries — a job requiring alertness, not lounging.
  • 🌡️ Like all flat-faced breeds, Shih Tzus are heat-sensitive — they overheat quickly in warm weather. Air conditioning, cool water, and shade are essential for summer safety.
  • 🏆 The Shih Tzu is currently one of the top 20 most popular dog breeds in the United States — despite being virtually unknown outside China just 90 years ago, a meteoric rise in popularity.
  • 🧸 Shih Tzus are one of the most popular breeds for emotional support animal certification — their calm, intuitive nature and genuine love of human contact make them natural comfort dogs.
