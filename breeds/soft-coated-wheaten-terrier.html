Breed Overview

The Soft Coated Wheaten Terrier is one of Ireland's oldest native breeds, developed as an all-purpose farm dog for hunting, herding, and guarding. Their most distinctive feature is their single-layer, silky, gently waving coat in warm wheat tones — a coat that, unlike most dogs, doesn't shed much and is considered allergy-friendly. Wheaten puppies are born dark and gradually lighten to their signature wheaten color by age two.

Wheatens are famously exuberant greeters — leaping enthusiastically to say hello to everyone (a habit called the "Wheaten greeting"). They combine terrier independence and mischief with an unusual degree of gentleness and affection, making them beloved family dogs worldwide.

Temperament

Wheatens are spirited, affectionate, and playful — they retain a puppy-like enthusiasm well into adulthood. They're devoted to family, generally good with children, and sociable with strangers. However, their terrier DNA means they can be stubborn, have a strong prey drive toward small animals, and may be dog-selective. They do not like being left alone for long periods and can develop separation anxiety.

Exercise Needs

Moderate-to-high energy — 30–60 minutes of vigorous daily exercise keeps them happy. They enjoy hiking, fetch, and agility. Their prey drive means leash manners and recall training are essential. A securely fenced yard is recommended.

✈️ Travel Tip: At 30–45 lbs, Wheatens typically travel as checked baggage on commercial flights. Their low-shedding, allergy-friendly coat means fewer concerns about dander in confined spaces. Their friendly, adaptable temperament makes them relatively good travel companions once crate-trained.

Grooming

The Wheaten's silky single coat requires brushing 3–4 times per week to prevent mats, especially behind the ears and on the legs. Professional grooming every 6–8 weeks maintains the coat shape. Despite being low-shedding, the coat picks up dirt and debris readily. Two coat types exist: the American coat (fuller, thicker) and the Irish coat (thinner, silkier) — both require similar maintenance.

Training

Wheatens are intelligent but classically terrier-stubborn. They learn quickly but decide independently when to apply that knowledge. Positive reinforcement with high-value treats works best. Consistency is key — Wheatens will find and exploit any inconsistency in rules. Early socialization prevents the prey drive from becoming problematic.

Health Considerations

Protein-Losing Nephropathy (PLN) Protein-Losing Enteropathy (PLE) Addison's Disease Hip Dysplasia Food Allergies

PLN and PLE — kidney and intestinal protein-losing disorders — are the most serious breed-specific health concerns. Reputable breeders screen their lines. Regular urine protein testing from age 2–3 years is recommended. Food sensitivities are also common — many Wheatens thrive on limited-ingredient diets.

Feeding Your Wheaten Terrier

Wheatens typically eat 1.5–2 cups of high-quality dry kibble daily, split into two meals. Food sensitivities are common — many do best on limited-ingredient or novel protein diets.

Life StageDaily AmountMeals/Day
Puppy (2–6 mo)1–1.5 cups3
Adult (1–8 yr)1.5–2 cups2
Senior (9+ yr)1–1.5 cups2

Omega-3 fatty acids support kidney health — especially important given PLN/PLE risk. Avoid high-sodium diets if kidney concerns arise. Consult your vet about protein levels appropriate for your individual dog.

Popular Wheaten Terrier Mixes

Whoodle (Wheaten + Poodle)

A low-shedding, highly intelligent mix combining the Wheaten's fun personality with Poodle brains. One of the most popular Wheaten mixes — cheerful, trainable, and allergy-friendly.

Wheaten-Labrador Mix

The Lab's trainability meets the Wheaten's silky coat and spirited energy. A friendly, medium-sized family dog that's often lower-shedding than a purebred Lab.

Wheaten-Cocker Spaniel Mix

A gentle, affectionate mix with beautiful wavy fur. Inherits the Wheaten's low-shedding qualities alongside the Cocker's sweet, loyal disposition.

Fun Facts About Wheaten Terriers

  • 🌾 All-Season Coat: The Wheaten's single-layer coat doesn't shed like a double coat — instead, loose hairs are trapped and tangled in, which is why brushing is so important but shedding is so minimal.
  • 🐕 The Wheaten Greeting: The characteristic leap-and-lick hello Wheatens give to everyone they meet is so famous it has its own name — the "Wheaten greeting." Training "four on the floor" behavior takes consistent effort.
  • 🇮🇪 Ancient Irish Roots: Wheatens were the dogs of Irish tenant farmers for centuries — too poor to afford purebred hunting hounds, farmers bred these versatile dogs who could hunt, herd, guard, and kill vermin equally well.
  • 🎨 Born Dark: Wheaten puppies are born with dark brown or black coats that gradually lighten over their first two years to the adult wheat color — confusing to new owners who wonder if they got the right breed.
  • 🌀 Coat Varieties: There are two officially recognized coat varieties — the Irish coat (thinner, silkier, waves loosely) and the American coat (fuller, thicker waves). Both are equally valid but have slightly different grooming needs.
