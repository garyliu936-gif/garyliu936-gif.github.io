Overview

The Norfolk Terrier is one of the smallest working terriers, bred in England to hunt vermin and work in packs. Distinguished from the Norwich Terrier by its drop ears, the Norfolk is equally spirited but slightly more sociable.

Norfolks are energetic, affectionate, and surprisingly adaptable. They get along well with other dogs and children, making them excellent family companions. Their wiry coat requires regular hand-stripping to maintain texture.

Did You Know?

  • Separated from the Norwich Terrier as a distinct breed in 1964
  • One of the smallest AKC-recognized terrier breeds
  • Hunted in packs unlike most terriers
  • Distinguished by folded ears vs. Norwich's prick ears

Common Health Issues

  • Patellar luxation
  • Mitral valve disease
  • Upper airway syndrome
  • Hip dysplasia (rare)

Is a Norfolk Terrier Right for You?

If you want a small dog with big terrier spirit who still plays well with others, the Norfolk Terrier is a wonderful choice.

Norfolk Terrier Diet & Nutrition Guide

🐶 Puppy (0–12 months)

Approach: 3–4 small meals daily of small-breed puppy food

Calories: ¼–½ cup per day total

🐕 Adult (1–7 years)

Approach: 2 meals daily of quality small-breed kibble

Calories: ½ cup per day

🦮 Senior (7+ years)

Approach: 2 meals daily of senior small-breed formula

Calories: ¼–⅓ cup per day

Key Nutritional Priorities

  • High protein for an active small terrier
  • Omega fatty acids for wiry coat health
  • Joint support supplements
  • Fiber for digestive health
