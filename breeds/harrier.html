Breed Overview

The Harrier is one of the oldest English scent hounds — a pack hare-hunting dog developed in Britain before the Foxhound, with records of Harrier packs dating to the 13th century. They occupy the size gap between the Beagle and the English Foxhound: bigger and faster than a Beagle, smaller and more suited to hare hunting than the larger Foxhound. Their name comes from the Old English word for hare, reflecting their centuries-long association with that quarry.

The Harrier is essentially a slightly larger, sturdier version of the Beagle — sharing the tricolor coat pattern, the warm brown eyes, and the broad, friendly expression that makes English pack hounds so appealing. But the Harrier has more speed, more bone, and more of the serious working dog underneath the friendly surface. In the US, the Harrier is extraordinarily rare — one of the least-registered AKC breeds — despite being a friendly, versatile, and excellent family dog. They are outgoing, playful, and robust enough for active families that want a medium-sized dog with real hound character.

Feeding Your Harrier

A medium-sized, high-energy pack hound, the Harrier needs quality nutrition to fuel active days. They have a typical hound appetite and will overeat if given the chance.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 12 mo)1.5–2.5 cups650–1,100 kcal
Adult (1–8 yrs)2–2.5 cups800–1,100 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)1.5–2 cups650–900 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • High-quality protein supports their lean, athletic build
  • Avoid free-feeding — hounds will eat until they can't move
  • Omega-3 fatty acids support joint health for these active dogs
  • Maintain healthy weight — excess weight is hard on hound joints over long hunts

An economical breed to maintain — their short coat needs almost no professional grooming, and they are generally very healthy with a long lifespan. Finding one is the challenge: Harriers are among the rarest AKC breeds, with fewer than 100 AKC registrations in most years. Expect a waiting list from the small number of dedicated breeders.

Fun Facts About Harriers

  • 📜 The Harrier may be the oldest identifiable hound breed in England — records of organized Harrier packs date to 1260, with the Penistone Pack in England documented continuously since that year. This makes the Harrier older as an organized breed than the Foxhound (which emerged in the 1700s) or the Beagle in its modern form.
  • 🐇 Harriers were specifically bred to hunt hare on foot — meaning the hunt was conducted by people walking or running behind the pack rather than on horseback. This required a hound that worked at a moderate but sustained pace, close enough for followers on foot to keep up through difficult terrain.
  • 🤔 Despite their ancient history and excellent temperament, Harriers are among the AKC's least-registered breeds. In most years, fewer than 100 Harriers are registered in the United States — making them rarer than many exotic breeds most people have never heard of, despite being friendly, adaptable family dogs.
  • 🧬 The Harrier, Beagle, and English Foxhound share very close genetic ancestry — centuries of crossing these three breeds to refine hunting characteristics means the genetic boundary between them is blurry. Modern Harriers were essentially defined by size: between 19–21 inches at the shoulder, deliberately occupying the gap between Beagle and Foxhound.
  • 🌍 George Washington kept a pack of Harriers at Mount Vernon, acquired from England, which he used for hare hunting — further evidence that the breed's history intertwines with early American colonial life alongside the better-known American and English Foxhounds he is famous for breeding.
