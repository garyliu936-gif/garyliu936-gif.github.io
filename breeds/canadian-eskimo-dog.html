🐾 Overview

The Canadian Eskimo Dog (also known as Qimmiq or Canadian Inuit Dog) is one of the oldest and rarest dog breeds in North America. Used by Inuit people for millennia to haul sleds, hunt, and survive in extreme Arctic conditions, this powerful spitz-type breed nearly went extinct in the 20th century. Conservation efforts have helped stabilize the population. It is an official symbol of Nunavut, Canada.

😊 Temperament & Personality

Brave, loyal, and energetic. The Canadian Eskimo Dog is affectionate with its pack but can be dominant with other dogs. Highly independent and alert. Best suited for experienced, active owners.

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

Very high energy: needs 60–90+ minutes of vigorous exercise daily. Thrives in cold climates with space to run. Not suited for warm climates or apartment living.

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

Thick double coat. Brush 2–3 times per week; heavy shedding in spring. Bathe occasionally. Well-adapted to cold; avoid overheating.

🎓 Training

Intelligent but independent and strong-willed. Requires experienced handlers, firm leadership, and early socialization. Positive reinforcement works best.

🏥 Health & Common Issues

Generally hardy. Hip dysplasia monitoring recommended. Gastric bloat possible. Keep cool in warm weather as the breed is not heat-tolerant.

🎉 Fun Facts About Canadian Eskimo Dog

  • Also called Qimmiq, the Inuktitut word for "dog."
  • Used by Inuit people for over 4,000 years for transportation and hunting.
  • Declared an official symbol of Nunavut, Canada's newest territory.
  • Population plummeted to fewer than 200 in the 1970s; conservation brought it back.
  • Considered one of the rarest purebred dogs in the world.
