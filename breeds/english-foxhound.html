Breed Overview

The English Foxhound is the original foxhound — a breed developed in England in the 16th and 17th centuries specifically for mounted fox hunting, where packs of hounds would pursue a fox across miles of countryside while riders on horseback followed. No breed in history has been more meticulously bred by a specific social class for a specific purpose: the English aristocracy maintained detailed stud books for Foxhound packs long before other breeds had any formal record-keeping at all. The Foxhound Kennel Stud Book, started in 1800, is one of the oldest canine registries in the world.

The English Foxhound is a study in athletic perfection — hard muscle, strong bone, excellent feet for covering ground at speed, and a coat of hard, close fur that sheds mud and water efficiently. They are sociable, pack-oriented dogs that thrive in the company of other dogs and are generally friendly with people, including children. However, they were bred to run 20–30 miles a day and are extraordinarily difficult to satisfy as house pets without serious daily exercise and stimulation. They remain one of the rarest AKC breeds in the United States.

  • Renal disease (familial nephropathy)
  • Epilepsy in some lines
  • Generally very hardy — bred for function, not appearance

Feeding Your English Foxhound

One of the highest-energy breeds that exists, the English Foxhound needs substantial, quality nutrition. A working hunt-season Foxhound may burn 2,000+ calories per day.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 18 mo)2.5–4 cups1,000–1,600 kcal
Adult – active (1–7 yrs)3–5 cups1,200–2,000 kcal
Senior (7+ yrs)2–3 cups900–1,300 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • High-protein, high-fat formula for active working dogs
  • Feed twice daily — never once — to reduce bloat risk in this deep-chested breed
  • Rest 1 hour before and after meals to prevent GDV
  • Reduce portions significantly in non-hunting/low-exercise periods to prevent obesity

Fun Facts About English Foxhounds

  • 📚 The Foxhound Kennel Stud Book — started by the Masters of Foxhounds Association in England in 1800 — is one of the oldest formal canine registries in the world, predating the AKC by 84 years and the Kennel Club by 73 years. English foxhound bloodlines have been meticulously documented for over 225 years.
  • 🏛️ George Washington was an avid Foxhound breeder and maintained a pack at Mount Vernon — he crossed his English Foxhounds with French hounds gifted by the Marquis de Lafayette to help create the American Foxhound. His daily diary entries frequently mention his hound pack, including named individuals.
  • 🏃 At the height of the English foxhunting tradition, a pack of Foxhounds would run 20–30 miles during a single day's hunt across open countryside — a distance that would exhaust most human athletes. The breed's entire physique was shaped by this extraordinary aerobic demand over hundreds of generations.
  • 🎨 The English Foxhound is arguably the most painted dog in the history of Western art — the aristocratic fox-hunting tradition spawned a rich genre of equestrian and hunting paintings in the 17th–19th centuries in which Foxhounds are ubiquitous. Artists like George Stubbs made careers depicting them.
  • 🦊 Despite being bred exclusively to hunt foxes for centuries, English Foxhounds are extraordinarily friendly and non-aggressive with the foxes themselves when not hunting — a paradox that baffles owners who expect aggression and instead find a calm, social dog. The hunting response is triggered by scent in context, not by appearance.
