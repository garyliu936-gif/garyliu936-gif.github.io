🐾 Overview

The Xoloitzcuintli (pronounced "show-low-eetz-QUEENT-lee") — often called the "Xolo" or "Mexican Hairless" — is one of the oldest and rarest dog breeds in the world, with a documented history spanning at least 3,500 years in Mesoamerica. Archaeological evidence of hairless dogs has been found at Aztec, Toltec, Colima, and Maya sites throughout Mexico and Central America, with clay figurines and burial artifacts depicting them as spiritual companions and guides to the underworld. The Aztec god Xolotl — deity of lightning and death — was said to have created the dog to guide human souls through the underworld, giving the breed its name: Xolo (Xolotl) + itzcuintli (dog in Nahuatl).

The Xoloitzcuintli comes in three sizes — Toy (10–14 lbs), Miniature (15–30 lbs), and Standard (30–55 lbs) — and in two coat varieties: hairless (the defining type) and coated (a short, dense coat). Hairless Xolos have smooth, tough skin that can range from dark charcoal and slate to liver, bronze, and spotted. Their body heat radiates warmly through bare skin — a trait that made them prized as living heating pads by Aztec people suffering from joint pain. They are calm, loyal, and deeply bonded to their family.

😊 Temperament & Personality

Xoloitzcuintlis are known for being energetic and loyal. They form deep bonds with their families and thrive on consistent human connection. They strike a good balance — lively enough to enjoy playtime and adventures, but calm enough to settle indoors when the day is done.

Training is generally a pleasure with Xoloitzcuintlis — they are responsive, motivated, and pick up commands quickly. They are typically gentle and patient with children, making them a reliable family companion.

  • Moderately active — enjoys exercise and playtime but appreciates downtime at home
  • Affectionate and people-oriented — thrives on closeness with their family
  • Patient and gentle with children — a dependable family companion
  • Intelligent and eager to learn — responds enthusiastically to positive training methods
  • Breed-typical personality is reliable and predictable — makes planning your lifestyle together easier
  • Genuinely enjoys relaxing — equally happy on a couch as on a trail with the right owner

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

Xoloitzcuintlis need regular daily exercise to maintain a healthy weight and an even temperament. They are versatile companions who enjoy active outings but are equally content to relax at home after their needs are met.

  • Daily exercise: 45 – 60 minutes of moderate activity
  • Vigorous daily exercise — swimming, fetch, trail hiking, or dog sports — plus mental enrichment
  • Daily walks, play sessions, and occasional trips to a dog park are ideal
  • Mental enrichment (puzzle toys, training) complements physical exercise
  • Adjust intensity based on age — puppies and seniors need gentler, shorter sessions
  • Indoor play can substitute on days with extreme weather

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

Xoloitzcuintlis have a short, low-maintenance coat that requires minimal grooming compared to longer-haired breeds. A consistent basic routine keeps them clean, comfortable, and healthy.

  • Brush once a week with a soft bristle brush or rubber grooming mitt
  • Bathe every 6–8 weeks, or when dirty — overbathing strips natural coat oils
  • Clean ears weekly and check for redness or odor that may indicate infection
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks — they often don't wear down naturally indoors
  • Wipe down with a damp cloth between baths to keep the coat gleaming
  • Feathered coats need brushing 2–3 times weekly. Pay special attention to the ears — floppy ears trap moisture and are prone to infections.

🎓 Training

Xoloitzcuintlis are highly responsive to training and genuinely enjoy learning. Their combination of intelligence and eagerness to please makes them a joy to work with — they pick up new commands quickly and retain them well.

Keep training sessions varied and engaging to prevent boredom. Xoloitzcuintlis can handle advanced training — agility, nose work, therapy work, or competitive obedience are all excellent options for channeling their intelligence.

  • Begin training and socialization as early as possible — the puppy window is critical
  • Reward-based training (treats, praise, play) produces the best and fastest results
  • Be consistent — the same rules must apply every session and every family member must agree
  • Sporting dogs are among the most trainable breeds. They respond enthusiastically to positive reinforcement and food rewards. Begin obedience training early.
  • Crate training establishes boundaries, aids house training, and gives your dog a safe personal space
  • Short, positive sessions daily beat long, infrequent sessions every time

🏠 Is a Xoloitzcuintli Right for You?

A Xoloitzcuintli fits well with families or individuals who can provide moderate daily exercise and consistent companionship. They adapt reasonably well to various living situations as long as their exercise and social needs are met. Not ideal for owners away from home for long hours without a pet care solution in place.

Feeding Your Xoloitzcuintli

Feed according to size variety — Toy Xolos have very different caloric needs than Standard. All benefit from skin-supporting nutrition given their unique hairless coat.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 12 mo)½–2 cups (by size)250–900 kcal
Adult (1–8 yrs)½–2 cups (by size)300–1,000 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)⅓–1.5 cups (by size)250–750 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • Omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids are critical for maintaining healthy, hydrated skin in the hairless variety
  • Vitamin E supports skin barrier — look for it in quality pet foods
  • Avoid overfeeding any size variety — their lean build makes excess weight very apparent
  • Dental health matters — missing teeth (linked to hairless gene) affect chewing; choose appropriate food texture

Fun Facts About Xoloitzcuintlis

  • 🏛️ The Xoloitzcuintli is one of the oldest dog breeds in the Americas — clay figurines of hairless dogs found at Colima burial sites in Mexico date to 300 BC, and DNA analysis suggests the hairless mutation in the breed's ancestors arrived in the Americas alongside the first human migrations from Asia over 15,000 years ago.
  • 🌡️ The Aztec people believed the Xolo's warm, bare skin had healing properties — the dogs were pressed against painful joints, the abdomen during illness, and aching limbs to transfer their body heat. While purely folk medicine, Xolos do radiate notably warm body heat through their bare skin, which modern owners still find comforting on cold nights.
  • 🎨 Frida Kahlo was famously devoted to Xoloitzcuintlis — she kept several at her home, Casa Azul, in Coyoacán, Mexico, and depicted them in at least two of her paintings. Kahlo and her husband Diego Rivera helped bring the breed back from near-extinction in Mexico in the 1950s by championing its recognition as a national heritage breed.
  • 🦷 The genetic mutation that causes hairlessness in the Xolo is linked to missing premolar teeth — most hairless Xolos are born with only partial dentition. This is not a defect but a genetic consequence of the hairless gene itself, and breed standards acknowledge and accept it.
  • 🎬 The Xoloitzcuintli gained significant pop-culture visibility in the 2017 Pixar film "Coco" — the spirit guide dog Dante is a Xolo, bringing worldwide attention to this ancient Mexican breed and sparking a notable increase in interest and registrations in the years following the film's release.
