Breed Overview

The West Highland White Terrier — affectionately known as the "Westie" — hails from Scotland, where they were bred to hunt foxes, otters, and vermin in rocky Highland terrain. Their signature brilliant white coat (the result of deliberate breeding from the 1800s) makes them instantly recognizable. Don't let their cute, fluffy appearance fool you — Westies are true terriers: independent, feisty, and full of character.

Westies have become one of the UK's most iconic dogs, famously associated with the Cesar dog food brand. They're popular worldwide for their manageable size, low-shedding coat, and spirited personality. They make great companions for active individuals and families willing to engage their clever minds.

Temperament

Westies are confident, curious, and entertaining. They love to be the center of attention and have a self-assured, almost cocky personality — typical of terriers. They're loyal to their families but can be reserved with strangers at first. Westies can coexist with children who understand how to handle dogs respectfully, but they may not tolerate rough handling. They have a strong prey drive and may chase smaller animals.

Exercise Needs

Westies need moderate daily exercise — around 30–45 minutes of walking or active play per day. They enjoy exploring and will happily join hiking adventures. Off-leash time should be in a securely fenced area as their prey drive can send them chasing squirrels or other small animals with total disregard for traffic or distance.

✈️ Travel Tip: Westies typically weigh 15–22 lbs — at the upper limit for in-cabin airline travel (most carriers allow pets under 20 lbs in-cabin). Consult your airline in advance. Their calm, self-assured temperament makes them reasonably good travelers, though some can be vocal when confined. A well-acclimated carrier introduced well before travel helps significantly.

Grooming

The Westie's double coat — a soft undercoat and a harsh, wiry outer coat — requires regular maintenance. Show dogs are "hand stripped" (dead hair pulled out by hand to maintain coat texture), but pet owners typically opt for professional clipping every 6–8 weeks. Brushing 2–3 times per week prevents mats. The white coat can stain — paw-licking or tear staining may require spot cleaning with specialized whitening shampoos. Ears should be checked weekly.

Training

Westies are intelligent but famously independent — a classic terrier combination. They can be trained but may decide to do things their own way. Positive reinforcement with high-value treats works best. Training sessions should be short, fun, and varied to keep their interest. House-training can take some patience. Early socialization is important to prevent territorial behavior. Recall training is essential given their prey drive.

Health Considerations

Westies are generally hardy dogs but have some breed-specific health tendencies. Working with a reputable breeder who screens for hereditary conditions is important.

Westie Lung Disease (Pulmonary Fibrosis) Skin Allergies (Atopic Dermatitis) Craniomandibular Osteopathy Patellar Luxation White Shaker Syndrome

Skin allergies are particularly common in Westies, causing itching, redness, and recurring skin infections. Food trials, environmental allergen management, and veterinary dermatology consults are often needed. Pulmonary fibrosis (also called "Westie Lung Disease") is a serious progressive lung condition that appears in older dogs — be aware of signs like persistent coughing or exercise intolerance.

Feeding Your Westie

Westies are small dogs with compact builds. They typically eat ¾–1 cup of high-quality dry kibble per day, divided into two meals. Because skin allergies are common, diet quality matters — a high-quality protein-based diet can help manage skin conditions.

Nutrition Tips

  • Choose formulas with a single, high-quality protein source to help identify food sensitivities
  • Omega-3 fatty acids (fish oil) help support healthy skin and coat
  • Avoid artificial dyes and preservatives which may aggravate allergies
  • Limited-ingredient diets can help manage food allergy symptoms
  • Monitor weight closely — Westies can become overweight which stresses small joints

Foods to Avoid

  • Grapes and raisins (toxic)
  • Onions and garlic (toxic)
  • Chocolate (toxic)
  • Common allergens if your Westie has food sensitivities (chicken, beef, wheat, corn)

Popular Westie Mixes

Westipoo (Westie + Poodle)

The Westipoo combines the Westie's spirited personality with the Poodle's intelligence and low-shedding coat. A popular choice for allergy sufferers, these clever, energetic dogs love to learn and play.

Westie-Pug (Pugland)

The Pugland is an unusual but charming mix of the Westie and the Pug. They tend to inherit the Pug's soulful eyes and the Westie's white or cream coat, with a playful, affectionate personality from both parents.

Cairnland (Westie + Cairn Terrier)

Two Scottish terrier breeds combine into the Cairnland — a scrappy, curious little dog with the same independent terrier spirit in an even more compact package. Hardy, playful, and devoted to family.

Westie-Bichon (Wee-Chon)

The Wee-Chon blends the Westie's confidence with the Bichon Frise's gentle, cheerful nature. Typically white, fluffy, and friendly — a wonderful mix for families seeking a small, low-shedding companion.

Fun Facts About Westies

  • ⚪ Always White: Westies only come in white — no other color variations are accepted by breed standards. The white coat was deliberately selected in the late 1800s so hunters wouldn't accidentally shoot their dogs in the field.
  • 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿 Scottish Origin: Westies were developed in Poltalloch, Scotland, by Colonel Edward Donald Malcolm in the 1800s — originally called the "Poltalloch Terrier" before being renamed the West Highland White Terrier.
  • 🐱 Cat-Like Agility: Westies are surprisingly agile for their size, able to climb and navigate rocky terrain with feline-like dexterity — a necessity for their original work hunting in the Scottish Highlands.
  • 📺 Advertising Star: The Westie is the face of Cesar dog food — one of the most recognizable brand mascots in the pet industry worldwide.
  • 🏆 Agility Champions: Despite being terriers, Westies can excel in agility competitions. Their quick, nimble movement and intelligence make them surprisingly competitive in canine sports.
  • 🎭 Dual Purpose: Black & White Scotch Whisky (now "Black & White") used a Scotch Terrier and a Westie as its brand mascots for over a century — two Scottish terrier breeds representing the brand's black and white label.
  • 💪 Big Dog Energy: Westies are famous for having absolutely no idea they're small dogs. They'll confidently stand their ground against much larger dogs and approach the world with bold, unshakeable confidence.
