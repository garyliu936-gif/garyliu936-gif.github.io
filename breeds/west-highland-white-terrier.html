Feeding Your Westie

Westies are small dogs with compact builds. They typically eat ¾–1 cup of high-quality dry kibble per day, divided into two meals. Because skin allergies are common, diet quality matters — a high-quality protein-based diet can help manage skin conditions.

Nutrition Tips

  • Choose formulas with a single, high-quality protein source to help identify food sensitivities
  • Omega-3 fatty acids (fish oil) help support healthy skin and coat
  • Avoid artificial dyes and preservatives which may aggravate allergies
  • Limited-ingredient diets can help manage food allergy symptoms
  • Monitor weight closely — Westies can become overweight which stresses small joints

Foods to Avoid

  • Grapes and raisins (toxic)
  • Onions and garlic (toxic)
  • Chocolate (toxic)
  • Common allergens if your Westie has food sensitivities (chicken, beef, wheat, corn)

Allergy care can be the biggest unexpected cost — Westies with atopic dermatitis may require regular vet visits, prescription medications, or allergy testing (which can cost $300–$800). Pet insurance is strongly recommended for this breed.

Lifetime Cost Estimate

Over a 14-year lifespan, expect to spend $15,000–$28,000 total, potentially more if significant allergy management is needed.

Popular Westie Mixes

Westipoo (Westie + Poodle)

The Westipoo combines the Westie's spirited personality with the Poodle's intelligence and low-shedding coat. A popular choice for allergy sufferers, these clever, energetic dogs love to learn and play.

Westie-Pug (Pugland)

The Pugland is an unusual but charming mix of the Westie and the Pug. They tend to inherit the Pug's soulful eyes and the Westie's white or cream coat, with a playful, affectionate personality from both parents.

Cairnland (Westie + Cairn Terrier)

Two Scottish terrier breeds combine into the Cairnland — a scrappy, curious little dog with the same independent terrier spirit in an even more compact package. Hardy, playful, and devoted to family.

Westie-Bichon (Wee-Chon)

The Wee-Chon blends the Westie's confidence with the Bichon Frise's gentle, cheerful nature. Typically white, fluffy, and friendly — a wonderful mix for families seeking a small, low-shedding companion.

Fun Facts About Westies

  • ⚪ Always White: Westies only come in white — no other color variations are accepted by breed standards. The white coat was deliberately selected in the late 1800s so hunters wouldn't accidentally shoot their dogs in the field.
  • 🏴󠁧󠁢󠁳󠁣󠁴󠁿 Scottish Origin: Westies were developed in Poltalloch, Scotland, by Colonel Edward Donald Malcolm in the 1800s — originally called the "Poltalloch Terrier" before being renamed the West Highland White Terrier.
  • 🐱 Cat-Like Agility: Westies are surprisingly agile for their size, able to climb and navigate rocky terrain with feline-like dexterity — a necessity for their original work hunting in the Scottish Highlands.
  • 📺 Advertising Star: The Westie is the face of Cesar dog food — one of the most recognizable brand mascots in the pet industry worldwide.
  • 🏆 Agility Champions: Despite being terriers, Westies can excel in agility competitions. Their quick, nimble movement and intelligence make them surprisingly competitive in canine sports.
  • 🎭 Dual Purpose: Black & White Scotch Whisky (now "Black & White") used a Scotch Terrier and a Westie as its brand mascots for over a century — two Scottish terrier breeds representing the brand's black and white label.
  • 💪 Big Dog Energy: Westies are famous for having absolutely no idea they're small dogs. They'll confidently stand their ground against much larger dogs and approach the world with bold, unshakeable confidence.
