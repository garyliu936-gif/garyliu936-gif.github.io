🐾 Overview

The Swedish Lapphund (Svensk Lapphund) is one of Sweden's oldest and rarest native breeds, developed thousands of years ago by the Sami people of Lapland as a hunting and reindeer herding dog. Its Spitz-type conformation — erect ears, curled tail, and dense double coat — reflects centuries of adaptation to the harsh subarctic climate of northern Scandinavia. The breed was officially recognized by the Swedish Kennel Club in 1944, and the FCI standard follows closely after.

Today the Swedish Lapphund remains uncommon outside of Scandinavia, though its warm personality and striking appearance are earning it admirers worldwide. It is energetic, vocal, and highly people-oriented — thriving in active households that can match its enthusiasm. Intelligent and eager, it excels in dog sports such as agility, rally obedience, and herding trials, where its natural working instincts shine.

😊 Temperament & Personality

The Swedish Lapphund is a lively, affectionate, and highly social dog. It bonds strongly with its family and does not cope well with long periods of isolation. It has a herding dog's instinct to work cooperatively with humans, making it responsive and eager to please — though it also has the independence of a primitive breed. It is vocal by nature, using a range of barks and yodels to communicate.

  • Highly affectionate — thrives on human companionship and forms deep bonds with all family members
  • Intelligent and trainable — quick to learn new commands and enjoys mental challenges
  • Vocal — uses a wide range of sounds to communicate; early training helps manage barking
  • Good with children — playful and gentle, making it a suitable family dog
  • Friendly with other dogs — sociable and generally gets along well with other pets
  • Alert watchdog — will bark to announce visitors but is not aggressive

🏃 Exercise & Activity Needs

The Swedish Lapphund is a high-energy working breed that needs substantial daily exercise and mental engagement. Without adequate activity, it can become bored and vocal. It excels in active families who enjoy outdoor activities, dog sports, or simply long daily walks in varied environments.

  • At least 60–90 minutes of vigorous exercise per day
  • Excels in agility, herding trials, rally obedience, and canicross
  • Loves outdoor adventures — hiking, running, and off-leash play in safe areas
  • Mental stimulation through training games is essential to prevent boredom-related behavior
  • Does well in cold climates and enjoys snowy environments
  • Not suited to sedentary lifestyles or small apartments without compensating exercise

✂️ Grooming & Coat Care

The Swedish Lapphund has a thick, weather-resistant double coat with a dense, wooly undercoat and a longer, straight outer coat. The coat is typically solid black, though bear-brown and black-and-brown variations exist. It sheds moderately throughout the year with heavy shedding twice annually.

  • Brush 2–3 times per week; daily during seasonal shedding
  • Bathe every 6–8 weeks, or when visibly dirty
  • The coat naturally repels dirt and dries quickly
  • Trim nails every 3–4 weeks
  • Clean ears weekly and check for signs of infection
  • Do not shave — the double coat provides insulation in both heat and cold

🎓 Training

The Swedish Lapphund is one of the more trainable Nordic breeds, combining intelligence with a genuine desire to work with its handler. It responds best to positive, reward-based training and dislikes harsh or repetitive methods. Its vocal nature and high drive make it a natural candidate for dog sports and working activities.

  • Start training early — puppies are quick to pick up both good habits and bad ones
  • Use positive reinforcement with food rewards and play to keep motivation high
  • Manage barking from puppyhood with consistent training and "quiet" commands
  • Socialization with people, dogs, and environments should begin before 12 weeks
  • Enroll in puppy classes and advance to agility or obedience sport training
  • Avoid repetitive drills — vary training sessions to maintain enthusiasm

🏥 Health & Common Issues

The Swedish Lapphund is generally a healthy and long-lived breed, but as with any purebred, certain hereditary conditions require monitoring. Responsible breeders test for hereditary cataracts and progressive retinal atrophy before breeding. The breed's small global population means genetic diversity is a priority for conscientious breeders.

🏠 Is a Swedish Lapphund Right for You?

The Swedish Lapphund suits active families, individuals, or couples who enjoy the outdoors and want a devoted, trainable companion. It is not well-suited to being left alone for long hours or to purely sedentary households. Its vocal nature means apartment living requires serious commitment to training and exercise.

🍽️ How Much to Feed a Swedish Lapphund

Swedish Lapphunds are medium-sized, active dogs with a good metabolism. Adults typically eat 1.5–2.5 cups of high-quality dry kibble per day, divided into two meals. Because they are highly active, nutritional density matters — choose a food that supports sustained energy rather than simple filler carbohydrates. Monitor weight regularly, as this breed can gain weight if exercise is reduced.

✅ Best Foods for Swedish Lapphunds

  • High-quality protein sources like salmon, chicken, or lamb as the primary ingredient
  • Foods rich in omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids to support the thick double coat
  • Whole grains or grain-free formulas depending on the individual dog's tolerances
  • Avoid artificial preservatives, colors, and excessive fillers that reduce nutritional value
  • Consider a working-dog or active-breed formula if the dog participates in dog sports

🚫 Foods That Are Dangerous for Swedish Lapphunds

Chocolate Grapes & Raisins Onions & Garlic Xylitol (artificial sweetener) Macadamia Nuts Alcohol Avocado Raw yeast dough

🦴 Healthy Treats

  • Carrots — low calorie, great for dental health
  • Blueberries — antioxidants and a sweet reward
  • Plain cooked chicken or turkey (no seasoning)
  • Apple slices (remove seeds and core)
  • Plain rice cakes — low-calorie training reward

💡 Tip: Boarding your Swedish Lapphund?

Always bring your Swedish Lapphund's regular food when boarding. Switching food suddenly can cause digestive upset.

🎉 Amazing Facts About Swedish Lapphunds

  • 🧊 The Swedish Lapphund is considered one of the oldest dog breeds in Scandinavia, with archaeological evidence suggesting it has been with the Sami people for thousands of years.
  • 🦌 Originally bred to herd reindeer, the Swedish Lapphund's herding style is unique — it works by barking rather than by the silent stalk of herding breeds like the Border Collie.
  • ⚫ The most common coat color is solid black, making it one of the few predominantly black Spitz breeds in the world.
  • 🎵 Swedish Lapphunds are famously vocal and have been known to "sing" in a range of yodels and howls, a trait shared with many Nordic breeds.
  • 🌍 Outside of Scandinavia, the Swedish Lapphund is extremely rare — considered one of the rarest FCI-recognized breeds in the world.
  • 🏅 Sweden's national dog breed designation is shared among the Swedish Lapphund, the Swedish Vallhund, and the Jämthund.
  • 🐾 Despite their working heritage, modern Swedish Lapphunds have adapted well to family life and are popular in Scandinavian homes as companion dogs.
