Breed Overview

The American Foxhound is one of the oldest dog breeds in the United States — descended directly from English Foxhounds brought to colonial America in 1650 by Robert Brooke of Maryland, and subsequently refined by generations of American huntsmen including, most famously, George Washington himself. Washington kept a large pack at Mount Vernon and documented his breeding program meticulously, crossing his English Foxhounds with French hounds gifted by the Marquis de Lafayette to produce a faster, harder-running hound suited to the larger territories and different fox species of the American countryside. The American Foxhound was officially the first breed developed in the United States.

The American Foxhound is taller, lighter, and faster than its English cousin — a lean, athletic hound built for speed over open Virginia and Maryland countryside rather than the enclosed English hunt parks. Their most celebrated quality is their voice — a resonant, melodious baying that carries for miles and was the soundtrack of colonial American fox hunts. Virginia designated the American Foxhound as its state dog in 1966, cementing its status as an American heritage breed. Despite their historical significance, they remain one of the least popular AKC breeds as pets — their need for extraordinary daily exercise, strong pack instinct, and powerful hunting drive make them challenging companions outside of an active hunting or farming context.

Feeding Your American Foxhound

A large, high-energy working hound, the American Foxhound needs substantial quality nutrition to fuel their active lifestyle. Their lean build requires careful feeding management.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 12 mo)2–3 cups800–1,200 kcal
Adult (1–8 yrs)2–3 cups900–1,300 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)1.5–2.5 cups700–1,000 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • Large-breed, high-protein formula supports their athletic working-dog physique
  • Active hunting Foxhounds may need significantly more calories on working days
  • Feed two meals per day to reduce bloat risk in this deep-chested breed
  • Omega fatty acids maintain the hard, dense weather-resistant coat

Fun Facts About American Foxhounds

  • 🇺🇸 The American Foxhound is the oldest dog breed developed in the United States — predating American independence itself. Robert Brooke brought English Foxhounds to Maryland in 1650, and the selective breeding that produced the distinctly American type was well underway before the Revolution. This makes the American Foxhound a living artifact of colonial American history.
  • 🎖️ George Washington is recorded as one of the most dedicated American Foxhound breeders in history — his Mount Vernon diaries document his pack management in meticulous detail, naming individual hounds, recording their performances, and describing the French hounds gifted by Lafayette that he used to improve his line. Washington considered his hounds a serious agricultural investment and spent considerable time developing faster, more enduring American types.
  • 🎵 The American Foxhound is particularly celebrated for its voice — fox hunters in the American South traditionally "race" hounds on moonlit nights by sound alone, listening to the pack's cry as the dogs pursue a fox across miles of countryside. Different hounds are recognized by their individual voices, and a hound with a particularly beautiful, carrying bay is considered a valuable asset. This tradition of "music hunting" is a uniquely American fox hunting culture.
  • 🏃 American Foxhounds run faster and cover ground more efficiently than English Foxhounds — the American terrain of larger properties and open countryside selected for speed over the more methodical, enclosed-park pace of English fox hunting. An active American Foxhound is capable of running 20–30 miles in a hunt day, a level of endurance that makes them challenging companions in anything but an actively working context.
  • 🌿 Despite being one of the most historically significant American breeds, the American Foxhound is also one of the rarest as a registered companion dog — working foxhound lines often go entirely unregistered with the AKC, as their owners are hunters unconcerned with show registry. This creates an unusual situation where a breed is simultaneously quite common in working contexts (thousands of hunting dogs) and extremely rare in AKC show and companion contexts.
