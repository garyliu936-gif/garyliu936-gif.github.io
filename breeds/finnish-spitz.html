Breed Overview

The Finnish Spitz is Finland's national dog — a striking, fox-like breed with a brilliant golden-red coat, alert triangular ears, and a curled tail. Developed in Finland over thousands of years by the Finno-Ugrian peoples, these dogs were bred as "bark pointers" — a unique hunting style in which the dog locates birds (particularly capercaillie and black grouse) in trees, then barks rapidly and rhythmically to distract the bird and alert the hunter. The dog's ability to bark fast and furiously is literally central to its job description.

As companions, Finnish Spitz — affectionately called "Finkies" — are lively, intelligent, and deeply devoted to their families. They are independent thinkers who retain a strong hunting drive and can be vocal (they were bred to bark, after all). They are gentle and playful with children but reserved with strangers. Their plush coat and fox-like face make them one of the most visually captivating breeds in the world.

Feeding Your Finnish Spitz

An active medium-sized breed, Finnish Spitz need quality nutrition calibrated to their moderate size and high activity level. Their dense double coat benefits from omega-rich foods.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 12 mo)1.5–2.5 cups600–1,000 kcal
Adult (1–8 yrs)1.5–2.5 cups700–1,100 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)1.5–2 cups600–900 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • Omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acids support the brilliant golden double coat
  • High-quality protein maintains their athletic hunting build
  • Avoid overfeeding — they will gain weight easily without sufficient exercise
  • Feed twice daily to maintain stable energy levels

Fun Facts About Finnish Spitz

  • 🇫🇮 The Finnish Spitz has been Finland's national dog since 1979 — and is so beloved in the country that they appear on Finnish postage stamps, postcards, and national art.
  • 🔊 Finnish Spitz are "bark pointers" — they hunt by locating birds in trees and barking rapidly to distract them, allowing the hunter to approach. Champion hunting Finkies can bark at rates of up to 160 barks per minute.
  • 🏆 Finland holds annual "King of the Barkers" competitions for Finnish Spitz, where dogs are judged on the speed, rhythm, and quality of their barking. It is a genuine and prestigious sporting event in their home country.
  • 🦊 The Finnish Spitz is one of the most fox-like dogs in appearance — the breed standard specifically requires a "fox-like" expression and form, and their golden-red coats intensify this resemblance.
  • 🕰️ The breed nearly went extinct in the early 1900s as crossbreeding diluted pure stock — it was saved by Finnish sportsmen Hugo Sandberg and Hugo Roos, who trekked into remote Finnish wilderness to find pure specimens and restore the breed.
