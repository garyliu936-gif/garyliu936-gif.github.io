Feeding Your Mastiff

Mastiffs require significant amounts of food — typically 6–10 cups of high-quality large/giant breed dry kibble per day for adults, divided into at least two meals to reduce bloat risk. Food formulated for giant breeds helps control growth rate in puppies (critical for joint health).

Nutrition Tips

  • Feed giant breed puppy formula — do NOT use adult food for puppies as calcium/phosphorus ratios matter greatly
  • Never exercise your Mastiff for at least 1 hour before or after feeding to reduce bloat risk
  • Elevated food bowls were once recommended but studies now suggest they may actually increase bloat risk — consult your vet
  • Glucosamine and chondroitin supplements support joint health in this heavy breed
  • Omega-3 fatty acids (fish oil) support joint, skin, and heart health

Foods to Avoid

  • Grapes and raisins (toxic)
  • Onions and garlic (toxic)
  • Chocolate (toxic)
  • Gas-producing foods (beans, dairy) which can contribute to bloat risk

Everything costs more with a giant breed. Medications, surgeries, and boarding are priced by weight — a Mastiff's vet bill for the same procedure can be 3–4x that of a medium-sized dog. Pet insurance is essentially mandatory for responsible ownership of this breed.

Lifetime Cost Estimate

Over an 8-year lifespan, expect to spend $20,000–$50,000+ total, especially if significant health issues arise.

Popular Mastiff Mixes

Mastador (Mastiff + Labrador Retriever)

The Mastador combines the Mastiff's calm, protective nature with the Lab's friendly, trainable personality. A large to giant-sized dog, the Mastador often makes a wonderful family dog — loyal, gentle, and devoted.

Boxmas (Mastiff + Boxer)

The Boxmas is a powerful, energetic large dog that blends the Mastiff's protective calm with the Boxer's playful, athletic energy. Loyal family guardians with a shorter, easy-care coat.

Mastiff-Rottweiler Mix (Rottmas)

Two of the world's most famous guardian breeds combine into the Rottmas — an imposing, deeply loyal protector. Requires experienced handling and extensive socialization, but can be a devoted, gentle family companion.

Bullmastiff (Mastiff + Bulldog — established breed)

The Bullmastiff is actually a recognized AKC breed developed by crossing Mastiffs and Bulldogs in 19th-century England. Slightly smaller than the Mastiff at 100–130 lbs, they combine the power and guarding instinct of both parents with a somewhat more active disposition.

Fun Facts About Mastiffs

  • ⚖️ World Record Holder: A Mastiff named Zorba holds the Guinness World Record for heaviest dog ever recorded — weighing in at 343 lbs and measuring 8 feet 3 inches from nose to tail in 1989.
  • 🏛️ Ancient War Dogs: Mastiffs were used as war dogs by ancient civilizations for over 3,000 years — including by Kublai Khan, who reportedly kept a kennel of 5,000 Mastiffs for hunting and war.
  • 🚢 Mayflower Passengers: Two dogs are documented as traveling on the Mayflower in 1620 — one of them was an English Mastiff. They are among the first European dogs to set foot in the Americas.
  • 🦁 Roman Colosseum: Romans imported English Mastiffs for gladiatorial combat in the Colosseum, pitting them against lions, bears, and other animals — a testament to their fearsome reputation.
  • 💧 Champion Droolers: Mastiffs can drool several cups of saliva per day. Their droopy jowls and loose facial skin are a natural consequence of their breed structure — keep towels handy at water bowl time.
  • 🐾 Gentle with Kids: Despite their intimidating size, Mastiffs are extraordinarily patient with children. They are often described as "nanny dogs" who will gently supervise toddlers and protect them instinctively.
  • 🌙 Big Sleepers: Mastiffs can sleep 16–18 hours a day — a necessary behavior for such a large body to conserve energy and allow their joints to rest. Don't mistake laziness for illness.
