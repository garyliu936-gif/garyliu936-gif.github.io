Breed Overview

The English Mastiff is one of the oldest and most massive dog breeds in the world. With males commonly reaching 200+ lbs and standing 30 inches at the shoulder, they hold records for the heaviest dogs ever recorded. Despite their imposing size, Mastiffs are famously gentle, affectionate, and even-tempered — earning the nickname "gentle giant."

Mastiffs have a history stretching back thousands of years, used as war dogs by ancient civilizations including the Romans, and later as estate guard dogs in medieval England. Today's Mastiff is a calm, loving family companion who is protective by nature but rarely aggressive without cause. They make excellent watchdogs simply by their presence — few intruders are willing to test a 200-lb dog.

Temperament

Mastiffs are patient, dignified, and deeply loyal to their families. They're remarkably tolerant of children and tend to be gentle even with toddlers, though supervision is always needed given their sheer size. They are naturally protective without being aggressive — a quiet but powerful deterrent. Mastiffs bond very closely with their families and can be prone to separation anxiety. They're not overly energetic and are content to be near their people.

Exercise Needs

Mastiffs have surprisingly low exercise requirements for such a large breed. Two moderate walks of 20–30 minutes per day is typically sufficient. Over-exercising puppies is dangerous — Mastiff puppies' growth plates are open until age 18–24 months, and excessive running or jumping can cause permanent joint damage. Adult Mastiffs enjoy leisurely walks but aren't suited for jogging partners or intense exercise.

✈️ Travel Tip: At 120–230 lbs, Mastiffs cannot travel in-cabin on any commercial airline. Air cargo or specialist pet transport services are required for long-distance travel. Ground transport in an SUV or van with adequate floor space is ideal for shorter trips. For professional pet transport services, Best Pet Travel specializes in large breed transport logistics.

Grooming

Mastiffs have a short, dense coat that requires minimal grooming — weekly brushing is sufficient to remove loose hair. They are moderate shedders. The facial wrinkles and skin folds must be cleaned regularly (at least weekly) to prevent moisture buildup and skin infections. Their large, floppy ears should be checked and cleaned weekly. Mastiffs are notorious droolers — keep a towel handy, especially after drinking or eating.

Training

Mastiffs are intelligent but can be stubborn. Early training and socialization are absolutely critical — a poorly trained 200-lb dog is a serious safety concern. They respond well to calm, consistent, positive training. Harsh methods are counterproductive with this sensitive breed. Obedience classes are strongly recommended. While they learn commands well, they may choose when to obey — building a strong relationship of trust and mutual respect is key.

Health Considerations

Giant breeds like the Mastiff have specific health concerns, many related to their size. Proactive veterinary care and working with a health-tested breeder are essential.

Hip & Elbow Dysplasia Bloat (GDV) Heart Disease (DCM) Cancer Osteosarcoma Panosteitis (Growing Pains)

Bloat (Gastric Dilatation-Volvulus or GDV) is a life-threatening emergency that Mastiffs are at elevated risk for. Feeding two smaller meals instead of one large meal, avoiding exercise right after eating, and knowing the signs (distended abdomen, unproductive retching, restlessness) can save your dog's life. Prophylactic gastropexy (a surgical stomach tacking procedure) is recommended by many vets for this breed.

Feeding Your Mastiff

Mastiffs require significant amounts of food — typically 6–10 cups of high-quality large/giant breed dry kibble per day for adults, divided into at least two meals to reduce bloat risk. Food formulated for giant breeds helps control growth rate in puppies (critical for joint health).

Nutrition Tips

  • Feed giant breed puppy formula — do NOT use adult food for puppies as calcium/phosphorus ratios matter greatly
  • Never exercise your Mastiff for at least 1 hour before or after feeding to reduce bloat risk
  • Elevated food bowls were once recommended but studies now suggest they may actually increase bloat risk — consult your vet
  • Glucosamine and chondroitin supplements support joint health in this heavy breed
  • Omega-3 fatty acids (fish oil) support joint, skin, and heart health

Foods to Avoid

  • Grapes and raisins (toxic)
  • Onions and garlic (toxic)
  • Chocolate (toxic)
  • Gas-producing foods (beans, dairy) which can contribute to bloat risk

Popular Mastiff Mixes

Mastador (Mastiff + Labrador Retriever)

The Mastador combines the Mastiff's calm, protective nature with the Lab's friendly, trainable personality. A large to giant-sized dog, the Mastador often makes a wonderful family dog — loyal, gentle, and devoted.

Boxmas (Mastiff + Boxer)

The Boxmas is a powerful, energetic large dog that blends the Mastiff's protective calm with the Boxer's playful, athletic energy. Loyal family guardians with a shorter, easy-care coat.

Mastiff-Rottweiler Mix (Rottmas)

Two of the world's most famous guardian breeds combine into the Rottmas — an imposing, deeply loyal protector. Requires experienced handling and extensive socialization, but can be a devoted, gentle family companion.

Bullmastiff (Mastiff + Bulldog — established breed)

The Bullmastiff is actually a recognized AKC breed developed by crossing Mastiffs and Bulldogs in 19th-century England. Slightly smaller than the Mastiff at 100–130 lbs, they combine the power and guarding instinct of both parents with a somewhat more active disposition.

Fun Facts About Mastiffs

  • ⚖️ World Record Holder: A Mastiff named Zorba holds the Guinness World Record for heaviest dog ever recorded — weighing in at 343 lbs and measuring 8 feet 3 inches from nose to tail in 1989.
  • 🏛️ Ancient War Dogs: Mastiffs were used as war dogs by ancient civilizations for over 3,000 years — including by Kublai Khan, who reportedly kept a kennel of 5,000 Mastiffs for hunting and war.
  • 🚢 Mayflower Passengers: Two dogs are documented as traveling on the Mayflower in 1620 — one of them was an English Mastiff. They are among the first European dogs to set foot in the Americas.
  • 🦁 Roman Colosseum: Romans imported English Mastiffs for gladiatorial combat in the Colosseum, pitting them against lions, bears, and other animals — a testament to their fearsome reputation.
  • 💧 Champion Droolers: Mastiffs can drool several cups of saliva per day. Their droopy jowls and loose facial skin are a natural consequence of their breed structure — keep towels handy at water bowl time.
  • 🐾 Gentle with Kids: Despite their intimidating size, Mastiffs are extraordinarily patient with children. They are often described as "nanny dogs" who will gently supervise toddlers and protect them instinctively.
  • 🌙 Big Sleepers: Mastiffs can sleep 16–18 hours a day — a necessary behavior for such a large body to conserve energy and allow their joints to rest. Don't mistake laziness for illness.
