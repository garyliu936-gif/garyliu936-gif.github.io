Breed Overview

The Lagotto Romagnolo (pronounced "la-GOT-oh ro-man-YO-lo") is an ancient Italian breed from the Romagna region of northeastern Italy, with a history as a water retriever dating back to the Renaissance. As the marshlands of Romagna were drained in the 19th century, Lagottos transitioned to a new and enormously valuable job: hunting truffles. Today, the Lagotto Romagnolo is the only dog breed in the world specifically recognized and bred for truffle hunting.

Their extraordinary nose — combined with deep intelligence, a willingness to work, and a curly, low-shedding coat — makes them the gold standard in the truffle-hunting world. A skilled Lagotto can find white Alba truffles worth $3,000 per pound buried feet underground. As companions, they are deeply affectionate, playful, and sensitive dogs that thrive on mental challenges. Their rising popularity in recent years reflects both their impressive working ability and their wonderful temperament.

  • Hip dysplasia — screen parents
  • Epilepsy in some lines
  • Generally a long-lived, healthy breed (15–17 yrs)

Feeding Your Lagotto Romagnolo

An active working dog, the Lagotto needs quality nutrition to fuel both physical activity and intense mental work. Their curly coat benefits from omega-rich nutrition.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 12 mo)1.5–2.5 cups600–1,000 kcal
Adult (1–8 yrs)1.5–2.5 cups700–1,100 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)1.5–2 cups600–900 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • High-quality protein supports their active, working-dog lifestyle
  • Omega-3 and biotin maintain the thick, curly, waterproof coat
  • Avoid overfeeding — they become obese without sufficient exercise
  • Feed twice daily at consistent times to support stable energy for work

Fun Facts About Lagotto Romagnolos

  • 🍄 The Lagotto Romagnolo is the only dog breed in the world officially recognized as purpose-bred for truffle hunting — their extraordinary nose can locate truffles buried several feet underground, in all weather, through the competing scents of the forest floor.
  • 💰 A skilled Lagotto's ability to find white Alba truffles — which can sell for over $3,000 per pound — makes a well-trained Lagotto one of the most economically valuable working dogs in the world, sometimes worth more than some livestock.
  • 🌊 Before they became truffle hunters, Lagottos were waterfowl retrievers in the marshes of the Romagna region. When those wetlands were drained in the 1800s, Italian hunters discovered their noses were equally suited to finding underground truffles.
  • 🧬 The Lagotto is one of the oldest water dog breeds in Italy, with evidence of curly-coated Italian water dogs appearing in artwork going back to the 1400s — making them a truly Renaissance dog.
  • 🐾 Despite their working-dog intensity, Lagottos are famously affectionate and family-oriented — Italian truffle hunters describe them as dogs that work hard in the forest and then come home and curl up in your lap like a stuffed animal.
