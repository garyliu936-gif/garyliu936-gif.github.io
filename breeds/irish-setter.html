Breed Overview

The Irish Setter is one of Ireland's great contributions to the dog world — a magnificent sporting breed developed in the 18th century for hunting birds over open ground. Their rich mahogany or chestnut-red coat, elegant silhouette, and exuberant gait make them one of the most visually striking dogs at any dog show or in any field.

Irish Setters were popular family dogs throughout the 20th century, including in the White House — President Nixon's Irish Setter "King Timahoe" was a beloved presence during his presidency. Today they remain a joyful, energetic, and deeply affectionate breed for active families who can match their enthusiasm for life.

Temperament

Irish Setters are famously friendly, outgoing, and enthusiastic about everything — from greeting strangers (everyone is a potential new friend) to chasing birds to cuddling on the couch. They're affectionate with family, gentle with children, and generally get along well with other pets. Their exuberance can be overwhelming for very young children or elderly individuals — not from aggression, but from sheer bouncy enthusiasm. They remain puppy-like in personality well into adulthood, often until age 3–4.

Exercise Needs

Irish Setters have very high exercise requirements — they need at least 1–2 hours of vigorous daily activity. They excel as running companions, hiking partners, and are naturals at field sports and agility. An under-exercised Irish Setter becomes destructive and anxious. They need both physical and mental stimulation — a tired Irish Setter is a well-behaved Irish Setter. A large, securely fenced yard is ideal.

✈️ Travel Tip: At 60–70 lbs, Irish Setters travel as checked baggage on most commercial airlines. Their athletic build and high energy can make confinement challenging — crate training from puppyhood is essential for stress-free travel. Their love of people means they typically settle once they're confident their owner hasn't disappeared. Summer heat restrictions apply on many airlines for large breeds.

Grooming

The Irish Setter's gorgeous silky coat requires regular maintenance. Brushing 2–3 times per week prevents tangles and removes debris, especially around the ears, chest, and legs where feathering is heaviest. Professional grooming every 8–12 weeks helps maintain the coat shape. Their long, floppy ears trap moisture and debris — weekly ear cleaning is essential to prevent infections. The feathering on legs and belly picks up mud and burrs readily — post-walk checks are a regular routine for Irish Setter owners.

Training

Irish Setters are intelligent and eager to please, but their exuberance and easily distracted nature requires patient, consistent training. They respond beautifully to positive reinforcement with praise and treats — they love to learn when it's fun. Harsh correction is counterproductive with this sensitive breed. Training sessions should be upbeat, varied, and kept short to hold their attention. Early socialization helps channel their enthusiasm appropriately. Recall training is important due to their bird-chasing instinct.

Health Considerations

Irish Setters are generally healthy, long-lived dogs. Being aware of breed-specific conditions helps with proactive care.

Hip Dysplasia Progressive Retinal Atrophy Hypothyroidism Bloat (GDV) Canine Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency (CLAD) Epilepsy

CLAD (Canine Leukocyte Adhesion Deficiency) is a serious immune disorder unique to Irish Setters — responsible breeders test for it, and it's been largely eliminated from well-bred lines through health testing. Bloat risk, as with many deep-chested breeds, warrants prophylactic gastropexy discussion with your vet.

Feeding Your Irish Setter

Active adult Irish Setters typically eat 2.5–3.5 cups of high-quality dry kibble per day, split into two meals. Their high energy level means caloric needs are greater than many breeds of similar weight — working or very active dogs may need more.

Nutrition Tips

  • High-quality protein as first ingredient supports their athletic build
  • Omega-3 fatty acids (fish oil) maintain the gorgeous silky coat
  • Split meals reduce bloat risk — never feed one large daily meal
  • Avoid exercise for at least an hour after eating
  • Some Irish Setters have grain sensitivities — consult your vet if skin or digestive issues arise

Foods to Avoid

  • Grapes and raisins (toxic)
  • Onions and garlic (toxic)
  • Chocolate (toxic)
  • Gas-producing foods (bloat risk in deep-chested breeds)

Popular Irish Setter Mixes

Irish Doodle (Irish Setter + Poodle)

The Irish Doodle is a beloved designer mix combining the Irish Setter's gorgeous red coat and playful personality with the Poodle's intelligence and reduced shedding. A wonderful family dog — energetic, friendly, and often sporting a wavy red or apricot coat.

Irish Setter-Golden Retriever Mix

Two of the most beloved sporting dogs combine in this mix — a friendly, athletic dog with the Golden's trainability and the Irish Setter's boundless energy. Typically a stunning reddish-gold color, affectionate with everyone, and excellent with children.

Irish Setter-Labrador Mix

The Irish-Lab mix blends the Lab's famous trainability with the Setter's speed and grace. An athletic, social dog well-suited for active families. Often inherits a reddish or chocolate coat and the lab's sturdy build.

Irish Setter-Border Collie Mix

A highly intelligent, energetic mix of two working breeds. The Border Collie's herding instinct and laser focus blend with the Setter's bird-finding nose and enthusiasm. Requires substantial mental and physical exercise — best for very active, experienced owners.

Fun Facts About Irish Setters

  • 🎨 Signature Red: Irish Setters come in only one color — a rich mahogany or chestnut red. Early Irish Setters were actually red and white (like the modern Irish Red and White Setter), but breeders in the 18th century selectively bred for the striking all-red coat we know today.
  • 🏛️ White House Dogs: President Richard Nixon's Irish Setter "King Timahoe" was a beloved First Dog in the White House (1969–1974). The breed has had multiple presidential admirers throughout American history.
  • 📚 Big Red: The 1945 children's book "Big Red" by Jim Kjelgaard, about a boy and his Irish Setter champion, became a classic piece of American literature and a 1962 Disney film — introducing millions of children to the breed.
  • 🐦 "Setting" Explained: The name "Setter" comes from their hunting style — they were trained to "set" (crouch low) when they located game birds, allowing hunters to throw nets over both dog and prey. As guns replaced nets, Setters were retrained to hold a point instead.
  • 🧠 Perpetual Puppies: Irish Setters are famous for their slow mental maturity — many don't fully "settle down" from their puppy exuberance until age 3–4 years. Owners lovingly call this the "Irish Setter curse" and "Irish Setter blessing" simultaneously.
  • 🌟 Show Ring Stars: Irish Setters are perennially popular in the show ring for their flashy red coat and elegant movement. They've won multiple Best in Show awards at Westminster and other major kennel club events.
  • 🏃 Field Champions: Despite their show ring beauty, Irish Setters remain capable hunters in the field. Dual champions — dogs that earn both conformation (show) and field titles — are the breed's ultimate achievement.
