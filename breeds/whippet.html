Breed Overview

The Whippet is a medium-sized sighthound developed in England during the 19th century, primarily from Greyhound crosses. Originally bred for rabbit coursing and racing — known as "the poor man's racehorse" — the Whippet became one of the fastest dogs in the world relative to its size, capable of reaching 35 mph in short bursts.

Despite their athletic capabilities, Whippets are gentle, quiet, and affectionate at home. They're often called "velcro dogs" for their tendency to press close to their people. They're one of the best all-around family dogs in the sighthound group — less aloof than Greyhounds, more manageable than some larger breeds, and genuinely sweet-natured.

Temperament

Whippets are gentle, affectionate, and calm indoors. They get along beautifully with children, are generally friendly with strangers (after an initial cautious assessment), and can live peacefully with other dogs. Their prey drive is strong, however — they will chase small animals instinctively. Despite their athletic build, they're known as low-drama dogs who rarely bark, are not destructive, and are deeply gentle in nature.

Exercise Needs

Whippets follow a burst-and-rest pattern — they need one or two good off-leash runs per day (ideally in a securely fenced area), after which they're content to lounge for hours. About 30–60 minutes of vigorous exercise meets their daily needs. They should never be off-leash in an unfenced area — once they spot something to chase, they're gone and won't hear you calling them back.

✈️ Travel Tip: At 25–40 lbs, Whippets are too large for most in-cabin airline travel. They can travel as checked baggage on many airlines — their short coat means temperature regulation is a concern in cold cargo holds. Their lean, sensitive build requires extra padding in crates. Whippets are naturally calm dogs, making them better travelers than many breeds once properly crate-trained.

Grooming

The Whippet's short, smooth coat is exceptionally low-maintenance — a weekly wipe-down with a damp cloth or rubber grooming mitt is usually sufficient. They shed minimally compared to most breeds. Whippets have very little body fat and almost no coat insulation, making them sensitive to cold — they should wear a dog coat in cold weather and avoid extended outdoor time in freezing temperatures. Regular nail trimming and ear checks complete their grooming needs.

Training

Whippets are intelligent and eager to please, making training generally smooth. They respond well to positive reinforcement and gentle methods — they're sensitive dogs who wilt under harsh correction. Recall training is challenging because their chase instinct overrides obedience when prey is spotted. Reliable recall requires extensive work. Lure coursing is a great outlet for their natural instincts. Crate training is usually easy as they naturally enjoy confined, cozy spaces.

Health Considerations

Whippets are one of the healthiest dog breeds overall, with few genetic conditions and a relatively long lifespan for their size. Their main vulnerabilities relate to their lean physique.

Cardiac Arrhythmia Sensitivity to Anesthesia Hypothyroidism Eye Conditions Cold Sensitivity

Like all sighthounds, Whippets are sensitive to certain anesthetic drugs — always inform your vet of the breed before any surgery. Their very low body fat means standard dosing charts don't apply. This is widely known among veterinary professionals and is easily managed with the right protocols.

Feeding Your Whippet

Whippets typically eat 1.5–2.5 cups of high-quality dry kibble per day, split into two meals. Their lean build means it can be hard to tell if they're at an ideal weight — a slight waist and visible last two ribs are normal. Avoid overfeeding as excess weight stresses their frame.

Nutrition Tips

  • High-quality protein supports their lean, athletic muscle mass
  • Avoid overfeeding — a slightly lean-looking Whippet is healthier than an overweight one
  • Omega-3 fatty acids support skin and coat health
  • Sensitive stomachs are common — gradual food transitions if changing brands
  • Active Whippets competing in lure coursing or racing may need higher caloric intake

Foods to Avoid

  • Grapes and raisins (toxic)
  • Onions and garlic (toxic)
  • Chocolate (toxic)
  • High-fat or high-sugar treats (unnecessary calories)

Popular Whippet Mixes

Whipador (Whippet + Labrador Retriever)

The Whipador combines the Whippet's elegant speed with the Lab's friendly, outgoing personality. A medium-to-large dog, the Whipador tends to be athletic, social, and highly trainable — a wonderful active family dog.

Whippet-Border Collie Mix

An athletic, brainy combination — this mix brings the Whippet's speed together with the Border Collie's legendary intelligence and herding instinct. High-energy and highly capable, this mix excels in dog sports like agility.

Pitwhip (Whippet + Pit Bull Terrier)

The Pitwhip is an athletic, muscular mix combining the Whippet's lean grace with the Pit Bull's powerful build. Often a loving, energetic companion with a streamlined build and strong prey drive — needs proper socialization.

Whippet-Greyhound Mix

Two sighthound breeds combined into one supremely fast, elegant dog. This mix shares all the best sighthound traits — quiet indoors, explosive outdoors, gentle with family, and deeply affectionate.

Fun Facts About Whippets

  • 🏃 World's Fastest Medium Dog: Whippets can reach speeds of 35 mph (56 km/h) — making them the fastest dog in their weight class. They regularly beat much larger breeds in short-distance racing.
  • 🏭 Working Class Origins: Whippets were originally bred by English coal miners in the 1800s who couldn't afford to keep Greyhounds. They were raced in makeshift tracks on weekends — becoming known as "the poor man's racehorse."
  • 😴 Marathon Sleepers: Don't mistake the Whippet's legendary laziness indoors for a lack of ability — they can sleep 16+ hours a day and then immediately reach full sprint speed in less than a second.
  • 🤫 Nearly Silent: Whippets are one of the least vocal dog breeds. They rarely bark without reason, making them surprisingly good apartment dogs despite their athletic physique.
  • 🥶 Cold Haters: With virtually zero body fat and a paper-thin coat, Whippets genuinely suffer in cold weather. Many dedicated Whippet owners have extensive dog coat wardrobes for their pets.
  • 🐱 Cat Personality: Whippets are frequently described as having cat-like tendencies — they groom themselves, seek warmth obsessively, and will curl into impossibly small spaces to stay comfortable.
  • 🎨 Most Colorful Breed: Whippets come in nearly every color and pattern recognized in dogs — solids, brindles, parti-colors, piebald, and more. No two Whippets look exactly alike.
