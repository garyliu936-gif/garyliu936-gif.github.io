Breed Overview

The Pharaoh Hound is the national dog of Malta, where it is known as the "Kelb tal-Fenek" — Maltese for "rabbit dog." Long believed to be one of the oldest dog breeds in existence, with ancient Egyptian artwork depicting nearly identical dogs alongside pharaohs, the Pharaoh Hound's resemblance to figures in 4,000-year-old Egyptian tomb paintings is striking. They are thought to have been brought to Malta by Phoenician traders thousands of years ago, where the breed has remained isolated and pure ever since.

The Pharaoh Hound has a sleek, rust-colored coat, amber eyes, large expressive ears, and a graceful, athletic build. They are known for one unique and charming trait: when happy or excited, the nose and ears of a Pharaoh Hound flush a deep rose-pink — they are one of the only dogs in the world that visibly blushes. As companions, they are affectionate, playful, and sensitive — excellent with families but reserved with strangers.

Health Notes

  • Generally a very healthy, long-lived breed
  • Sensitive to anesthesia — inform your vet
  • Hip dysplasia (less common than in many breeds)
  • Thin coat — susceptible to cold; needs a coat in winter

Feeding Your Pharaoh Hound

An athletic, lean sighthound type, Pharaoh Hounds need quality nutrition calibrated to their athletic build. They can look thin even when at a healthy weight — judge by feel, not appearance.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 12 mo)1.5–2.5 cups600–1,000 kcal
Adult (1–8 yrs)1.5–2.5 cups700–1,100 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)1.5–2 cups600–900 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • High-quality protein maintains their lean, athletic sighthound build
  • Omega-3 fatty acids support their short coat and sensitive skin
  • Sighthounds metabolize anesthesia differently — note this for vet visits
  • Avoid bloat by feeding twice daily and not exercising immediately after meals

Fun Facts About Pharaoh Hounds

  • 😊 The Pharaoh Hound is one of the only dogs in the world that visibly blushes — when excited or happy, the nose and inner ears flush a deep rose-pink. Maltese breeders call this "the glow of the dog."
  • 🏺 Ancient Egyptian tomb paintings from as early as 4,000 BC depict dogs strikingly identical to the Pharaoh Hound — the same large ears, sleek red coat, and curled tail. Many believe they are looking at the direct ancestors of today's Pharaoh Hound.
  • 🇲🇹 The Pharaoh Hound has been the national dog of Malta since 1974 — it appears on Maltese coins and stamps, and its silhouette is one of the country's most recognized national symbols.
  • 🔇 Unlike many sighthounds, Pharaoh Hounds hunt primarily by sight AND scent AND hearing — the large, mobile ears can pivot independently to locate sound, making them exceptionally versatile hunters of rabbit in rocky terrain.
  • ❄️ Despite their ancient Mediterranean origins, Pharaoh Hounds are extremely sensitive to cold — their thin coat and almost no body fat means they must wear a coat in cold climates and should sleep indoors. They are the opposite of a cold-weather dog.
