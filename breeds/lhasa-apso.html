Lhasa Apso

Once the sacred sentinel of Tibetan palaces and monasteries, the Lhasa Apso is an ancient, dignified breed that combines a flowing coat with a surprisingly bold and independent spirit.

Feeding Your Lhasa Apso

Lhasas eat ½–1 cup of high-quality small breed dry kibble daily, split into two meals. Small breed formulas with smaller kibble are recommended. Their long lifespan means quality nutrition pays dividends for many years.

Life StageDaily AmountMeals/Day
Puppy (2–6 mo)¼–½ cup3
Adult (1–8 yr)½–1 cup2
Senior (9+ yr)½–¾ cup2

Kidney health is important in this breed — avoid high-phosphorus diets and ensure fresh water is always available. Omega-3s support coat and skin health. Avoid overfeeding — obesity strains their small frame.

Popular Lhasa Apso Mixes

Lhasa-Poo (Lhasa + Poodle)

A low-shedding, intelligent mix combining the Lhasa's independent character with Poodle smarts. Typically small, fluffy, and longer-lived — a great choice for those wanting a hypoallergenic small dog with personality.

Shih Apso (Lhasa + Shih Tzu)

Two Tibetan palace dogs combine in the Shih Apso — a sweet, long-haired small dog that often takes the best temperament traits from both breeds. Generally friendlier than a purebred Lhasa.

Lhasa-Maltese Mix (Lhatese)

The Lhatese blends the Lhasa's dignity with the Maltese's white coat and gentle nature. A small, beautiful companion dog popular with those who love long-haired toy breeds.

Fun Facts About Lhasa Apsos

  • 🏔️ Sacred Dogs: In Tibet, Lhasas were believed to be reincarnations of lamas (monks) who hadn't achieved nirvana. Harming one was considered deeply inauspicious — they were revered rather than merely kept.
  • 🎁 Never Sold: Tibetan Lhasas were never sold — they could only be gifted. The Dalai Lama reportedly gifted a pair of Lhasas to C. Suydam Cutting in 1933, bringing the first Lhasas to the United States.
  • 🦁 Lion Dog: The Tibetan name for the breed is "Abso Seng Kye" — meaning "Bark Lion Sentinel Dog." Their flowing mane was seen as resembling a snow lion, a sacred creature in Tibetan mythology.
  • 🧬 Ancient DNA: Genetic studies place the Lhasa Apso among the most ancient of all dog breeds — among 14 breeds confirmed to be closest to the ancestral wolf genetically, alongside Akitas, Chow Chows, and Shiba Inus.
  • 🔔 Palace Alarms: Lhasas served as indoor sentinels in Tibetan palaces and monasteries, alerting the larger Tibetan Mastiffs outside to any intruders. The Mastiffs provided the muscle; the Lhasas provided the ears and voice.
