Breed Overview

The Norwegian Elkhound is one of the oldest dog breeds in the world — a true ancient Spitz type whose ancestors have been hunting alongside Scandinavian people since the Stone Age. Skeletons of Elkhound-type dogs have been found in Norwegian archaeological sites dating back 6,000–7,000 years, making them one of the oldest known domesticated dogs in Northern Europe. They are the national dog of Norway.

Originally bred to hunt moose (called "elk" in Scandinavia) and other large game like bear and lynx, the Elkhound would track prey through vast mountain wilderness, then hold it at bay — circling, barking, and dodging until the hunter arrived. This required extraordinary endurance, courage, and independence. Today's Elkhound carries all of those traits in a compact, wedge-shaped body with a distinctive silver-grey double coat, tightly curled tail, and bold, confident expression. They make loyal, alert family dogs with a strong independent streak.

Feeding Your Norwegian Elkhound

A medium, high-energy working dog, the Elkhound has a surprisingly strong tendency to gain weight. Their hunting heritage means they evolved to eat heartily after exertion — without adequate exercise, calories pile up quickly.

Life StageDaily AmountCalories/Day
Puppy (under 12 mo)1.5–2.5 cups600–1,000 kcal
Adult (1–8 yrs)1.5–2 cups700–1,050 kcal
Senior (8+ yrs)1–1.5 cups550–800 kcal

Nutrition Tips

  • High-protein formula supports their active, muscular build
  • Omega-3 fatty acids maintain their dense double coat through shedding seasons
  • Elkhounds are notorious for weight gain — measure meals precisely and limit treats
  • Joint supplements beneficial for older dogs given their athletic workload history

Fun Facts About Norwegian Elkhounds

  • ⚔️ Norwegian Elkhound-type dogs have been found in Viking burial mounds and Stone Age archaeological sites across Scandinavia — making this one of the oldest documented dog breeds on Earth, with a hunting partnership with humans stretching back at least 6,000 years.
  • 🦌 The name "elkhound" is a bit misleading — in Scandinavia, "elk" refers to what North Americans call moose. Norwegian Elkhounds were bred to track moose through mountainous terrain, then hold the enormous animal at bay by circling and barking furiously until the hunter arrived on foot.
  • 🏔️ Elkhounds are built for endurance over extreme terrain — they were expected to run 25+ miles per day through Norwegian mountain wilderness, tracking by scent while staying light and agile enough to dodge the dangerous kicks of a cornered moose.
  • 🐻 Beyond moose, Elkhounds were used to hunt bear and lynx — animals that could seriously injure or kill a dog. Their courage in facing large, dangerous prey is a deeply ingrained breed characteristic still evident in their bold, fearless personality today.
  • 🇳🇴 The Norwegian Elkhound is the national dog of Norway — a title that reflects not just their hunting heritage but their role as loyal companions to Norwegian families across thousands of years of harsh northern winters, long before the concept of a "pet dog" existed.
