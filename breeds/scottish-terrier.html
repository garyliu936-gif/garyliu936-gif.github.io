Breed Overview

The Scottish Terrier — universally called the "Scottie" — is one of the most iconic dog silhouettes in the world. That distinctive shape: low-slung body, erect ears, wiry coat, and long beard has appeared on everything from Monopoly game pieces to whisky labels to presidential pet records. Developed in the Scottish Highlands to hunt fox, badger, and otter in rocky terrain, the Scottie was bred for tenacity and independence.

Scotties have a dignified, almost aristocratic bearing that sets them apart from other terriers. They're loyal to their family but not overtly demonstrative, and they carry themselves with a self-contained confidence that some describe as "almost human." Three US presidents owned Scotties — more than any other breed.

Temperament

Scotties are described as "dignified, bold, and self-reliant" in the AKC standard — an accurate summary. They're devoted to their immediate family but reserved with strangers and not interested in making friends with everyone they meet. They can be one-person dogs who choose their human carefully. They're feisty, brave, and sometimes argumentative — they have opinions and aren't shy about expressing them. They do better with older children who respect their independence.

Exercise Needs

Moderate exercise — 30–45 minutes daily. They enjoy walks and exploration but aren't high-endurance athletes. They love to dig (a strong terrier instinct) — providing a dedicated dig spot in the yard saves your garden. Always on leash or in a fenced area; their prey drive makes them unreliable off-leash.

✈️ Travel Tip: At 18–22 lbs, Scotties are at the upper edge of in-cabin airline weight limits. Check your specific airline's policy (most cap pets at 20 lbs including carrier). Their reserved, quiet nature can make them reasonable travel companions once carrier-acclimated, though their independence means they may protest confinement.

Grooming

The Scottie's double coat — a soft undercoat and harsh, wiry outer coat — should ideally be hand-stripped (dead hair pulled by hand) to maintain proper texture and color. Most pet owners opt for professional clipping every 6–8 weeks, though this softens the coat over time. The beard and leg furnishings require regular brushing. Black Scotties are the most common, but wheaten and brindle colors also occur. Regular ear cleaning and nail trimming are standard.

Training

Scotties are intelligent but legendarily stubborn — they rank among the most independent-minded of all terriers. They respond to positive reinforcement and consistency but will not perform on command for performance's sake. Building a trusting relationship is essential before expecting compliance. They can excel at earthdog trials and agility with the right motivation. Early socialization prevents excessive wariness from developing into aggression.

Health Considerations

Scottie Cramp Von Willebrand's Disease Craniomandibular Osteopathy Bladder Cancer (TCC) Cushing's Disease

Scotties have an elevated risk of bladder cancer (transitional cell carcinoma) — higher than almost any other breed. Awareness of symptoms (blood in urine, straining) and regular vet checks are important. Scottie Cramp is a benign but sometimes concerning movement disorder triggered by stress or excitement that causes temporary leg stiffness — it looks dramatic but isn't painful or progressive.

Feeding Your Scottish Terrier

Scotties eat about ¾–1 cup of quality dry kibble daily, split into two meals. Small-to-medium breed formulas work well. They can be prone to weight gain if overfed and under-exercised.

Antioxidant-rich diets may provide some protection against cancer. Omega-3 fatty acids support coat and skin health. Avoid high-fat treats. Fresh water always available.

Popular Scottish Terrier Mixes

Scottie-Westie Mix (Sco-Westie)

Two Scottish terrier cousins combined — bold, compact, and full of terrier spirit. Often more sociable than a purebred Scottie while retaining the characteristic short-legged, bearded look.

Scottiepoo (Scottie + Poodle)

Combines the Scottie's dignified character with Poodle intelligence and reduced shedding. A clever, somewhat independent small dog with a soft, wavy coat.

Scottie-Cairn Mix

A compact, fearless mix of two Highland terriers. Typically similar in size and temperament to both parents — scrappy, curious, and devoted to their person.

Fun Facts About Scottish Terriers

  • 🏛️ Presidential Favorite: Three US presidents owned Scotties: FDR (Fala), Eisenhower (Telek), and George W. Bush (Barney and Miss Beazley). Fala is the only presidential dog with a memorial in Washington, D.C. — his statue stands beside FDR's.
  • 🎲 Monopoly Token: The Scottie dog is one of the most beloved Monopoly playing pieces — introduced in the 1930s and repeatedly voted by players as their favorite token, surviving multiple rounds of token replacements.
  • 🥃 Brand Icon: Black & White Scotch Whisky has featured a black Scottish Terrier and a white Westie as its mascots for over a century — making the Scottie silhouette one of the most recognizable brand symbols in the spirits industry.
  • 🎨 Three Colors: Despite the iconic image of a black Scottie, the breed comes in three colors: black, wheaten (pale gold), and brindle (mixed black and brown). All three are equally valid — the black just became the cultural default.
  • 💪 Digging Machines: Scotties were bred specifically to enter underground burrows and fight badgers and foxes in their dens. Their short, powerful legs and muscular shoulders are built for digging and leverage underground — instincts very much alive in modern Scotties.
